# Client context and promise fulfillment

Contract version: 1.1, October 9 2026.

Apply this agency-wide to Dulcy and every B2B client, including agencies, professional services, manufacturers, and future sectors. Human is one case study, not the default business model. Share the decision rules; keep commercial facts and sales motions client-specific. Use this same contract in campaign building, reply coaching, and AI reply-system design.

## Bind both systems to one client context

Before campaign copy or a substantive reply, resolve a versioned, approved client context with:

- Identity: client, workspace, campaign, sender, offer/version, source links, approver, and approval date.
- Buyer: target business model, roles, use case, desired outcome, exclusions, language, locale, and timezone.
- Offer: approved scope, quantities, pricing or pricing limits, free/paid status, guarantees, proof and limitations, and the exact prospect-facing promise.
- Delivery: mode, disclosed conditions, required prospect inputs, available assets, owner, turnaround, capacity, and readiness evidence.
- Sales motion: qualification criteria, expected stages and decision process, technical/procurement requirements, stakeholder route, approved next step, calendar, and booking owner.
- Operations: reviewer, backup, staffed coverage, escalation route, suppression/compliance constraints, communication permissions, and current draft-only controls.

Unknown is a valid value. Missing material facts block launch readiness or a send-ready reply; they do not invite invention. Never copy another client's prices, proof, claims, assets, capacity, or calendar. An example from a manufacturer or agency is not approval of an offer for another client.

The campaign builder must pass the context version and exact outbound promise into the reply system. The reply system must also read the actual outbound and current full thread: a later context change cannot retroactively rewrite what a prospect was promised. A mismatch goes to human review. Keep historical promise versions and explicit agreed amendments.

## Apply the selected replier settings

1. Before drafting, resolve the effective configuration for the correct client/workspace/campaign and record its source/version: tone, length, language, response objective, CTA/scheduling method, office hours/timezone, follow-up cadence, asset eligibility, reviewer, and handoff mode. Apply explicit workspace overrides over documented shared defaults; never guess a generic profile or treat an old SOP example as live configuration. Missing material settings require a precise review blocker.
2. Start with substance: the answer, requested information, promised deliverable, or necessary next step. Do not thank people for replying or add phatic openings such as “Thanks for getting back to me,” “Appreciate the reply,” “Great question,” or “Hope you’re well.” Warm means clear and considerate wording, not filler. Ask at most one useful question; answer every material question the prospect asked.
3. Apply authority by field. Current explicit user instructions override outdated examples. Effective replier settings control style, length, language, CTA and cadence; the actual thread controls state and prior promises; approved client facts control commercial claims. Reject legacy templates that conflict. A historical example is not authority to use thanks, withhold an answer, or force a call.
4. Keep settings within truth, approved facts, the exact promise, suppression, and human-send controls. A call CTA cannot add a condition to a promised emailed asset. A scheduling setting cannot invent availability. Office hours/cadence govern review timing and proposed next actions; a prospect’s specific requested window and hard stops take precedence. No setting or style choice authorizes autonomous sending.
5. Validate the draft against the resolved profile and record deviations/blockers for the reviewer. A profile change affecting the reply invalidates stale drafts/approvals. Keep material settings gaps visible rather than producing a supposedly configured send-ready answer.

## Record each promise

Capture these fields separately from general sentiment or meeting intent:

- Promise source: exact outbound/inbound wording, message IDs and timestamps, client/workspace/campaign, and context version.
- Asset or service: what the prospect will actually receive, quantity, scope, quality criteria, format, and whether they can retain/use it. A screen-share is not delivery of a promised take-away list.
- Delivery mode: email, accessible link, call, after call, or another expressly agreed method.
- Disclosed conditions: necessary inputs, eligibility, timing, call requirement, cost, and any purchase requirement, with evidence of when they were disclosed and accepted. Preserve free status when promised.
- Owner and readiness: accountable person, backup, due time or agreed trigger, dependencies, capacity, status, approved asset/version, and access check.
- Fulfillment evidence: exact artifact/quantity supplied, provider message/link or call record, delivery timestamp, and verified access/transfer evidence. Keep produced, ready, sent, delivered, and received/read distinct; do not claim receipt or reading without evidence.

Use states such as promised, awaiting inputs, in production, ready verified, awaiting review, sent awaiting verification, fulfilled, blocked, or amended by agreement. A booking, held call, draft, attachment mention, or completed task label alone does not prove fulfillment.

## Honor the actual promise

1. Read the current complete thread and apply opt-out, identity, booked/service, and ownership rules first.
2. Identify the requested next step and all open promises, then compare any draft against the contract.
3. If the prospect accepted an explicitly call-delivered offer, it is legitimate to arrange that call. Preserve the promised quantity, free status, conditions, and actual delivery. Do not turn the call into a purchase requirement or substitute a pitch for the asset.
4. If the promise was to send an asset, fulfill that promise through the agreed method. Do not add a new mandatory call, payment, purchase, or qualification hurdle. An optional call cannot gate delivery.
5. If delivery mode or conditions were not clear, do not retroactively assume a call was agreed. Flag the ambiguity, ask the owner, and prepare an honest clarification for human review. A transparent prospect-approved amendment can change the method; pressure or silence cannot.
6. If the asset is missing or not ready, create an exact fulfillment blocker with an owner and next checkpoint. Draft an honest status/clarification only when supported. Do not invent an asset, link, attachment, production progress, deadline, or completion claim. Do not replace the missing asset with a meeting pitch.
7. If the prospect requests email after agreeing to call delivery, acknowledge the preference and check whether the owner can approve the changed method. Explain any genuine dependency plainly; offer an honest choice. Do not falsely claim email is impossible or silently promise an unsupported change.
8. After fulfilling the promise, choose the next step from the actual buyer need. Answer information requests, schedule explicit meeting intent, honor review windows, and permit no CTA when delivery alone is useful.

Do not treat free-asset acceptance as purchase intent. Do not ban calls: an openly agreed call can be the delivery method, a legitimate input-gathering step, or the right next decision.

## Adapt the commercial path

- Dulcy free lead-list example: if 1,000 free leads are explicitly offered for delivery on a call and the prospect agrees, schedule that fulfillment call and transfer the actual agreed usable list. Do not claim a booked/held call fulfilled the offer by itself.
- Agency or professional-services illustration: deliver an approved teardown or examples by the promised method; then discuss a specific remaining need if useful. A proposal, workshop, or diagnostic call may be the approved next stage. Do not assume every client sells audits or AEO.
- Manufacturer illustration: a catalog request, technical feasibility question, sample, drawing/specification review, RFQ, quote, and procurement approval are different stages. Ask only for inputs genuinely needed for the immediate stage. Verify capability, certifications, minimum quantities, sample terms, lead times, and prices with this client's approved sources; do not invent them or apply a universal one-call-close target.

These are routing examples, not approved client offers or promises. Measure the client's actual progression; qualified held meetings may be useful without being the sole success metric for every B2B motion.

## AI reply design requirements

Keep these as design requirements until an implementation is separately authorized and verified:

- Retrieve matching context and actual message evidence before classification/drafting. Fail closed on missing identity, material offer conflict, unsupported conditions, or missing fulfillment facts.
- Expose the promise record, readiness, unanswered requests, required owner action, and evidence alongside the draft. Distinguish schedule agreed fulfillment, deliver asset, answer, clarify, wait, and escalate.
- Make promise fidelity a blocking review check, not a confidence score. Show added conditions, changed quantity/mode, missing attachments, and unsupported completion wording.
- Revalidate exact draft, asset/version/access, latest thread, suppression, recipient, and sender before the human send action. New replies or changed promises/assets invalidate stale approvals.
- Keep every client draft-only. No timer, confidence threshold, recovery branch, or context approval authorizes autonomous sending. Record fulfillment only on evidence; reconcile uncertain sends before retry.
- Track promise-to-delivery time, blocked promises, completeness/quantity, and client-specific stage progress separately from booking counts.

## Regression cases

| Given | Expected result | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dulcy explicitly offers 1,000 free usable leads delivered on a call; prospect accepts; owner can fulfill | Reviewable scheduling reply; record call delivery and verify full list transfer when it occurs | Reject every call, invent a purchase condition, or mark fulfilled at booking |
| Sender promises to email examples; prospect says send them | Supply approved examples by email or expose a fulfillment blocker; optional later call | Require a call to obtain the promised examples |
| Prospect accepts a report; asset is not ready and no deadline is approved | Assign owner; honest review-only status/clarification without made-up timing | “Attached,” “already sent,” fake link, or invented turnaround |
| Call delivery was agreed; prospect now prefers email | Seek an honest approved method change or explain a real dependency; preserve the original promise until amended | Lie that email is impossible or silently approve an unsupported change |
| “Could I build you 1,000 leads?” has no delivery method | Clarify method and real conditions before a commitment; do not infer call acceptance | Treat an asset yes as consent to a mandatory call |
| Manufacturer asks for specification compatibility and MOQ | Use that manufacturer's sources or route exact questions to its technical owner | Reuse agency pricing, invent certification, or force a 45-minute sales call |
| Another client's approved asset is the only retrieved match | Block cross-client substitution and request the right source | Reuse another client's proof/asset as if approved here |
| Call happened, but promised list was only previewed and not transferred | Keep fulfillment open with an owner and evidence gap | Count the held call or screen-share alone as delivered |
| Warm / Brief selected; old template begins with thanks | Answer directly in concise, considerate wording; no generic acknowledgment | Preserve thanks or filler because the tone is warm |
| Direct / Brief selected for the same supported answer | Use a short factual answer matching the selected profile | Reuse a padded generic warm template |
| Formal / Detailed or another language selected | Adapt formality, depth and language while keeping the same supported facts and promise | Ignore settings or add claims merely to make the reply longer |
| Selected CTA requests times but availability is missing | Expose the missing calendar evidence; prepare no invented slots | Treat style/CTA configuration as availability or send authority |
| Client/workspace style settings are missing or conflicting | Return the exact configuration gap for review | Silently assume Warm / Brief or another default |
| Configured call CTA conflicts with a prior email-delivery promise | Honor delivery and flag the CTA conflict; keep any optional call separate | Withhold the promised asset until booking |
| Office-hours/cadence profile changes while a draft is pending | Recheck timing and invalidate stale approval; remain draft-only | Send on a timer or claim the settings change authorizes sending |
