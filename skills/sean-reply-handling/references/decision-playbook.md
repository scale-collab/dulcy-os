## Action selection and mixed intent

| Current evidence | Best next step | Do not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Send the report/examples/list” after email/link promise | Deliver approved accessible asset, or assign exact missing fulfillment | Add a mandatory call, purchase or new qualification gate |
| Explicit acceptance of disclosed free call-delivered value | Arrange agreed delivery using configured method; preserve quantity/free status/transfer | Reject all calls or count the booking as fulfillment |
| Asset delivery mode never specified | Clarify real conditions with owner; honest review-only clarification if needed | Treat asset interest as agreement to a mandatory call |
| Call-delivery agreed, buyer now asks for email | Check an approved amendment or explain a genuine input/delivery dependency | Invent that email is impossible or silently make a new promise |
| “What does this mean / what do you do?” | Explain the client's approved relevant offer plainly | Diagnose an unexpressed problem or demand a calendar slot |
| “How does it work / why should I believe it?” | Explain approved inputs, actual process and tangible deliverable; add relevant sourced proof or limits | Invent a special mechanism, a result guarantee or a meeting gate |
| Price/fees/terms question | Answer approved number/range and actual scope drivers; escalate missing facts | Treat a factual question as an objection or always hide price on a call |
| Technical compatibility/certification/lead-time request | Answer sourced facts; route unknowns to technical owner or ask the one necessary spec | Borrow agency claims, assert unverified compliance or feasibility |
| Proof request | Supply this client's approved, relevant example with context/limits | Borrow another client's results or training-pitch metrics |
| “We tried this and it failed” | Address a stated failure with evidence or ask the meaningful failure detail | Blame their prior execution or guarantee a different result |
| “We have a supplier/agency” | Determine from context whether this is a decline or a continued evaluation; respect either | Invent supplier weaknesses or force a switch conversation |
| Vague interest with meaningful uncertainty | One useful clarification or concise explanation | Fixed BANT questionnaire, unrelated rapport or a question for its own sake |
| Explicit meeting request/time selection | Scheduling support after essential fit/purpose checks | Another interest question or compulsory nurture loop |
| “Not until Q1 / after relaunch / give me a week” | Retain exact review trigger and chosen cadence | Premature urgency, repeated links, invented reserved slots |
| Human referral | Verify real recipient, workspace, introduction context, sharing permission and purpose | Treat an OOO contact as referral; send or CC without authorization |
| Partnership/supplier/mentorship/interview-only | Route the actual motion to a named owner | Count as paid-service demand or misrepresent research as sales |
| Booking reschedule, failed link or missing invitation | Service repair with current event/route evidence | Start the pitch again or claim invite sent without evidence |
| Held/current customer/client-owned | Preserve open obligations and route through current owner | Send acquisition nurture to an active relationship |
| Clear no / opt-out / automated message | Respect policy and stop/no draft as appropriate | Default “one last question,” courtesy sales pitch or automatic alternate-contact outreach |

Mixed intent: enumerate all requests, then order safety/identity → existing obligations → factual answers → one optional decision. “Yes, but what does it cost?” needs the cost answer before scheduling. “Remove me; why did you contact me?” is opt-out handling; it cannot reopen sales. “Already booked; can you send the case study?” is a service answer/asset task, not another booking CTA.

## Mechanism and credibility

For a how-it-works or skepticism reply, connect three approved facts: the buyer input actually needed, the process the client actually performs, and the concrete output the buyer receives. Explain why those steps address the buyer’s stated issue; add only relevant approved proof and limitations. Use the chosen length and language rather than a mandatory three-part script.

Example structure, not approved offer copy: “Using [genuine required input], we [verified process], so you receive [exact deliverable].” Resolve every placeholder before presenting a usable draft. A manufacturer’s specification check, a research firm’s fieldwork and an agency’s campaign process require different facts. If the mechanism/capability is unknown, route the exact missing answer to the owner.

Do not replace an answer with a feature list or use “problem first” as a new obstacle. When the prospect asks for price, provide the approved price/range and scope directly; a mechanism explanation may support that answer but must not delay it. Do not add inputs or qualification conditions to a previously promised free asset.

## Questions that earn the next step

Ask only if the answer changes feasibility, relevance, scope, next stage or fulfillment. Prefer questions anchored in what the buyer already said. Keep the rest of qualification for the appropriate stage.

Illustrations, not approved client copy:
- Agency lead-generation buyer: distinguish needing more qualified enquiries from converting existing opportunities only if that changes the offer fit.
- Ecommerce service: distinguish improving an existing store from a new-market launch before suggesting the relevant service.
- Research firm: identify whether the immediate gap is recruitment, fieldwork, analysis or reporting, rather than repeating a generic growth pitch.
- Manufacturer: obtain the missing tolerance/material/quantity/delivery location that actually changes feasibility or quotation. If several specifications are indispensable, explain the need and offer the approved specification form rather than disguising a long questionnaire as one question.
- Referral: identify the right technical/commercial stakeholder only when not already clear.

Do not ask for budget merely to make a promised free asset harder to receive. Do not request confidential financial data casually. If the service truly needs sensitive inputs, use its approved secure process and the active confirmation policy. Do not put sensitive data into public search or unrelated tools.

## Meeting readiness and logistics

A meeting can be appropriate now when:
1. The buyer explicitly asks for a meeting, calendar or actual time.
2. The buyer accepts a clearly disclosed call-delivered offer and the client can fulfill it.
3. The buyer is considering the relevant solution, necessary questions have been answered, and a concrete approved meeting purpose will advance their stated decision.

A meeting is premature when it withholds an owed asset, evades an answer, ignores a review window, conceals fit uncertainty that blocks the offer, or has no credible purpose beyond “learn more.” A meeting may still be useful with unknown budget if that is not a required gate in this client's sales motion. Never invent qualification requirements.

Before proposing specific slots, verify availability and the correct calendar, future date, IANA timezone and daylight saving, duration, medium, attendees and scheduling authority. If only a verified calendar link is approved, use it when appropriate. If the buyer explicitly asks for another method, surface the conflict or use an authorized alternative; old no-link templates do not override the chosen mode.

Phone versus video is a material preference. Do not assume an email address supplies a callable number or promise the client will call without confirming the process. When a slot is selected, move to scheduling service rather than asking if they want to meet again. Booking/create/send actions remain outside this draft-only skill.

For Dulcy's explicitly agreed 1,000-free-lead call: keep 1,000, free status, eligibility and usable delivery intact; call completion alone is not transfer. A sample of 25 cannot silently stand in for the promised list. If fulfillment is blocked, route to the owner before presenting the call as ready.

## Client-specific motions

Agencies/services: approved preview → relevant finding → genuine fit/scope question if needed → diagnostic/workshop/proposal or meeting. Answer price and delivery honestly. Do not assume every service sells AEO, profit analysis or paid ads.

Manufacturing/industrial: catalog/capability → specification feasibility → drawings/sample/technical review → RFQ/quote → procurement/stakeholders → order. Respect approved NDA/access processes; do not sign an NDA, accept procurement terms or upload sensitive drawings through a drafting task. Certifications, MOQ, tolerances, production capacity, Incoterms and lead time require this client's approved sources.

Research/interview: preserve research purpose and stated conditions. Agreement to an interview does not authorize a sales pitch or qualify a paid opportunity. A later commercial discussion needs actual buyer interest and honest transition.

Partnership/referral: distinguish business development or vendor onboarding from end-buyer demand. Route to the relevant owner; preserve the original contact's exact introduction context and privacy.

## Follow-up and service recovery

Propose follow-up only if there is permission/status support and a useful reason: fulfill an owed item, answer an unresolved question, add relevant approved proof, act on an agreed trigger or make scheduling easier. Do not generate empty bumps or repeated calendar links.

Use the effective client cadence, clarified delay semantics, office hours and prospect's specific window. If these conflict or are missing, ask the owner rather than inventing a sequence. Prospect timing, opt-out, human ownership, booking, a newer reply and uncertain send supersede generic cadence. A timer may propose a fresh review/task only; it cannot send.

For missing invitation, wrong timezone, failed link or seller no-show, identify the operational issue and repair path. Do not attribute it to low buyer interest. Do not falsely apologize on behalf of a client for an unverified event; state the verified issue plainly and route the correction. Preserve reschedule interest even when the CRM still says no-show.

For post-reply diagnostics, assign one accountable owner plus backup and separate the clocks: inbound received, assigned/acknowledged, draft ready, reviewed, confirmed sent, promised asset delivered, and client-specific next stage. Use provider/event timestamps and the approved staffed-hours configuration; do not substitute record-modified time, conflate an automated acknowledgment with a substantive answer, or invent a universal response-time SLA. Identify whether delay sits with ownership, a missing client answer/asset, review or dispatch before prescribing faster follow-up or more volume. Creator anecdotes suggest what to inspect; they do not establish a causal lift or a required target.

When no useful new step exists, wait for the agreed trigger or return no draft. Persistence is not indefinite pursuit.

## Relevance complaints

When a buyer challenges research or fit, identify the demonstrated relevance mistake and route it to Scout/Atlas. Answer whether actual research was done only from evidence. Do not invent diligence, defend generic targeting as tailored work, repeat unsupported performance claims or replace the answer with a meeting ask. A tone complaint does not revoke an earlier opt-out.
