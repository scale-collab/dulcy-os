## Facebook Advertising

### FB-1 Managing & Scaling Ads
- [Pixel] Fill in a daily tracking sheet by hand: ad spend, leads, calls booked, calls showed, offers, price, closes, revenue, cash collected.
- [Pixel] The main early KPI is **cost per qualified booked call (CPBC)**, not ROAS. Course claim: B2C under $300 CPBC, B2B under $400, and higher for corporate or high-end B2B.
- [Pixel] A "winning ad" means CPBC is within KPI, it has closed several deals at about 2-3x cash ROAS, and the metrics are steady (course claim).
- [Pixel] Call an ad a loser only after spending 3-5x the target CPBC. Judge ROAS and CPA only over a longer period (30+ days), because closing skill also drives ROAS.
- [Pixel][Cole] Scale budget to match sales capacity. If you can take 3x more calls, you can spend 3x more. If not, hire closers before scaling.
- [Pixel] Scale by duplicating the winner at a higher budget and leaving the original on ("DON'T TURN OFF THE ONE THAT IS WORKING").
- [Pixel][Sienna] Testing order: first **variants** (new hook, location or video style, same message), then **new angles** (a different message, one campaign per angle), then new audiences.
- [Pixel] The video angle matters more than the ad copy. Change the copy only if it no longer matches the new video.

### FB-2 Meta Ads Training (main lesson, about 50k characters)
- [Pixel] The course compares funnel types. Group and DM-ad funnels are easy and cheap to validate but scale only moderately. A VSL is medium-hard, needs more budget and copy skill, and scales high. Direct-to-sale is the hardest. Course claim: DM ads reach "300-500k/m", and VSLs reach "multiple 8figs".
- [Pixel][Forge] If ads fail and the market is big enough, the cause is one of three: **offer, copy or media buying**. Fix them in that order of likelihood. Course claim: when the total market (TAM) is under 20k accounts, such as the Fortune 500, use cold outbound instead of ads. This is relevant to Dulcy's positioning.
- [Forge] Changing channel does not fix a bad offer. The course says its cold email division grew quickly only because ads had already validated the offer.
- [Pixel] Budgeting has three rules. (1) Aim for 30+ conversion events per campaign per week, with 15 as the floor. (2) Test for at least 4-6 days. (3) Spend 3-5x the KPI before deciding.
- [Pixel] Test structure: 3-5 creative **angles** (5 is ideal) and 5 audience "buckets". Use one campaign per angle with CBO, 2 ad sets (audience 1 and audience 2) and the same ad in each.
- [Pixel] Course KPIs by funnel. VSL: CPL $50 (B2C) or $100-150 (B2B); calls $300 (B2C) or $400 (B2B). Group: CPL $5-30, join cost 1.5-2x CPL, join rate 60%+. DM ads: about $30 per message for a direct offer, $50-100 for B2B. All are course claims and depend on the offer.
- [Pixel] Audiences matter much less than creative. Use interests *or* lookalikes, not both at the start (the course found interests better). Lookalikes need 1,000+ records, and the seed should move deeper over time: clicks, then leads, apps, calls and buyers.
- [Pixel][Lex] Avoiding bans: always log in through business.facebook.com, verify the business, turn on 2FA, keep 2 payment methods, have a real site with terms and privacy pages, and use only domains you own. Avoid swearing, politics, negative words and direct callouts such as "Men over 40". Warm up a new account with ads that get approved.
- [Pixel] Disapproval workaround from the course: change words and the thumbnail. If it is still rejected, publish a one-line, safe-image ad to get approval, then turn it off and repeat. (This is a policy-sensitive tactic, so Lex should review before use.)
- [Pixel] Optimization: if the account is within KPI, "No Touchy". If it is borderline, check each campaign at 4, 7, 14 and 30 days before cutting, and cut no more than 33% of campaigns every 4-5 days. The course almost never raises the budget of a live campaign. It launches new campaigns instead, and it may cut a slipping campaign's budget by 20-50%.
- [Pixel] Scaling: work out the budget gap, then size each campaign at about 30x the cost of the event it optimizes for, per week. Scaling types are vertical (duplicate at a higher budget, or optimize for calls/apps instead of leads), horizontal (new audiences, cross winning creatives with winning audiences) and "spherical" (a new concept or pain point, used last).
- [Pixel][Riley] Tracking: put the pixel and CAPI events (Lead, SubmitApplication, Schedule) on the funnel through GHL, ClickFunnels or Zapier. Add Hyros or Stape for VSL and application funnels above about $300/day.
- [Pixel] Retargeting: build audiences from site visitors, leads, apps, booked calls and engagers, run the best ads as dynamic creative, and spend about 10% of ad spend on it (course rule of thumb).
- [Pixel] Advanced daily workflow (for $10k+/month spend): a VA fills an "Ad Audit" sheet daily with Meta columns and Hyros sales, calls and MQLs, colour-grades each campaign, and decides cut or scale from the grades.

### FB-3 VSL Ads Launch Checklist
- [Pixel] Before ads: copy and script reviewed, creative approved, and a banner on the video that calls out the target customer.
- [Pixel] Business Manager setup: real LLC details (needed for verification), a second trusted admin, page and Instagram connected, dataset (pixel), domain verification through a DNS TXT record, and 2 payment methods.
- [Pixel] Funnel page order: headline, subheadline, VSL, CTA, case studies, CTA, FAQ, CTA, plus separate privacy and terms pages.
- [Pixel] Campaign: Leads objective, manual setup, name format "VSL Ads - Angle # - Date", Advantage campaign budget on, $100-300/day.
- [Pixel] Ad set: Website conversion. Optimize for Lead if there is an opt-in, otherwise Submit Application. Start at midnight, no end date, original audience options, turn off "reach beyond detailed targeting", 10-20M audience (no more than 50M), manual placements (Reels, Stories, Feed).
- [Pixel] Ad: turn off multi-advertiser ads and "All Optimizations", use the "Learn More" CTA, and select the pixel.
- [Pixel] Lead-form variant: "Higher Intent" form, 1 knock-out qualifying question (for example revenue above or below a threshold), prefill only name, email and phone, and a real privacy link (the course says Facebook checks it).

### FB-4 / FB-7 Lead Form & Application Funnel Training (same text in two folders)
- [Pixel] Prerequisites: pixel, connected pages, at least 1 approved angle, calendar and thank-you page, 5 audience ideas, privacy policy, and approved application questions.
- [Pixel] Course KPIs. Lead form: B2C leads $5-45 and calls up to $300; B2B leads $30-150 and calls up to $400. Application funnel: B2C apps $30-200; B2B apps $80-200. B2B costs depend on TAM and how strict the qualifying questions are.
- [Pixel] Decision timeline at $100/day: on day 4 ($400 spent), turn it off if leads cost over about $100 (or apps over about $150). On day 9 ($900, 3x the CPBC KPI), cut unless calls cost near $300, or wait until day 15 (5x) if it is borderline.
- [Pixel] Keep a campaign whose cost per lead is high if its cost per call is in range. Also keep one whose CPBC is high if blended ROAS is above 2:1, and keep testing new ads meanwhile.
- [Pixel][Cole] Poor lead quality: first rule out sales through call reviews. Copy is usually the cause, more often than the audience.
- [Riley] Application funnels need the SubmitApplication and Schedule pixel events firing.

### FB-5 Master Tracking Google Sheet (XLSX template)
- [Pixel][Atlas] It holds 11 tracking tabs: YT metrics, FB metrics (by day, by campaign), IG DM ads, FB group ads, lead form, upsell/low-ticket (ULLP) funnels, and one summary "SM" tab.
- [Pixel] Its full-funnel column chain: spend, CPM, CTR, link clicks, leads, landing-page conversion, CPL, applications, bookings, cost per booking, approved apps %, MQL, sets, cost per set, sales, revenue, upfront cash, revenue ROAS and cash ROAS.

- [Pixel] The checklist is the same as the VSL one except: Leads (or Engagement) objective, "IG DM Ads - Angle # - Date" naming, at least $60/day, Instagram-only placement (Stories and Feed), a chat template with a single CTA, and a real privacy link.

## TikTok Advertising

### TT-1 Setting Up & Launching TikTok Ad Campaigns
- [Pixel] Monthly budget = target CPA × units planned that month. Course example: $1,200 × 25 = $30k.
- [Pixel] Campaign budget: about 30-50 pixel events per week to leave the learning phase. Course example: $20 CPL × 50 = $1,000/week, which is about $143/day minimum. Optimizing for calls needs more, for example $150 CPBC × 5.7 per day ≈ $855/day.
- [Pixel] Even with a big budget, start a new account at $250-300/day to avoid compliance flags.
- [Pixel] Structure is simple and creative does most of the work. Campaigns: main CBO, main ABO (budget split evenly across 4 ad groups) and a creative-test campaign.
- [Pixel] The 4 ad groups: open targeting, broad stacked interests, narrower stacked interests, and stacked hashtags, with 2-3 ads each.
- [Pixel][Sienna] Creative test: use the best audience, $100-200/day, 1 ad per ad group, optimize for leads, and test 5-10 new ads per week. Course claim: a CTR "Above 2%" is generally a winner, so move it into the main campaign.

### TT-2 Optimizing & Scaling TikTok Ad Campaigns
- [Pixel] Scale move 1: duplicate the best campaign at the same budget and audit after 5-7 days. Or make several copies, keep only the best new one after 3-5 days, and kill the rest ("too simple... but it works").
- [Pixel] Scale move 2: duplicate and raise the budget 30-50% (for example $400/day to $600/day).
- [Pixel] Scale move 3: duplicate and optimize for a lower-funnel event, such as calls instead of leads. Lead cost goes up, but quality and CPA usually improve. This needs a bigger budget.
- [Pixel][Sienna] Scale move 4: put the best ad group into its own campaign with a different proven creative.

### TT-3 TikTok Ad Creative Breakdowns (video only)
- [Sienna] The only text is 10 hook titles used as ad openers, for example "I think I cracked the code", "3 businesses I wouldn't start", "What if I told you there was a way?", "5 reasons why", "Don't start a biz", plus one remake of an Instagram Reel. These are useful hook patterns: contrarian, list and curiosity. The breakdowns themselves are in the linked videos, which were not reviewed.

## Youtube Advertising

### YT-1 Hyros Setup Guide
- [Riley][Pixel] Use a custom tracking subdomain (a CNAME such as t.yourdomain) so browsers such as Safari are less likely to block tracking. The course says it takes about 24 hours.
- [Riley] Put the universal script in the head of every page. Connect ad platforms natively, without Zapier. Add a Google Ads tracking template to each ad group (campaign, ad group, creative, keyword and placement parameters).
- [Pixel] Turn on offline conversions with "Lead Offline Conversion" (the course stresses this). Google accepts sent-back conversions for only 90 days, so do not optimize for purchase when payment plans run past 90 days.
- [Pixel][Riley] Unqualified-call tracking: the application sends unqualified bookers to a thank-you URL that contains "?unqualified". A Hyros call rule then stops those calls being sent to the ad algorithm, so the platform learns only from qualified calls. This method works with any ad platform.
- [Pixel] Attribution: use "Scientific" (first click within 30 days) by default, check first-click for struggling cold campaigns and last-click for retargeting, and keep a 30-day window for long high-ticket sales cycles.
- [Pixel] The Chrome extension puts Hyros sales, calls, cost per call, ROAS and new leads (5 columns or fewer) on top of ad-manager views. Test by going through the funnel yourself and checking that the lead appears.

### YT-2 How To Edit Engaging Video Ads (outline only)
- [Sienna] Keep balance and edit for the audience. Use the "5-7 Second Rule" (it is a heading only; it probably means changing the visual every 5-7 seconds, but this is unconfirmed).
- [Sienna] B-roll and music should "enhance not distract", match the target demographic and always be copyright-free (the course uses Storyblocks and Epidemic Sound).
- [Sienna] Colour grading, captions and graphics keep viewers engaged but take a lot of time. You can do it yourself or hire an editor (Upwork, Fiverr and similar), essentially paying for speed.

### YT-3 VSL Templates & Examples
- [Sienna][Scribe] Two script frameworks. **Direct lead** is for B2B, agencies, SaaS and solution-aware buyers, which is the closest fit to Dulcy. **Indirect lead** is mainly for B2C and is harder to make work for B2B solo founders without a "VERY compelling idea".
- [Sienna] There is also a slide-deck VSL option for people who prefer webinar-style delivery. Add as many proof visuals as possible.
- [Sienna] Study successful VSL funnels in your niche (5 example funnels are linked, not reviewed).

### YT-4 Claims & Guarantees (also relevant to Forge and Lex)
- [Lex][Pixel] An income claim ("how to make 10k in 30 days") is not the same as a guarantee ("if we don't add X, you get your money back"). Avoid income promises in ads. A guarantee is fine if its full terms are disclosed.
- [Forge] Course claim: an offer is Claim (10%) + Guarantee (10%) + **Evidence (80%)**. With no evidence, you need both a claim and a guarantee. With strong evidence, you can drop the guarantee.
- [Forge][Lex] Never offer a guarantee without stipulations. **State** stipulations are what the client must already have, such as running ads or having a sales team. **Action** stipulations are what the client must do, such as attend calls, keep the calendar open, call 80% of opt-ins, or not touch campaigns for 7 days.
- [Forge] Put the stipulations in the agreement. The course claims a long stipulation list does not hurt the close rate. The guarantee examples in the lesson are other people's, so do not copy them.
- Dulcy rule check: this lesson's examples must never become Dulcy or client promises without real proof and Alex's approval (RULES non-negotiable 3).

### YT-5 How To Film Engaging Video Ads (outline only)
- [Sienna] Elements: meet the viewer where they are, good framing and background, good light and audio, tonality, and a mix of graphics, B-roll and talking to camera.
- [Sienna] Before recording: practise the night before, do several takes to reach flow, say the title on camera (it helps the editor), use fresh batteries and cards, and double-check that recording is on.
- [Sienna] On camera: manage your inner state, match your tone to the market, talk to one person rather than "the camera", and keep your own signature style.
- [Pixel][Sienna] For swipe-file research, use VidTao (a YouTube ad library) and the linked "7 figure YT ads" doc (not opened).

### YT-7 Choosing Your Target Market
- [Atlas][Forge] Cold traffic needs specificity: a specific problem, for a specific person, solved a specific way, as long as the TAM is big enough.
- [Forge] Specialists charge more, are simpler to run (like a "conveyor belt"), and can still feed a broader backend through a narrow lead offer.
- [Atlas][Forge] Choose the niche by listing your last 20 clients and scoring each 1-10 on how much you enjoy the work and 1-10 on results. The niche where both scores are high wins.
- [Sienna][Forge] Testimonials and case studies make up "80% of your marketing content" (course claim). Ask past clients for results now.

### YT-8 Funnel + Application Training
- [Pixel] Funnels: Ad → Opt-in → VSL → Calendly with questions → Thank you; or Ad → Opt-in → VSL → Application → Calendly → Thank you.
- [Pixel][Echo] Add friction (an application step) only after lead flow is enough. Send qualified leads to booking and unqualified leads to a lead magnet or cheaper product.
- [Pixel] Test 5-12 application questions. If leads are too few, cut questions. If too many leads are unqualified, add questions.

- [Forge][Atlas] "3S formula": Specific problem, Specific person, Specific way, in one line.

### YT-13 Creating Your Ads (YouTube creative)
- [Pixel][Sienna] Course claim: "80% of your success on YouTube comes down to your creative". People are on YouTube to learn or be entertained ("edutainment").
- [Sienna][Scribe] Use a "Hook, Story, Close" script. Reuse copy that already works (Facebook ads, organic posts, emails) rather than starting from scratch.
- [Sienna] Film somewhere comfortable with natural light and little echo. Do a dry run, check the footage, and keep your energy high.
- [Sienna] Post-production: captions (Veed), B-roll (Storyblocks), music and sound effects (Epidemic) and an eye-catching thumbnail.
- [Pixel][Sienna] Upload ads to the main YouTube channel as **UNLISTED** so they do not hurt the channel's organic growth.

### YT-14 Picking Your Model & Pricing
- [Forge] Two models that work on cold traffic: an 8-16 week course or coaching programme, and a DFY agency or B2B service. Both have high margins, which lets you outspend competitors.
- [Forge] Course price claims: DFY or B2B $15k-50k+, or retainers from $2,500/month, or performance-based fees (% of ad spend or profit). Coaching for B2B ranges $5.8k-30k+.
- [Forge] A hybrid DFY + done-with-you model works: course material fills the client's gaps (offer, sales, mindset), and group calls are extra support. An account manager is the main point of contact.
- [Atlas] Client management: move clients through journey stages in Asana, Monday or ClickUp, and paste the call recorder's summary into each client's card after every call.
- [Forge] Backend: make DFY "sticky" so clients depend on it, then add a mastermind or community layer. For coaching, a rough "2x the time, 2x the price" ascension.

## Most useful for Dulcy (top 8)
1. **[Pixel] CPBC-first decision rules.** Judge ads on cost per *qualified* booked call. Spend 3-5x the target before cutting, use 4-6 days minimum, and aim for 15-30+ conversions per campaign per week. Keep a campaign whose CPL is high if its CPBC is fine. Course B2B guide: calls at about $400 or less (FB-1, FB-2, FB-4).
2. **[Pixel][Riley] Send only qualified calls to the algorithm.** The "?unqualified" thank-you URL plus a call rule stops unqualified bookings from training the platform. It is the paid-ads version of Dulcy's separate booking states (YT-1).
3. **[Forge][Lex] Claims vs guarantees, with state and action stipulations.** Evidence is 80% of the offer (course claim). Never offer a guarantee without stipulations, never make income claims, and disclose terms. This fits Dulcy rule 3 (YT-4, YT-10).
4. **[Pixel][Sienna] Angle vs variation testing.** Use 3-5 angles, one campaign per angle, and the same ad across 2 audience buckets. Test variants (hook, location, style) before new angles. Creative matters far more than audience or ad copy (FB-1, FB-2, TT-1, YT-13).
5. **[Pixel] "No Touchy" optimization discipline.** Do not edit an account that is in KPI. Cut at most 33% of campaigns every 4-5 days. Scale with new duplicated campaigns rather than raising live budgets (FB-2, TT-2).
6. **[Forge][Pixel] Low-TAM rule.** Course claim: markets under about 20k accounts suit cold outbound better than ads. Bad offers fail on every channel. This supports Dulcy's cold-email-first position and is a filter before any client ads test (FB-2).
7. **[Atlas][Pixel] Full-funnel tracking chain.** Track spend, leads, apps, bookings, approved %, shows, offers, closes and cash ROAS over trailing 2/4/7/14/30 days, report on yesterday's data, and keep self-reported sales numbers separate (FB-5, FB-1).
8. **[Sienna] Lean video production playbook.** Use Hook-Story-Close, start with an iPhone, mic and teleprompter, use copyright-free B-roll and music, add captions, upload as unlisted, and remember that clear audio is non-negotiable (YT-5, YT-11, YT-13, YT-6).
