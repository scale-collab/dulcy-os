## 2. Lesson notes (my half, unread lessons)

### 1. (Quick & Easy) Indirect VSL Ads — DOCX ~4.6k chars
- Template: Hook -> "Daggers" -> Unique Mechanism -> Social Proof -> CTA. Course warns indirect ads need superior copywriting; get copy reviewed before publishing. [Pixel][Scribe]
- 4 hook types: Secret, Story, Promise, Problem; each followed by "Well, it wasn't by..." / "Here's what NOT to do..." open loop. [Scribe][Pixel]
- "Daggers" = list the common alternatives/mistakes the buyer already tried (e.g., for lead gen: FB ads, YouTube ads, TikTok, SEO), dismiss each quickly, then pivot to your mechanism. [Scribe][Forge]
- Unique mechanism outline: name it -> 1-4 sentence explanation -> benefits. Warns against overselling/TMI. [Forge][Scribe]
- Social proof: short "NAME got (result) in (timeframe)" lines; CTA: click, watch short video, book demo. (Dulcy rule: proof must be real, approved client facts.) [Scribe]

### 2. Ad Angles Training — DOCX (STA Ads Templates) ~17k chars
- Angle = a different way of explaining the SAME offer to a different want/desire. Variation is the key once an offer is validated; every creative eventually fatigues. [Pixel][Scribe][Atlas]
- Course claim: same funnel ran 2+ years with "millions" in ad spend by constantly refreshing angles.
- Angle research sources: TikTok ads library, competitors in Facebook ads library, YouTube. [Pixel]
- B2B angle patterns: (1) call out the problem + 3-5 numbered reasons you are the best option; (2) call out industry dogma ("999 fancy ways...") then the boring proven way; (3) direct problem call-out with "only 2 reasons why"; (4) list competitors' many tactics, then one simple boring method ("Sexy? No. Works? Yes."); (5) call out what they want to avoid + the benefit they want. [Scribe][Pixel]
- Hook frameworks (credited to Frank Kern): "yay minus boo" (get X without hated Y), "The truth about X", "What to do if X". Usable as cold-email subject/opening lines too. [Scribe]
- Course claims inside the ads (Closers.io's own, not usable for Dulcy): 3,486 applicants/month, 854 interviews, 61 hires; ~$6,600 revenue per call; "0 to 3.1M/month in ~2 years"; performance guarantee "if they don't book 40+ calls in month 1, you don't pay". Note the same ad set gives inconsistent numbers (2.2M in 18 months vs 3M in 3 years; 450 vs 2000+ teams) — shows why proof must be single-sourced and current. [Scribe][Forge]
- Risk-reversal line pattern: "if this doesn't work you don't pay, so we have no incentive to pressure you." Only usable where a real, approved guarantee exists. [Forge][Scribe]

### 4. Application Funnel Training Doc — DOCX ~19k chars ("Short Form Funnel V2")
- Funnel: Offer Ad -> Application -> Booking Page -> (optional Thank-You -> Homework page) -> Sales Call. Pitched as low-tech, low-budget, good for first validating an offer or paid traffic; course claim that great ads can reach the first $100-200k/month without a VSL. [Pixel][Atlas]
- When NOT to use: small-TAM B2B, enterprise buyers, TAM under 10k, or when a VSL/webinar already works (warns against "shiny object syndrome"; scale what works first). Most Dulcy client markets look like this small-TAM B2B case, which supports cold outbound over this funnel. [Atlas][Pixel]
- KPI chain to track daily: ad spend, applications, cost per application, calls booked, cost per booked call, shows, show rate, closes, close rate, revenue, cash collected, total CPA. Same stage chain Dulcy should report for outbound (sent -> reply -> positive -> booked -> showed -> closed). [Atlas][Pixel]
- Course targets: B2C ~$50/application and <=$300/booked call; B2B ~$100/application and <=$400/booked call (course claim, paid-ads context). Useful as an outside benchmark when a client compares Dulcy's cost per meeting to paid ads. [Atlas][Forge][Pixel]
- Direct vs indirect: B2B and B2-solopreneur are mainly DIRECT ("(audience) - we'll (outcome) in (timeframe) or (guarantee)"); B2C mainly INDIRECT. Advice: write both and test. [Scribe][Pixel]
- Direct ad structures: A) call-out, offer, 3 key results, restated guarantee, social proof, CTA; B) call-out, offer, proof, CTA. Test long vs short on both booked calls AND lead quality. [Scribe][Pixel]
- Strong CTA pattern: CTA, restate key benefits, CTA again; and "we have zero incentive to sell you if it won't work" when backed by a guarantee. [Scribe]
- Application grading: 1-4 scale; 1s and 2s go to setters as triage calls, 3s and 4s to closers' calendars; solo founders take all, then start cancelling 1s/2s if quality is very low. [Sean][Cole]
- Process gates: mandatory template use for review; course rejects obvious ChatGPT copy without feedback. Videos 4x5 (1080x1350) with a banner. [Pixel][Sienna]

### 9. Creating a Lead Magnet — DOCX ~5.5k chars
- A lead magnet solves ONE problem with a quick win, takes under ~30 min to consume and under an hour to implement, and calls out the ideal audience in the title/subtitle. [Forge][Scribe][Sienna]
- It should create the NEXT problem you solve (e.g., setter-model guide -> "now where do I find a good setter?"), so the natural next step is a call. [Forge][Atlas]
- Pick the format you enjoy (written PDF vs video); put a CTA to book a call every 3-5 pages. [Sienna]
- Framework: (1) short "how I solved this" story that builds authority (not rags-to-riches); (2) content in 3-5 steps, ~2 pages each, with a case study in every step; (3) "What's next?" section: book a call to get help implementing, or to solve the next problem. [Forge][Sienna][Scribe]
- Examples cited: a 12-page PDF ("4 Proven Setter Models"), a video + guide, a long ebook. Fits Dulcy's "front-end lead magnet or preview" offers in Forge and low-friction cold-email CTAs ("want the guide?"). [Forge][Scribe]

### 10. Direct Lead - VSL Builder — DOCX ~15k chars (VSL messaging + ad breakdown + funnel + VSL math + mistakes)
- Direct VSL is for B2B only: agencies, DFY/coaching hybrids, SaaS, solution-aware buyers who already identify with a market (realtors, advisers, consultants). [Atlas][Forge]
- Script order: optional pattern interrupt (0-5s) -> quick hook calling out who/circumstance/problem/desire (0-5s) -> ONE big claim/benefit + short USP (5-20s) -> big guarantee -> big proof/track record (20-45s) -> soft CTA -> mechanism -> hard CTA -> more case studies -> final CTA -> optional FAQ/objections. [Scribe][Pixel]
- Hook quality test: Unique, Useful, Ultra-specific, Urgent (plus trendy). Same test works for cold-email subject lines/openers. [Scribe]
- Mechanism formula: "Most people trying to get [result] do X -> the problem with that -> which means [consequence] -> so instead we do [method] -> which lets us [benefit] -> which means [benefit of benefit]". In crowded markets lead with ONE reason. [Scribe][Forge]
- Without big-name proof, "rapid-fire 5 case studies with quick before/afters in a specific time frame". [Scribe][Forge]
- Budget from the goal: calls wanted x target cost per call (course default $250-300) = spend; need >=15 (ideally 30-50) conversion events per week per campaign. [Pixel][Atlas]
- One campaign per ANGLE (different pain point = different campaign); micro-variations (first line, headline, image) inside an angle. Mirrors Dulcy's rule of one variable per test and separate campaigns per offer/angle. [Pixel][Atlas][Scribe]
- VSL math (course claims): ads ~20% of P&L (~5x cash ROAS); aim 3-4x upfront cash ROAS and 6-10x contract ROAS early; target CPA 33-50% of upfront cash; 20-25% close rate on LIVE calls; CPA / closes-per-live-call = cost per live call, then halve for no-shows/DQs = cost per BOOKED call. Booked vs live distinction matters. [Atlas][Ledger][Cole]
- Course claims: ~3% total-lead-to-close, ~10% booking-to-close while cancelling ~70% of applications; "scalable" at 1.5% and 5%. Ignore vanity metrics (CPM, CPC, CPL) as KPIs; optimise to CPA and cost per booked call. [Atlas][Pixel]
- Common mistakes: weak attribution data feedback; under-budgeting; testing tiny tweaks instead of 4 different big concepts, run ~5 days then cut losers (overspend and cut beats underspend and scale); no opt-in/email follow-up; "not outbound calling the leads"; good VSL but bad ads (ad matters more). [Pixel][Atlas][Sean]

### 12. DM Ads - Overview — DOCX ([UPDATED] DM Ads Training Doc) ~20k chars
- DM ads send people straight into FB/IG DMs. Fit: B2C or B2-solopreneur, first validation of an offer or paid traffic. NOT for B2B, small-TAM B2B, enterprise, TAM under 10k, or scaling past ~$400k/month (DMs get hard to manage at $100-200k/month spend; course claim). [Pixel][Atlas]
- Same KPI chain as the application funnel (spend -> leads -> cost per lead -> booked -> cost per booked call -> showed -> closed -> revenue -> cash -> CPA). [Atlas][Pixel]
- Keyword CTA: "DM me the word SETTERS". Messenger welcome flow: one-word greeting, required auto-reply that admits it is automated ("robot ... the human me will send details shortly") so the follow-up manual message reads as clearly human. [Pixel][Sean]
- Setter DM rules (strongest part for Dulcy) [Sean]:
  - Do NOT qualify intent-based leads before offering a call: no rapport yet, finance questions cause drop-off, longer threads lose people.
  - Ask ONE easy qualifying question ("what's your core business/offer?") — it is a micro-commitment and the course says it converts better than sending the link cold.
  - Then offer the link: "What's your week like? I can drop my calendar link here if that's easier..." -> "Here you go: LINK. Will you let me know if you find a time? My availability is tough sometimes..."
  - Send a bare calendar link (no sales page, no testimonials) so it feels personal; it can redirect to the application.
  - "It's me btw (not a bot)" opener; if asked whether the call is with the founder, answer honestly ("probably my team"). Never deliberately lie.
  - Trust the script, but stay human; change one thing at a time and only after ~100 conversations.
  - If the lead asks for info first, offer a quick 5-minute call / triage to get them booked.
- Course claim: founder scaled the B2B company to $400k/month with only himself in the DMs using this short script.
- B2B 2-message variant: "Did you find the [solution] you were looking for? Or are you still looking?" -> one qualifier -> link. Directly reusable as a reply-to-interest pattern for Josh/Reply Agent. [Sean][Scribe]
- 5 boosters: VSL as link in bio, 3 pinned "pillar" posts that explain the offer's thesis, daily Reels, regular story CTAs, paid shout-outs. [Sienna]
- "Overnight" mission-post ad: "I'm looking for (number) (specific people) who want (specific result). DM me XYZ." Credible real photos raise lead quality. Same structure works as a scarcity-framed cold-email opener if the cap is real. [Scribe][Pixel][Sienna]
- Lead-magnet DM ads only after offer ads fail and the team agrees; they need a longer message sequence. [Pixel][Forge]

### 23. Fb Group Content Strategy — DOCX ~5.5k chars
- Why content: highest-quality leads vs paid inbound, less exposed to ad volatility, supports value-based follow-up of the pipeline; best to run content AND setters together. [Sienna][Sean]
- "Pipeline equity" = goodwill in your audience not yet tapped. It grows by adding people and giving value; it shrinks with bad content and with every offer made. [Sienna][Atlas]
- Appointments from content track the number of offers made. 3 buckets: Direct offer (offer clear in first 1-2 lines; most effective, most under-used, burns most equity), Indirect offer (value story ending in a CTA), Non-CTA pure value (builds equity, worst for appointments). [Sienna][Scribe]
- Steering rule: check closers' calendar volume and audience equity each week. Calendar full -> post more value; calendar light -> push more offers and "pay it back" later. Same logic can steer how hard Dulcy's own content and nurture push for calls. [Sienna][Atlas]
- Baseline weekly plan: Mon engagement + personal/lifestyle post; Tue value post or live + indirect CTA/case study; Wed 2-step (bi-weekly) + direct offer or mission post; Thu promo for Friday; Fri live training with CTA, client interview or guest training. [Sienna]

### 27. Google VEO 3 Ads Setup — DOCX ~6k chars
- Method: start from a PROVEN ad already within KPI -> extract a 10-20s hook -> use an LLM prompt to (a) tighten the script and (b) split it into 8-second scenes with fully self-contained, very detailed visual prompts (actor look, wardrobe, setting, lighting, camera angle, motion) -> generate in Google's Veo 3 Flow tool (needs the top-tier subscription; ~100 credits per variation, course note). [Pixel][Sienna]
- Principle: use AI to make more VARIATIONS of a validated hook, not to invent new messaging. Same idea fits Dulcy copy tests: vary only the proven element. [Pixel][Scribe]
- Editing tips: heavy editing hides AI flaws and raises retention; layer under the base-edit graphics; speed to 1.15-1.2x and pitch-shift audio down. [Pixel][Sienna]
- Note: the example AI spokesperson reads a factual business claim; any Dulcy use of AI actors must not imply a real person or invent proof (Dulcy rule on invented proof). [Pixel][Lex]

## 3. Most useful for Dulcy (top 8)

1. **Answer an interested reply with one easy question, then offer the calendar link** (DM Ads Overview / DM Setter Scripts). The course says not to ask qualifying questions before offering the call. Ask one low-effort question (for example "what's your core business?"), then "What's your week like? I can drop my link here if easier", then send a bare calendar link. Change one thing at a time, and only after about 100 conversations. This fits Josh's and the Reply Agent's positive-reply flow almost word for word. [Sean]
2. **Work every reply fast and through more than one channel until it is booked** (Facebook Group Email Strategy). Ask for a reply keyword. A setter watching the inbox answers immediately, then calls, texts or leaves a voicemail, and keeps following up until a triage call is booked. This supports Dulcy's speed-to-reply and multi-touch rules. [Sean][Atlas]
3. **Measure the same stage chain every time, and keep booked calls separate from live calls** (Application Funnel, both VSL Builders). Spend, leads, cost per lead, booked, cost per booked call, showed, show rate, closed, close rate, revenue, cash, CPA. Work backward from target CPA and close rate to a cost per live call, then adjust for no-shows and disqualified calls to get a cost per booked call. Course benchmarks for paid ads: B2B about $100 per application and $400 or less per booked call. Atlas can use these to compare Dulcy's cost per meeting with paid ads (they are course claims, not a promise). [Atlas][Ledger][Cole]
4. **The "boring but works" contrast angle** (Indirect Hook To Direct Offer, Ad Angles 2 and 4). List the "fancy" channels the buyer has tried (ads, SEO, webinars, content), then offer the simple method that works: a person or system follows up with every lead daily. "Sexy? No. Works? Yes." This fits Dulcy's own done-for-you outbound positioning and its cold-email openers. Use only true, approved Dulcy facts. [Scribe][Forge][Sienna]
5. **Treat a new angle as a new campaign, and test variations inside one angle** (Direct Lead VSL Builder, Ad Angles Training). A different pain point needs its own campaign. Inside a campaign, vary only small elements such as the first line or headline. Every creative wears out over time, so refresh the angle rather than the offer. Test 4 clearly different concepts, cut the losers after about 5 days, and judge by meetings and lead quality, not by opens or clicks. This lines up with Dulcy's one-variable testing rule. [Atlas][Scribe][Pixel]
6. **Lead magnet rules** (Creating a Lead Magnet). Solve one problem with a quick win. It should take under 30 minutes to read or watch and under an hour to apply, and it should name the ideal buyer. It should naturally raise the next problem that you solve. Add a call CTA every 3-5 pages. Structure: short credibility story, 3-5 steps each with a case study, then a "What's next?" call ask. This is ready for Forge's front-end offers and for soft cold-email CTAs ("want the guide?"). [Forge][Scribe][Sienna]
7. **Copy formulas Scribe can use directly.** A direct offer line: "(audience): we'll (outcome) in (timeframe) or (guarantee)" (only with a real guarantee). A hook test: Unique, Useful, Ultra-specific, Urgent. Frank Kern-style hooks: "yay minus boo", "The truth about X", "What to do if X". A mechanism chain: what most people do, the problem with it, the consequence, our method, the benefit, the benefit of that benefit. Clear disqualifiers ("this is NOT for..."). Worst case and best case of taking the call. A CTA, restated benefits, then the CTA again. [Scribe]
8. **"Pipeline equity" sets how hard to push** (Fb Group Content Strategy). Every offer spends goodwill and every useful piece of content adds it back. When calendars are full, post more value. When they are light, make more direct offers, and repay the goodwill later. Sienna can use this for Dulcy's organic content, and Atlas for how hard follow-ups and nurture sequences push.
