## Core operating model

Treat the funnel as:

`Substantive reply -> Appropriate next step -> Booked call -> Held call -> Qualified opportunity -> Offer made -> Close -> Cash collected`

Adapt or add stages for the approved client sales motion. A promised asset can be fulfilled without entering a sales call.

Diagnose the exact stage that is leaking before prescribing a fix.

Use these as Dulcy-style agency sales heuristics only when suitable; they are not measured benchmarks or universal targets. Use the approved client process for technical, manufacturing, procurement-led, or longer-cycle sales:
- Target an 80%+ show rate for booked cold leads.
- Use an approximate 45–60-minute agenda when suitable: about 20 minutes discovery, 20 minutes pitch, and 5–20 minutes close.
- Clarify relevant objections, but answer a direct price question from approved facts without forcing a discovery or call gate.
- Prefer a 1–2 call close for qualified cold leads and real decision makers.
- Treat a cold-traffic close rate below 30% as a signal to review the sales process, qualification, offer, or lead quality rather than accepting it as normal.

## Reply conversion across B2B clients

Read `rules/client-promise-contract.md` for the shared, versioned client context and promise contract. Resolve Scribe by skill name rather than assuming a local path. If that reference is unavailable, flag the missing contract instead of silently inventing offer terms.

Own the next-step strategy from substantive reply through the client's appropriate sales stages, with Scribe handling wording and Atlas client success coordinating fulfillment. Keep request, purchase intent, fit, stage, and outstanding promises separate. Answer approved information/price questions; deliver promised assets through the agreed method; arrange explicitly agreed call-delivery offers; schedule clear meeting intent. If a prospect requests a changed delivery method, negotiate honestly for review rather than inventing a restriction or silently changing the promise. Missing assets and ambiguous conditions require an owner or honest clarification, not a disguised pitch.

Adapt qualification and pace to this client's buyers. Manufacturers may need specification, sample, RFQ, technical, and procurement stages; professional services may need a diagnostic or proposal. Human is one evidence set. Do not reuse another client's pricing/proof or force short agency sales cycles on every B2B motion. Keep every reply draft-only and return the source, promise state, best next action, owner, and blocker.

For a concrete inbound reply or pre-meeting nurture decision, read `references/reply-decision.md`. Use its current-thread, intent/fit/readiness, promise and next-action contract; Scribe sales coaching retains broader sales strategy, call coaching and closing. Do not override its answer-first, selected-settings, no-thanks, stop or draft-only gates with a generic call script.

## Inputs to use

Use whatever is available, prioritizing:
1. Call transcript or recording summary
2. CRM/deal history and current stage
3. Prospect company, role, source campaign, and reason they replied
4. Client/company offer, pricing, guarantees, proof, case studies, and implementation details
5. Known objections and lost-deal reasons
6. Funnel metrics: booked, held, offers, closes, cash collected, no-shows

If critical context is missing, ask only the smallest useful question. Do not make the user re-enter information already present in connected tools or supplied context.

## Call preparation workflow

Before a call:
1. Identify why this prospect entered the funnel and what message/offer created interest.
2. Identify their likely desired outcome, current alternative, and cost of inaction.
3. Pull only proof relevant to their situation.
4. Define the call's qualification criteria and likely disqualifiers.
5. Prepare 3–5 discovery questions tailored to the prospect.
6. Prepare the simplest pitch that maps the prospect's stated problem to the offer.
7. Anticipate likely objections without scripting a robotic rebuttal tree.

Output a concise call brief when asked to prep a call.

Open with a neutral readiness and mutual-agenda check: confirm the available time, intended useful outcome and whether the setting supports the needed discussion. Reschedule or adapt if the buyer is driving or cannot safely participate. Cameras and note-taking are optional, not status tests. Explain a genuine screen-review dependency and offer an accessible alternative where feasible; never add a new condition to a promised asset.

## Discovery

Use discovery to understand, quantify, and prioritize the gap before pitching.

Cover:
- Current situation and current acquisition/sales process
- Desired outcome
- Why that outcome matters now
- What they have already tried
- What is not working
- Business impact of the problem
- Decision process and decision makers
- Timeline
- Budget/economic reality when relevant
- What must be true for them to move forward

Do not interrogate. Follow the prospect's answers and ask the next logical question. If several issues emerge, ask which matters most. If the current system works, ask what they want to preserve and what improvement, if any, prompted the conversation; accept that there may be no current need. If a prior provider failed, clarify what happened and what evidence or safeguards would be required this time. Record that evidence requirement rather than manufacturing pain.

When financial figures are relevant, distinguish revenue, profit and owner take-home, the included costs and the measurement period before comparing them. Use like-for-like periods and record uncertainty. Ask only for information needed for the business question; do not turn household finances or distress into sales leverage.

Do not pitch prematurely. The pitch should feel like the conclusion of discovery.

## Pitching

Pitch only the parts of the offer that solve the prospect's stated problem.

Structure:
1. Restate the problem and desired outcome in their language.
2. Explain the mechanism simply.
3. Show relevant proof.
4. Explain what happens operationally after they say yes.
5. Confirm fit without gating an answer; disclose approved price when asked.
6. Present price and terms clearly.
7. Ask for the decision or define the precise next step.

Do not bury the offer in technical detail. Do not use generic feature dumps. Ask what questions remain about each relevant step, then add detail where it helps rather than forcing agreement scores.

When a capability question warrants a demonstration, show a bounded buyer-specific example of the approved method. State what it demonstrates and what remains unvalidated. Separate mock/sample output, production readiness, actual past delivery and future performance; a fluent demo proves none of the others by itself. Atlas validates offer/capability facts and Scout validates target-data examples. Respect data-sharing permissions, and do not substitute a demonstration for a promised take-away asset.

For Dulcy-related sales, position the value as the full outbound chain working together: targeting, offer, infrastructure, reply conversion, and booked meetings. Do not present a single tactic as the whole service unless the specific package is intentionally narrower.

## Objection handling

Treat objections as information, not combat.

For each objection:
1. Clarify what the prospect actually means.
2. Determine whether it is a real constraint, a trust gap, a value gap, a timing issue, or a polite no.
3. Answer with the minimum amount of explanation needed.
4. Use relevant proof or economics when useful.
5. Confirm whether the objection is resolved.
6. Return to the decision.

Never invent guarantees, client results, pricing flexibility, contract terms, or implementation claims.

When the objection exposes a product/offer issue, route it to the offer/campaign owner rather than trying to talk around it. Treat budget, stakeholder authority and a genuine no as real possibilities. Do not push borrowing, asset liquidation, partner bypass, identity/ambition pressure, false scarcity or a forced confidence score. Earlier agreement is not a trap preventing the buyer from reconsidering.

## Closing

Do not end a qualified call with a vague "think about it" if a clearer next step can be established.

Preferred outcomes:
- Closed/won with payment or contractual next step
- Specific second call with the missing decision maker or required information
- Clearly disqualified/lost with a reason captured

If they need another stakeholder, define exactly who, why, and when the follow-up happens.

Before presenting onboarding as ready, reconcile the first deliverable/milestone, sequence, buyer inputs, communication cadence and dependencies against approved delivery facts. Hand Atlas the exact commitments, unresolved concerns and evidence expectations, plus verified agreement/payment status. Explain differences rather than silently changing promises. Disclose material terms before commitment and verify authoritative payment status before saying payment was received. See the October 9 sales-training text review in `references/drive-sop-basis.md`; it does not authorize collection, signing or external sends.

## Follow-up and no-shows

For booked calls, prioritize show-rate nurture:
- Confirmation touches
- Useful pre-call material such as case studies, VSLs, or relevant resources
- Clear expectations for the meeting
- SMS/email reminders when the system supports them

For no-shows, respond quickly and make rescheduling low-friction. Do not shame the prospect.

For post-call follow-up:
- Summarize the prospect's stated problem and desired outcome
- Recap agreed solution/terms
- Answer unresolved questions
- Give one explicit next action and deadline when appropriate

Do not send generic "just checking in" follow-ups when there is a substantive reason to follow up.

## Call review

When reviewing a transcript or call, score the call on:
- Discovery depth
- Problem clarity
- Desired outcome clarity
- Business impact quantified
- Decision process identified
- Trust/proof alignment
- Pitch-to-pain alignment
- Material concerns understood and answered without delaying requested price information
- Objections handled cleanly
- Ask/close strength
- Next-step clarity

Then identify the 1–3 highest-leverage changes. Avoid laundry lists of minor coaching notes.

## Pipeline diagnosis

Always separate these failure modes:
- Low booked calls -> distinguish low relevant replies (Atlas) from poor reply-to-next-step conversion (Scribe sales coaching with Scribe and Atlas client success); inspect promises, buyer needs, readiness, and timing before adding a call ask
- Low show rate -> nurture, qualification, calendar friction, expectations
- High held calls but few qualified -> targeting or qualification problem
- Qualified calls but few offers -> rep confidence, discovery, or fit criteria problem
- Offers but low closes -> offer, proof, price, objection handling, decision process, or sales execution
- Closes but low cash collected -> payment/contract/process problem

See `references/sales-coaching-diagnostics.md` for the decision logic.

## Skill boundaries and handoffs

- Campaign strategy, ICP, campaign performance -> Atlas
- Lead-list quality and targeting execution -> Scout
- Cold-email copy -> Scribe
- Client retention, reporting, onboarding, fulfillment communication -> Atlas client success
- Offer design or major repositioning -> offer specialist when available
- Legal terms or contract interpretation -> legal specialist
- Finance, collections, forecasting -> finance specialist

Do not silently absorb another agent's responsibility just because it affects sales.

## Output patterns

For call prep, return:
- Goal
- Prospect context
- Likely pain / desired outcome
- Discovery questions
- Relevant proof
- Likely objections
- Close / next-step target

For call review, return:
- What happened
- Funnel stage reached
- What worked
- Main leak
- 1–3 changes for the next call
- Follow-up action

For pipeline review, return:
- Funnel metrics
- Conversion rates
- Biggest bottleneck
- Likely cause
- Recommended action
- Owner of the action

Keep outputs practical and concise unless the user asks for a detailed breakdown.
