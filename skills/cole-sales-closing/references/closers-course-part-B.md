## 2. Lesson notes (31 read in this batch)

### Hiring decisions and timing

**When To Hire Closers** [Harper]
- Hire only to remove a current (or clearly coming) constraint. Otherwise the team gets bloated. Before hiring, first try to change the model (automate or eliminate the work).
- Hand off the lowest-leverage work first: fulfillment and operations go before closing. Setting and media buying are optional before a closer.
- The founder should master 1:1 selling first and build a call library. That library becomes the training material later.
- **Ready-to-hire checklist:** (1) the offer is validated, meaning you have sold it yourself at 25% or better over 20–30+ calls; (2) you can give the closer 3+ calls a day (15 a week minimum); (3) the price allows a competitive OTE; (4) ops and fulfillment are already delegated.
- Hiring too early means low rep confidence, no baseline close rate to coach against, weak candidates (good closers want leads), and quick quitting.

**When To Hire Setters** [Harper]
- A setter fixes two constraints: **lead volume** (too few appointments) and **lead efficiency** (low return on ad spend or margin). Course claim: setters can lift ROAS from 3x to 6x.
- Requirements: a validated offer; 2+ active streams of "curated opportunity volume"; and the manager has done the setter training (you must "inspect what you expect").
- Hiring too early means setters struggle with no baseline process, quit, and you can't tell why they're failing.

**Need Both? Which To Hire First** [Harper]
- Go by the constraint. If the problem is volume or efficiency, hire setters. If calendar availability is the problem, hire closers.
- If the founder does both jobs, hire the setter first and the founder closes full-time. Exception: a standout closer is available, or opportunity volume is low.

**Can Setters Turn Into Closers?** [Harper]
- Yes, but they have to earn it. Put the best players on the field.
- Many strong setters lack "closer DNA". Give them a setter-lead role instead, which can pay just as well.

### Comp, OTE and contracts

**Determining OTE & Comp Structure** [Harper] [Ledger]
- Comp exists to drive the right behavior: **new business**, not ops, admin or fulfillment. Keep new-business sales separate from retention or upsell work where you can. Also check that commission per product doesn't steer reps to the wrong product.
- **OTE (on-track earnings)** = what the rep earns in a year at KPI. Work out the target OTE *before* you design the plan.
- **Closer OTE / month** = (calls given per day × working days) × validated close % (scheduled → close) × commission per sale + bonuses. Course example: 4/day × 20 days = 80 calls; at 25% that's 20 sales; at 750 per sale that's 15k; plus bonuses = 17.1k a month (about 205k a year).
- **Setter OTE / month** = (sets per day × working days) × validated close % × commission per closed set + bonuses. Course example: 15 sales × 300 + 1k bonus = 5.5k a month (66k a year). Use 3 sets/day if you have no setter data yet.
- Pull three levers to tune OTE: commission per sale, lead volume, close rate. Put **3 OTE tiers** on the scorecard: minimum KPI, good, A-player.
- Course claim, 4 talent tiers: setter 40–60k (no experience, hunger); setter 60–120k (mostly B2B, some experience); closer 70–150k (prior sales or business experience); closer 200–500k+ (B2B and professional services, proven high-ticket experience).
- The author's own closer plan: 10% of cash collected, plus 500 per 50k generated, plus 200 per paid-in-full deal.

**Closer Tracker / Closer Tracker (Revenue Based) / Setter Tracker** (commission record templates) [Ledger] [Harper]
- These are three workbooks with the same layout: an inputs tab (year, name, minimum collection, bonus amounts, tier size, base), a monthly payout dashboard, a commissions tab per deal (client, status, commission %, sale date, deal amount, payment terms, cash collected by month) and a transaction data tab (amount paid, refund, setter, closer, account manager, processor, deal-won date).
- Commission is **paid as cash is collected**, and only once a minimum share is collected. Default closer template: 10% of collected, 20% minimum collection, $300 per paid-in-full deal, $500 for every $60k collected.
- The revenue-based closer variant pays $1,000 per $100k of revenue generated, plus $500 per $50k *if* 40% of the cash on new deals is collected that month.
- Setter variant: $1,500 base, 5% of collected, $1,000 minimum collection, $500 for every $60k collected.
- Lesson for Dulcy: tie payouts to **collected cash**, set a minimum collection, and log refunds in the same sheet. Placeholder amounts only.

**Contracts & Agreements** (two links: Independent Sales Contractor Agreement and NDA) [Harper] [Lex]
- The ICA template (2020, linked and read) sets out commission per payment type, a bonus for every 100k of monthly gross, two pay dates per month, and commission **only on signed contracts**. Custom payment plans need manager approval first. Third-party-funded deals pay out once the funder pays.
- It also has a 24-month non-compete, non-solicit and confidentiality clause, at-will termination with 14 days' written notice, and contractor status with no tax withholding.
- **Lex flag:** the non-compete scope and the contractor classification depend on the state and country. A lawyer must review before use. Don't copy it as-is.

### Recruiting and interviews

**Recruiting & Hiring Setters/Closers – Intro** [Harper]
- An outline of that course week (when to hire, comp, scorecards, legals, recruiting, interviews, onboarding, playbook, ramp, firing).
- A rep who is an A-player at one company can be a C-player at another because of culture. Check fit yourself, and don't just trust a recruiter.

**Recruiting & Hiring – Overview** [Harper]
- Recruit externally (recruiters) and internally (your own audience and clients) at the same time to "stack the deck".
- **Update in this lesson:** Closers.io *no longer recommends mock calls for interviews*. They say mock calls gave many false positives and false negatives and added time for no benefit. This **conflicts** with the batch-1 "Mock Call + Scorecard Interview SOP" and the A-Players guide. Treat the newer guidance as current, and ask Alex which one to use.

**Recruiting & Hiring A-Players** [Harper]
- Hold A-player standards. A small team of A-players beats a big team of B-players. High churn is a sign of poor vetting, not a requirement.
- Course claim: about 10% of candidates are A-players. Use the process to filter out the other 90%.
- **Always be recruiting.** Keep a "full bench" of 4–5 possible reps, so you can replace underperformers without getting needy.
- Recruit types, best to worst: experienced and aligned with your offer and market (rare) > aligned but inexperienced > experienced but new to the market > neither (avoid).
- Recruit in widening circles: referrals → client base → audience → broad social → ads and job boards.
- **Pipeline:** ad or post → application + DISC profile (+ optional 1-minute video) → screening interview (20–30 minutes) → mock triage / scorecard interview (finalists only) → offer → onboarding → ramp → **60-day trial** → optimization. On the application, look for effort, honesty, income goals that fit the role, relevant experience, and a positive view of past jobs.

**Screening Interview Guidelines** [Harper]
- A Zoom call held after the application, personality test and resume arrive. The course recommends the book *Who*.
- Question bank: why this role, fit, the company, the industry; comfort with 100% commission and full-time; side businesses; past roles (likes, dislikes, strengths, low points, why they left); "how would your last 3–5 bosses rate you 1–10?"; sales history (revenue, price point, appointments, offer rate, close rate, best and worst month, process, lead gen); sales philosophy and books; career goals; start date.
- **Look for:** will (hunger), skill against the scorecard KPIs, coachability, honesty, clear direction, natural rapport, a clear story about past job moves, and income goals that fit the role.
- **Red flags:** never mentions failures, exaggerates, criticizes past bosses, has *both* no market experience and no sales experience, can't explain job moves, has a bad attitude, or wants only money.

**Group Interview Training** [Harper]
- A first-round culture screen. Move every "yes" and "maybe" on to 1:1 interviews.
- Myths it rejects: candidates don't need related experience, high-ticket experience or a great mock call.
- It looks for culture fit, self-awareness / EQ, and purpose and goals.
- Questions: recent work, what drew them, what stresses them, biggest weakness and strength, the environment they thrive in, their hardest time and lesson, and "who else here would you hire?"
- Logistics: book 1 hour and plan to finish in 45 minutes. Pick one slot for the next day. Put the meeting link in the calendar invite. Give a same-day 1:1 to anyone who can't make it. Email feedback to those you don't move forward.

### Onboarding, ramp and firing

**OB & Creating Your Sales Playbook** [Harper]
- Two training phases: **Ramp** (new → at KPI or let go; aim for "good enough", since the rep won't close like the founder) and **Optimization & Regulation** (push past KPI and remove ruts).
- Playbook outline: company onboarding (software, mission/vision/values, contracts, product, communication rules), then role onboarding split into General / BOD / MOD / EOD.
- Weekly meeting rhythm: Monday wins + projections + training (60–90 minutes); Tuesday and Thursday wins + projections + Q&A (15–20 minutes); Wednesday and Friday wins + projections + training (60 minutes).
- MOD training: in week 1 the new rep sits in on your calls with coaching after. Give access to 10–20 good recordings, 3–5 of them broken down. For off-call work (follow-up, messaging), record yourself doing each activity, then write the SOP from the recording.
- **EOD checklist:** EOD report; finish hot-list follow-ups; add today's consults to follow-up lists; update personal projections, the team tracker and the CRM (deal notes, follow-up tasks, handoff to client success); store recordings; check tomorrow's applications for red flags; **text to confirm tomorrow's appointments**; prepare for the morning meeting.

**Ramp Up & When To Fire** [Harper]
- **Days 1–7:** company and role onboarding, shadowing, 2–3 1:1s. At the end of the week, a 1:1 decides whether they start selling or get 3–4 more days. Asking no questions about the training is a bad sign.
- **Days 8–14:** setters work freely; closers take 2–3 appointments a day. Five meetings a week. Listen to at least 1 call a day. Do 3 live call breakdowns a week. Don't flood them with feedback: praise what's good and fix 1–2 things. Don't micromanage. For message-based setters, ask for a daily silent Loom scrolling through 10 conversations.
- **Days 14–28+:** full volume (3–5 calls a day) once their calls sound good, even before they hit KPI.
- **Closer cut rules (course claims):** judge on 30–50 calls minimum because of regression to the mean. At 20 live calls: 10–15% close and trending up. At 30–40: near KPI (20%). At **50: "judgement day"**, at KPI or out. If they miss, ask whether the rep got enough feedback, whether they are coachable, or whether the talent gap is too big.
- **Qualitative check:** meeting presence, proactivity, questions, hunger; whether call problems are technical (coachable) or mindset/tonality (harder); lead quality compared with the best rep; gut feel.
- **Setter cut rules:** chronically missing numbers for 3–4+ weeks without knowing why. Course claim: 1 set a day is the bare minimum, 2 a day is solid, 3+ is very good. Also watch for lead quality from their sets going down.

**Closer Ramp** (weekly rubric, weeks 1–5) [Harper] [Cole]
- Scores 1–4 on six areas: **Product Knowledge, Sales Process, Sales Skill Execution, Performance (vs. ramp KPIs), Preparedness & Follow-Up, Attitude & Coachability.** Each score has a written description. Examples: a 1 in Skill = "vague discovery… no tie downs… don't ask for the close"; a 3 = "leads discovery clearly… rarely forgets tie downs… drills down on objections".
- The rep scores themselves before each 1:1. The leader scores too, then they go through the gaps together. There are rep-notes and leader-notes fields.
- **This is the closest thing in the module to a call-review grading sheet.** It grades the rep week by week, not one call at a time.

**Setter Ramp** (weekly rubric, weeks 1–5) [Harper] [Echo]
- Scores 1–4 on five areas: **Sales Process/Execution, Follow Up/Show Rate, Time Management & Work Ethic, Preparedness, Attitude & Coachability.** It uses the same self-score + leader-score method.
- At level 4 for Follow Up/Show Rate, the setter knows why each no-show happened, runs a "consistent pre-call text/check-in sequence", and always fills the pre-call form for the closer. This fits Josh's inbox-to-meeting work.

### Managing the team

**Managing Your Team – Overview** [Harper]
- Course claim: "Sales teams will never exceed the level in which they are LED". The top 20% of teams it coached were all well led.
- Lead with inputs. Quantitative inputs come from the dashboard. Qualitative inputs come from meetings, EODs, 1:1s, call reviews, temp checks, live reviews and Slack.
- Responsibilities: coaching/training (meetings, projections, call reviews, Q&A, 1:1s, EOD comments); admin (grading applications, attribution, telling marketing about calendar capacity, rep admin, tracker accuracy); leadership (meetings, vision, projections).
- **Four phases for stepping out of management:** (1) the founder does everything; (2) hand off admin or hire a sales coordinator; (3) promote a sales lead + coordinator (at about 4–6+ reps, strong performance, 25%+ close; never promote out of a slump), while the founder keeps the morning meetings and EODs, about 30–60 minutes a day; (4) a full-time sales manager (course claim: at 12–15+ high performers, or about $1M a month in B2B). Most people hand off too early.

**Sales Management & Leadership** (master doc for the week; repeats batch-1 BOD/MOD/EOD and adds the items below)
- **Projections are not goals.** A projection is the minimum you'll hit with full accuracy. Coach reps to set projections they have about a **70% chance** of hitting if they do everything right (the culture section says 60–70%). There are only two reasons for a miss: the projection wasn't accurate, or the rep didn't do enough of the activities they control.
- **Meeting order:** wins (reps jump in, not round-robin) → announcements → projections (team first, then each rep: last period, lessons, this period, on pace?, action plan, "hedged"?) → training (call breakdown, drills/role-play) → Q&A. A rep who is behind and doesn't ask questions after being reminded can be let go.
- **Sorting applications/bookings into 4 buckets** (done 4–5 times a day, weekends included): **Approved** (stays on the calendar), **Rejected** (taken off), **OTF/outbound bucket** (could be good but not worth a closer slot, so it goes to triage or a setter list), **Setter set** (the setter tags "MINE – SOURCE – CLOSER'S CALENDAR"). Grade more strictly when the calendar is full and more loosely when it is empty.
- **Calendar KPI:** each closer has a minimum number of calls on the calendar (course: 5 a day ramped, 3 while ramping). The coordinator reports yesterday's availability to marketing each day.
- **Show-rate fixes, in order** (show rate under 70–80% is the trigger; course claim: 70–80% is standard for high-ticket): (1) **manual personal confirmation texts**; course claim: this alone fixes it about 90% of the time; (2) fix the application or setting process; (3) fix the messaging or funnel.
  - Text sequence, paraphrased: the day before, "Saw you booked with me at [time] tomorrow, still good?" Day of, if they replied: "Awesome, talk soon." If no reply: "?" One hour before, if still no reply: "Haven't heard back, are we still on in 60 minutes?" Don't automate it. For sets from outbound, put the setter, prospect and closer in one group chat.
  - Inbound tie-down on the booking form: "Will you 100% be able to make this time?" Add a thank-you-page video. Put the application before the scheduler.
  - Outbound: a weak setting call means no-shows. Causes: qualifying too hard, making step two feel like a step backward, or framing it as "come ready to decide". Use tie-downs and pre-call content that sells the method.
- **Sales manager comp principle:** with the same revenue, the manager earns slightly less than as a rep but has more upside and downside. Not taking calls is part of the "win".
- **Culture via intrinsic motivation:** set projections that can't be hit without follow-up and self-generated calls. Make sure the pressure is the rep's own (for their reasons), not the manager's, or it fails.
- Note: this doc's "Big 3" lists **four** items (it adds weekly 1:1s). The standalone Big 3 lesson lists three.

**Reminder: The Big 3** [Harper]
- When a team underperforms, check tracking, daily meetings and the qualitative EOD. Course claim: 95% of the time one of them is missing, done wrong, or not *used* for coaching.
- **Metrics to know at all times:** scheduled and live consults, show %, offers, offer %, deposits, closes, close %, offer commitment %, offer close %, call commitment %, call close %, units and revenue and front-end cash per product, total revenue, total front-end cash, collected per offer and per call, revenue per offer and per call; plus ad spend, three ROAS views, and the P&L (labor, marketing, overhead).
- Common EOD failures: not posted in Slack, thin effort, no coaching on them, not done every day. Common meeting failures: not daily, no drilling, projections treated as goals, low energy, no value, part of the team missing.

**Build A High Performance Culture** [Harper]
- Uninspired reps don't sell. Celebrate wins and keep meeting energy high.
- Pay should excite reps, but **significance** often drives top performers more than money. Recognize people weekly, give awards or bonuses monthly, and keep a visible leaderboard.
- Make results the culture: "There's no shame in missing your numbers. There's only shame in missing and not knowing why".
- Reps should spend only 5–10% of their time on non-revenue admin. Keep them out of ops and fulfillment.
- Use culture-check questions (from *Sales Management Simplified*): how it feels to be on the team, accountability, how big wins are celebrated, whether meetings energize or drain, and whether comp drives the right behavior.
- **Keeping A-players:** it is easier to keep one than to replace one. Keep investing in top reps. Check their clarity, necessity, inspiration and growth, and whether they feel appreciated. Promoting them to lead or manager keeps them.

**Promoting Managers & Leaders Within Your Org** [Harper]
- Build a leader in three steps: **Acquire** (you learn the skill first, ideally about 20% above what the business needs, e.g. 30% close vs. a 25% need) → **Transfer** → **Develop**. Or "buy" a proven leader and have them teach you, so you can inspect their work.
- Course claim: a 6+ direct-report rule. Above 5–6 reps, a lead or manager is needed.
- **Transfer is a volume knob, not a light switch.** Start with a player-coach as a 30–90-day test. Cut their KPI by 20–30% and add a small base so their pay doesn't drop. Define exact duties, e.g. 1 meeting a week, 10 call reviews a week with a written report, daily 1-hour training with each rep, an end-of-week report, shadowing new hires. The course says player-coaches in sales rarely work well, because their own numbers come first.
- Full-time manager comp example: a base plus variable pay in 5 tiers tied to 90/95/100/105/110% of team projection, or a profit share.
- **A manager's day is 80–90% quality control.** Example day: 1-hour huddle (wins, projections, call review split 50% good / 50% improve), 2 hours of deep work, 2 hours reviewing calls (at 2–3x speed; listen to the first and last 10 minutes; "where there's smoke, there's fire"), three 30-minute 1:1s, 1 hour more of call review, 30 minutes on EODs. Reps ping the manager after a no-show for quick coaching. Hand off the daily meeting last.
- **Develop:** a weekly 30-minute 1:1 with the manager plus the manager's EOD (status of each rep, team KPIs). **Check their checking:** review their meetings, 1:1s, interviews and their team's calls. Send them a problem call you reviewed and ask where it could improve, to find their blind spots.

**Sales Coordinator Scorecard** [Harper] [Riley]
- Mission: work with ops, marketing and fulfillment so reps are set up to win.
- KPIs: grade and approve applications every day (morning, 3 batches during the day, before bed); cancel or move weak apps; send emails to borderline apps; a daily calendar report to marketing and setters in Slack; correct attribution on every appointment (inbound source, or which setter and source); daily paid-traffic and outbound reports; check that reps did their EOD admin.
- Course figure: up to 4k a month. Fold the role into another ops job until volume is high (course: $10–20M a year).
- Dulcy parallel: this is close to Josh's role plus Riley's automations. Attribution and the daily calendar-capacity report could come from Airtable.

**SC Application Grading System** (sheet) [Cole] [Echo] [Atlas]
- Two scoring grids. The B2B grid scores **current revenue, lead flow (consults per week), challenge/need, ability to invest, answer quality, vertical**, each 1–4, then averages them.
- Result: **1 = cancel, 2 = move to setter (triage), 3–4 = approve.**
- Automatic disqualifiers (score 1): answered "no" to willingness to invest, wrong vertical, certain excluded business models. The second grid (B2C income replacement) adds "obvious incongruence in answer quality".
- Useful as a scoring pattern for qualifying Dulcy's own booked calls. The categories and cut-offs must be rebuilt for Dulcy's ICP; don't use the Closers.io revenue bands as they are.

### Tracking templates

**Personal Projections Tracking** (sheet) [Cole]
- A month block and 4 weekly blocks, each with Current vs. Projection for: scheduled and live consults, show %, offers, offer %, deposits, closes, offer commitment %, offer close %, call commitment %, call close %, units per product, total revenue, revenue per offer, revenue per call.
- **Formulas (checked against the sample numbers):**
  - Show % = live ÷ scheduled
  - Offer % = offers ÷ live
  - Offer commitment % = (deposits + closes) ÷ offers
  - Offer close % = closes ÷ offers
  - Call commitment % = (deposits + closes) ÷ live
  - Call close % = closes ÷ live
  - Revenue per offer = revenue ÷ offers
  - Revenue per call = revenue ÷ live
- Projection vs. goal method (paraphrased from the sheet note): set the projection (e.g. 10 units), raise it by about a third to a half for the goal (the sheet's example gives 13), then plan your activity to cover 13 while still committing to 10. You need to know your close % to do this.

**Closer Tracking (Automated / Manual Revenue), Expanded Closer Tracker Template** (sheets) [Cole] [Riley]
- These are team workbooks for up to 12 reps. Each has a daily data tab, month/quarter/year rollups, a team summary dashboard, a rep report, a product report (sold, revenue, collected, collected %), a **weighted leaderboard** (rank and overall ranking), and current-vs-last-month charts.
- The metrics are the Big 3 list plus total collected %, collected per offer, collected per call. "Automatic prices" looks prices up from a product price table (10 products, placeholder prices). "Manual" has revenue typed in per deal. The 2024 file is the earlier version of the 2025 one.
- The weighting formula for the leaderboard is not visible in the extracted text: UNKNOWN.

**2 Call Close Tracker** (sheet) [Cole] [Atlas]
- Built for a **discovery call → sales call** process. Fields: discovery calls scheduled and taken, sales calls scheduled and taken, **Call 1 show %**, **Call 2 scheduled ÷ Call 1 taken %**, offers, one-time fees (deposits), agreements signed, offer %, offer commitment %, offer close %, call commitment %, **Call 1 close %**, total collected and revenue, collected per offer and per call, revenue per offer and per call.
- It has separate product lines for **One-Times** and **MRR** (sold, price, revenue, collected, collected %), with daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly dashboards and a flag for departed reps.
- **Closest fit to Dulcy's model** (a retainer agency with a discovery call then a proposal or close call). Good base for Dulcy's own sales-pipeline fields in Airtable/Close, owned by Riley.

**Setter Tracking Sheet** (sheet) [Echo] [Harper]
- Setter funnel fields: leads assigned, total dials, meaningful conversations/triages, meaningful-convo %, sets, convo → set %, sets on calendar, live calls, sets offered, sets closed, show %, offer %, set → close %, show → close %, offer → close %, front-end cash revenue, collected and collection %, revenue and collected per call and per close.
