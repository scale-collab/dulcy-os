### Intro & Onboarding (program overview)
- Mostly program admin. The owner gets course material, Slack support, 1-on-1 calls with an account manager, a community and office hours. The sales team gets the course, call reviews and sales clinics.
- One useful idea: the clients who use support most get the best results. You must pull the value out yourself.
- You can use account-manager calls as live call reviews for your reps. Example question: "Is this rep worth keeping?"

### 8-Figure Blueprint (two identical files)
- **Stage 1, product-market fit:** get 10+ *cold* buyers (not referrals). Treat the offer as a hypothesis and pitch, learn and adjust. If you already have an audience, validate with it first.
- **Stage 2, Optimal Selling System:** build a "conveyor belt": a repeatable way to generate sales calls and a repeatable way to convert them. The offer follows the 3S formula: specific problem, specific person, specific way.
- In Stage 2 your only jobs are to generate sales calls, run sales calls and get clients results. The common trap is spending time on branding, websites or product polish instead.
- **Stage 3, productize:** hire client-success people, delegate onboarding and 1-on-1 calls, and build a set client journey. A full-service agency that does "everything to everyone" is harder to scale.
- **Stage 3.5, remove yourself from sales:** the usual failures are poor management, hiring too soon, too little lead flow and an unvalidated sales process.
- **Stage 4, master manager:** fix problems with a better team, not with more marketing. The cure is "QC, QC, QC" and 1-on-1s.
- Ride one working campaign for as long as you can. Reinvent only when it maxes out.
- Plan your learning by your current constraint. List the trainings in order and work through them.

### Determining OTE & Comp Structure
- Pay should reward **new business only**, not admin, fulfillment or CRM work. Keep new sales separate from retention and backend sales where you can.
- The commission on each product drives what reps sell. Example: 800 on an 8k product versus 1,200 on a 30k product.
- **Closer OTE = validated close % (scheduled → closed) × daily appointments × working days × commission per sale, plus bonuses.** Course example: 25% × 4/day × 20 days = 20 sales × 750 = 15k/month, plus PIF and volume bonuses, about 17.1k/month (course claim).
- **Setter OTE = validated close % × sets per month × commission per closed set, plus bonus.** Course example: 60 sets × 25% = 15 × 300 = 4.5k, plus a 1k bonus = 5.5k/month (course claim).
- Put 3 OTE tiers on the scorecard: minimum KPI, good, and A-player.
- Your levers are commission per sale, lead volume and close rate. For setters you can also add a small base.
- Talent tiers and pay (course claim): setters 40–60k and 60–120k a year; closers 70–150k and 200–500k+. The top tier is mostly B2B and professional services.
- The author's own closer plan: 10% commission on cash *collected*, 500 per 50k sold, 200 per paid-in-full deal (course claim; do not copy it).

### Diagnosing & Fixing Constraints (the key KPI lesson)
- Track the full chain every month: **ad spend → availability (open slots) → scheduled → approved scheduled → live → offers → closes (not deposits) → contracted revenue → upfront cash → backend upgrades → total cash → P&L (labor, marketing, overhead, net profit).** Leave owner salary, courses and legal fees out.
- Formulas:
  - **Booked ratio = scheduled / availability**
  - **Show % = live / approved scheduled**
  - **Offer % = offers / live**
  - **Close % = closes / live**
  - **Revenue ROAS = contracted revenue / ad spend**
  - **Upfront cash % = upfront cash / contracted revenue**
  - **Cash ROAS = total cash / ad spend**
- **Constraint 1, too little volume:** get ONE channel working that is consistent, scalable and profitable. Cold email and LinkedIn count if you can scale them.
- **Constraint 2, too little availability** (booked ratio near 100%): add a closer. About 5–6 slots of 45 minutes a day is right at roughly 80% show. Use 7–8 slots at 50–70% show. Target 4–5 *live* calls per closer per day (course claim).
- **Thresholds (course claim):** show below 60% is the top priority and below 80% is worth fixing. Offer % below 80% needs a look, though a very specific B2B offer can sit near 60%. Close below 25% needs fixing and 30%+ is exceptional.
- A low offer % usually means the closer is gun-shy, is disqualifying people who are actually a fit, or is protecting an "offer-to-close" ratio, which you should never track.
- **If ads are unprofitable, look at price FIRST.** Upfront cash above 50% suggests room to raise the price and add payment plans. Agency benchmark: 5–7.5k over 90 days (1,750–2,500/month). True B2B: 10–50k (course claim).
- Then improve messaging (a "new opportunity" hook), add outbound setters who call opt-ins, add content and retargeting, add a backend at 2× price for 2× time, and improve follow-up.
- Labor benchmarks (course claim): group coaching ≤20%, hybrid 20–25%, DFY/high-end B2B 25–30%, red flag above 35%. A pure-outbound, zero-ad model can run 40–50% labor. Target 30–40% profit or better. Usually 1–2 badly paid roles cause the problem.

### Group Interview Training
- The group interview is a culture screen. Move every "yes" and every "maybe" to a 1-on-1. Move 50/50 people forward.
- Myths: candidates don't need relevant experience, high-ticket experience or a great mock call.
- Look for culture fit, self-awareness and emotional intelligence, and real purpose and goals. Notice who leads, their energy, confidence and original answers. How they say it matters more than what they say.
- 8 questions: most relevant recent work; what caught your attention; what stresses you; weakness; strength; the environment you thrive in; your hardest time and its lesson; who else on this call you would hire.
- Logistics: book 1 hour and finish in 45 minutes. Pick one time the next day. Put the meeting link in the calendar invite. Don't wait for latecomers. Email everyone feedback.

### How Recruiting Works & Expectations
- Hire for **buy-in (non-negotiable)**, specialized knowledge and industry knowledge. Hire a 7/10 and coach them to a 10/10.
- Hiring steps: application, resume, mock call or call recording, group screen, group interview, 1-on-1, then scorecard interview and offer.
- Book the interview within 24 hours and finish the process within 1 week. Good reps have options.
- The mock call is a 15-minute roleplay of a triage call. Judge rapport, coachability, effectiveness and whether you can develop them.
- A new hire's success depends on ramping them properly. Book the ramp right after the hire. A-players quit when onboarding is poor.
- Treat recruiting like selling: the job is your offer (income, mission/vision/values, room to grow). Part-timers must commit to going full-time within 30 days.
- The CEO runs the screening and mock call, not HR or ops. HR people tend to reject bold salespeople.
- Unless your job is top tier (B2B, 200k+ OTE, strong case studies, plenty of inbound), hire people from online business, salespeople from other industries, or people from your industry who are new to sales. Hire on attitude.
- Management rhythm: a meeting every weekday. Every day: wins and projections. Plus a call review or training on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Plus a daily end-of-day report.

### STA Recruiting & RAMP Up Worksheet (checklist)
- **Before you hire:** do you have enough leads and sales calls? Is the offer and process validated with **10–15+ deals sold**?
- **Phase 1:** job scorecard, recruiting form, prep call, and a sales playbook started.
- **Phase 2:** playbook finished, group interviews and 1-on-1 scorecard reviews (all recorded), offers.
- **Phase 3:** contract, ramp rubric, onboarding (email, tech, payroll, mission/vision/values), weekly checklist, and the setter or closer ramp checklist.

### STA Recruiting Crash Course
- Flow: confirm the roles and how many → opportunity scorecard → prep call → sourcing → group interview (3–6 reps) → 1-on-1 scorecard review → hire and ramp.
- Don't commit to anything on the group call. Reply within 24–48 hours. **Record every interview on Zoom. No phone calls.**
- "GOOD REPS HAVE OPTIONS". Don't play hard to get. Sell the job and move fast.
- Build the sales playbook while you wait for candidates. Reps without a ramp and training tend to leave.
- **Ramp rubric:** each week the rep and the manager both score the rep on the core categories, then compare.
- Onboarding covers tech plus mission (how we get there), vision (where we're going) and values (how we treat each other). Repeat them often.
- **"The Big 3": tracking, daily meetings (2–3 a week is fine for small teams), and qualitative end-of-day reports.** "Self-managing reps do not exist."
- Share a new rep's numbers weekly and do 1–2 call reviews in the first 3–4 weeks. Some good reps ramp slowly, so track, train and be patient.

### Setter RAMP Checklist (DOCX) and Setter Ramp rubric (XLSX)
- **Week 1:** onboarding, mission/vision/values, team intro, playbook, testimonials, **daily mock calls, no live calls**, daily team meeting (on time), daily end-of-day report.
- **Week 2:** mock calls Monday, Wednesday and Friday, a daily 30-minute 1-on-1, **1–2 sets a day from at least 10–15 leads a day**.
- **Week 3:** weekly 1-on-1, one mock call, **2–3 sets a day from 15–20 leads a day**. Training on follow-up, pipeline management and skilled questions.
- **Week 4+:** **3+ sets a day.** No mock calls unless the setter is under KPI (course claim targets).
- **Rubric columns (score 1–4, weekly tabs Weeks 1–5):** Sales Process/Execution; Follow-Up/Show Rate; Time Management & Work Ethic; Preparedness; Attitude & Coachability. Rows: rep score, leader score, rep notes, leader notes.
- How to use it: the rep scores themselves before each 1-on-1, and the leader coaches on the gaps between the two scores.
- A level-4 setter has a high show rate thanks to a consistent pre-call text and check-in sequence, always fills in the closer's pre-call notes, and has a high say-do ratio. A level-3 setter does the first follow-up but has no long-term follow-up system.

### Closer Ramp rubric (XLSX, in the Closer RAMP lesson)
- **Columns (1–4):** Product Knowledge; Sales Process; Sales Skill Execution; Performance vs ramp KPI (not close / progressing / meets / exceeds); Preparedness & Follow-Up; Attitude & Coachability. Same weekly rep-versus-leader format.
- Level 3 skill: leads discovery clearly, moves between sections smoothly, rarely forgets tie-downs (small yes-questions that confirm agreement), digs into objections, and closes some deals on the first call.
- Level 4 follow-up: reviews and diagnoses their own calls, follows up with every prospect after the call, and keeps working the pipeline.

### Tracking Systems: KPI columns and formulas
**Closer Tracker (Manual Prices / Automatic Prices).** It has a team dashboard (up to 12 reps), per-rep monthly, quarterly and yearly rollups, a product report, a leaderboard and a daily calendar for input. The Automatic version adds a price list, so revenue = units × price.
- Daily inputs: Schedule Calls, Live Calls, Offers, Deposits, Closes, and per product: Sold, Revenue $, Collected $.
- Derived columns (inferred from names; they match the Projections template, whose math is checked below):

  - **Offer % = Offers / Live**
  - **Offer Commitment % = (Deposits + Closes) / Offers**
  - **Offer Close % = Closes / Offers**
  - **Call Commitment % = (Deposits + Closes) / Live**
  - **Call Close % = Closes / Live**
  - **Total Collected % = Collected / Revenue**
  - Collected per Offer, Collected per Call, Revenue per Offer, Revenue per Call
- Weighted leaderboard: **Closes 40%, Total Revenue 20%, Call Close % 40%.**

**Setter Tracker v.0124.** Columns: Leads Assigned, Total Dials, Meaningful Convos/Triages, Sets, Sets On Calendar, Live Calls, Sets Offered, Sets Closed, FE Cash Revenue, FE Cash Collected, Revenue/Call, Revenue/Close.
- **Meaningful Convos % = convos / dials** (inferred)
- **Convo-to-Set % = sets / convos** (inferred)
- **Show % = live / sets on calendar** (inferred)
- **Offer % = offered / live** (inferred)
- **Set-to-Close % = closed / sets** (inferred)
- **Show-to-Close % = closed / live** (inferred)
- **Offer-to-Close % = closed / offered** (inferred)
- **FE Cash Collection % = collected / revenue** (inferred)
- Headline tiles: Total Dials, FE Cash Collected, Sets Closed, Offer-to-Close %.

**2-Call Close Tracker.** Built for a discovery call followed by a sales call, which fits an agency model.
- Columns: Discovery Calls Scheduled, Discovery Calls Taken, Sales Calls Scheduled, Sales Calls Taken, Offers, One-Time Fees (Deposits), Agreements Signed, Total Collected, Total Revenue, Collected/Offer.
- Rates:
  - **Call 1 Show % = discovery taken / discovery scheduled** (inferred)
  - **Call 2 Sched / Call 1 Taken % = sales calls scheduled / discovery taken** (inferred)
  - **Call 2 Show % = sales taken / sales scheduled** (inferred)
  - Offer %, Offer Commitment %, Offer Close %, Call Commitment %, Call 1 Close %, Call 2 Close %, Total Collected %
- The product report splits **MRR** (units sold, price, revenue, collected, collected %) from **one-time fees** (sold, price, revenue, collected, collected %).
- Rollups: daily, weekly (current vs previous week), monthly, quarterly, year-to-date and rep average.

**Projections (personal).** Monthly block plus 4 weekly blocks, each with Current and Projection columns. Formulas checked against the demo values:
- **Show % = Live / Scheduled** (9/15 = 60%)
- **Offer Commitment % = (Deposits + Closes) / Offers** ((3+6)/15 = 60%)
- **Offer Close % = Closes / Offers** (6/15 = 40%)
- **Call Close % = Closes / Live** (6/9 = 66.7%)
- **Revenue per Offer = Revenue / Offers**; **Revenue per Call = Revenue / Live**
- Goal-setting note: project the number you will hit (for example 10). Set the goal 33–50% higher (13). Plan your activity for the goal, and know your close % so you can plan around it.

### An Easy Path To Success If You Have Grit
- For unvalidated offers under about 50–100k a month: model an offer that already works, make it different and better, and treat it as a hypothesis.
- **Outbound-call and text every lead yourself. Don't wait for them to book.** Email the list daily. Hire setters only after it's validated.
- Mastering sales is the highest-leverage skill. Course claim: the author took 17 calls in a month and closed 13, and converted 50% of backend upgrade calls. A high price helped.

### Offer Creation Overview (+ Cheat Sheet)
- Offer creation only has to answer two questions: **Will it work with cold traffic? Is it scalable?** Don't spend 3–4 weeks stuck. Pick a proven offer, research it and launch.
- B2B mistakes: a market that's too narrow (aim for 40–50k+ prospects, ideally 80–100k, unless you're selling to large corporates); selling an info or mindset offer to businesses; too little 1-on-1 or done-for-you work, since businesses rarely consume info products.
- **An ad should solve ONE problem**, even if the program solves 50.
- If nobody else is successfully selling something similar to the same audience, probably don't do it.
- B2B should usually be a direct offer. A unique mechanism helps in crowded or jaded markets.
- **Cheat sheet (one page):**
  - Niche: specific person, specific problem, specific way
  - High-level promise: outcome, timeline, guarantee
  - 3–5 pillars (the steps you take clients through)
  - Fulfillment and onboarding: how you deliver
  - Price
- Setup list: CRM pipeline (leads → setters, deal status, email/SMS automations, calendars), a contract reviewed by an attorney, email marketing, a dialer, and trackers. "If it's just you, use the Closer Tracker."

### Designing A Compelling & Scalable Offer
- People buy when there is a gap between where they are and where they want to be, AND they want to close it faster, can't do it alone, or doing it alone would cost more.
- Two kinds of gap: pain (below par → par) and unmet desire (par → excellent). Each works on a surface level and an emotional level.
- **You can't create desire, only channel it.** Find it with about 30 research calls with customers and by studying ads that have run for a long time.
- Evolution beats revolution: copy what already works, add a clear advantage and execute at a very high level. Possible advantages: better IP, fulfillment, average order value or lifetime value, acquisition, method, or a different demographic or psychographic.
- **Why the 3S formula matters:** cold traffic needs specificity; specialists charge more; delivering the same thing every time keeps operations simple; and you can use it as a *lead offer* that feeds a broader backend. A DFY agency can keep full service behind it.
- **Start from your client list:** who do you like working with most, who do you get the best results for, who is easiest to serve, and who is easiest to sell?
- Mechanism template: "Most people do X → the problem is Y → which means Z → so instead we do M → which lets us get B → which ultimately means B2."
- Offers that sell well for B2B: ones that bring the client new customers, or "give $100 for $20" (for example, reviving dead customers).
- You're not married to the offer: hypothesis → test → learn → new hypothesis.
- Final checklist: proven before? Growing or neutral market? Is it a skill you have or can learn? Can you find the market? Market size over 30k? A clear advantage? Existing desire?

### Pricing (section inside the Designing doc)
- **Set the first price** by checking competitor prices and the value delivered. Aim for about 1/10 of the value. Match or beat competitors on price while giving much more value. It's fine to start a bit low and step up.
- Benchmarks, with at most 3–5 payments over 90–150 days (course claim): health 2,800 → 5,800–8,800; relationships 3,800 → 5,800–7,800; biz-opp 4,800 → 9,800; solopreneur 5,800 → 9,800–12k; **B2B 5,800–48k+**.
- **Raise the price when:** day-1 upfront cash is above 40%, actual receivables collected are above 80%, and close rate is above 20–25% (or simply above KPI) (course claim).
- **Step size:** cautious +400–600, normal +1k, aggressive +2k.
- **Roll it out:** your best rep sells the new price first. After about 10 calls of proof, roll it out to the team.
- Payment plans should last no longer than the front-end program, with a 5-payment maximum and no interest. Still collect as much upfront as possible.
- **Commission trap:** don't keep a 15% rate set for a low price as the price rises. Step the rate down (for example to about 10%).

### Copy Platform - Advanced
- **Headline by awareness:** if they know the product, lead with the product; if they know the desire, lead with the desire; if they only know the problem, lead with the problem. Make it ultra-specific, unique, useful and urgent.
- Hook = name the audience + a specific situation + a new idea. Promise = the benefit of consuming *this message*.
- Feature → benefit → benefit of the benefit. Cost of not having it → cost of that cost.
- Words that pull: New, Only, Big, Fast, Easy, Safe, Predictable, Simple. Always answer: why should I listen to you, what's in it for me, why now?
- **Proof ranked:** in-person referral > documentary > video > written. Also case studies of people like the prospect, endorsements, results in advance, the reason-why mechanism, and specificity ("$1,347" beats "1000s").
- Write to 3–5 real best-fit clients. State the insight (the real reason they're stuck) and the "new opportunity" that explains why past attempts failed.
- **Marketing thesis template:** "If I can make people believe that [new opportunity] is the key to [desire], and it's only obtainable through [mechanism]… they buy." Marketing makes sale #1 (the method). The sales call makes sale #2 (you).
- Also list: what they try now and why they dislike it; what they won't give up; objections (about themselves, their situation, and the method) plus "why not from you".

### Market Research - Advanced
- Competitor teardown, top 3 competitors: positioning, deliverables, price, payment terms, bonuses, guarantees, headlines, USP, claims, mechanism, testimonials, ads. Keep a swipe file.
- Customer profile: what keeps them up at night, fears, who they're angry at, top 3 daily frustrations, secret desire, decision style, jargon.
- If you've already had sales calls, list: how calls were generated, traits of buyers versus people who said no, how you described the offer on calls that closed, objections pulled from recordings, and your answers to them.
- Best research: "book 20–30 calls with your customers" and run them like a sales call.
- Awareness levels: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware. Market sophistication stages 1–5: simple claim → bigger claim → mechanism → more detailed mechanism → identity-based copy.

### Proven Offers & Funnels
- Proven B2B offers include cold-email agencies, marketing agencies (contractors, ecommerce), email agencies, recruiting and fractional CFO.
- **Lead-gen path for B2B with a small market or corporate buyers (under 50k prospects): cold email or cold LinkedIn.** Markets of 50k+ default to Meta ads with a direct video sales letter (VSL). LinkedIn and Google ads were untested by them.

### The Supreme Skill Slide Deck (partial)
- Thesis: "Better offers make marketing & selling easy."
- **Offer elements:** deliverables, functional benefits, experiential benefits, emotional benefits, price, terms, bonuses, risk reversal, reason to act now.
- **An attractive solution** is quick and easy to start, gets fast results, looks new and better than the options, solves a significant problem, can be partly done-for-you, gives the buyer confidence, and leads naturally to an upsell.

### Aloware Lead Flow SOP
- **Speed to lead is the biggest lever.** New leads (opted in within 24 hours) always come first.
- Course claims: contact within 10 minutes = 400% more meaningful conversations. Old leads connect 11% of the time on the first call and 90% by the 6th touch.
- **On opt-in:** text within 5 minutes (friendly, mention what they clicked, ask a simple question). No reply by minute 10 → call twice.
- Run two queues: one for new leads and replies (priority), one power-dialing leads 1–4 days old with a "bumping this up" message on no-answer. Repeat all day.
- Spend one hour a day re-dialing every lead from that day. Course claim: calls after 4pm get 164% more meaningful conversations, so do this in the last 1–1.5 hours.
- Filter out existing clients, people already booked and job candidates before dialing.

### Hiring, Ramping & Leading A-Players (deck)
- **Before hiring reps you need a validated offer plus an Optimal Selling System** (repeatable leads plus a repeatable sales process). OTE follows from lead flow, pay plan and the founder's baseline close rate.
- Hire for specialized knowledge, industry knowledge and buy-in. Mid and senior roles need at least 2 of the 3. The best recruits already sell something similar successfully, and you make them a better offer.
- Sources, best to worst: referrals from good reps in your industry → outside your industry → general referrals → your client list → outbound to employed reps → job boards. "Interview at least 20 people before you make a hire" if you're new to it.
- Interview probes: last 3 jobs (and "any other reason you left?"), their hero, the hill they'd die on, their hardest season (shows whether they own their outcomes), and their 5-year goals plus how this job gets them there.
- **Rule of 2:** hire two at a time, train them together, build the playbook live and record everything.
- **28-day ramp:**
  - Days 1–7: three 1-on-1s a week, onboarding, the call library, shadowing, daily meetings, daily roleplay.
  - Days 8–14: half volume for inbound closers. Review 1–2 calls a day and give pointed feedback on ONE. Expect 8–12 live calls and want at least 1 close.
  - Days 14–28: full volume.
- **When to fire (course claim):** 10 live calls → 1 close; 20 → 10–15% and trending up; 30–40 → near KPI (about 20%); 50 live calls = decision day. Fewer calls is mostly variance. Also weigh qualitative signs: meetings, proactivity, hunger, how calls sound (technical issues are coachable; mindset and tonality are harder), and lead quality compared with the best rep.
- **Daily meeting:** wins (people jump in, not round-robin), announcements, projections, training, call review, roleplay.
- **Projections are not goals.** A projection is the minimum the rep hits about 80% of the time if they do everything in their control. A miss means the projection was inaccurate or the inputs were too low. Once reps are accurate, stretch projections to 60–70% odds so follow-up and self-generated deals become necessary.
- Keep reps to about 10% admin. Their only jobs are generating calls and running them. Keep them out of ops and fulfillment. Course claim pay ranges: SDR 4–9k a month; closers 8–12k and 15–25k a month.

### Leadership & Culture
- A healthy organization is the edge competitors can't copy. Disorder grows as the team grows.
- The 20% that matters: **mission, vision and values alignment** (over-communicate it; "vision leaks, values drift"), **structure** (10–12 direct reports at most; teams of 4–8), and **cadence**.
- Cadence: monthly all-hands; daily department meeting (wins, announcements, projections, breakout training); a weekly 30-minute 1-on-1 ("how are you personally / having fun and growing / pacing to goals / where to improve / bring a call"); daily tracking; and a weekly per-account QC done inside the 1-on-1.
- Meetings: "You get what you tolerate." Cameras and laptops on. Lateness is announced in advance. Encourage healthy conflict. A silent attendee is a warning sign, so raise it privately. "Manage agreements, not expectations."
- What a prepared answer to "are we on pace?" sounds like: "No, we're slightly behind and need about 1.5 sales a day until Friday, so we'll send an extra email."
- **Hard-conversation steps:** set the frame → address their assumptions ("am I fired?") → state the outcome you want for them → attack the behavior, not the person → tie it to values → rate the severity 1–10 → show support → get agreements.
- Order of escalation: clear feedback → a written improvement plan with a deadline (PIP) → let go.
- **Missed projections:** a missed week is fine if they know why and have a plan. A missed month requires a written report 24 hours before next month's projections: why, ownership, plan, lessons. Reject victim language ("it's the leads").
- Drama: get both people on a call right away. Opinions aren't principles. Leadership = moral authority, groundedness and real expertise.

### Offer Models & Delivery
- **Three models.**
  - (1) Evergreen group coaching, mostly B2C: 3,800–9,800 for lifetime access.
  - (2) 8–16 week intensive: 5,800–15k.
  - (3) **DFY agency / high-end B2B:** 15–50k+, or retainers from 2,500 a month, or performance-based (% of ad spend, % of profit after acquisition cost) (course claim).
- Choose by asking how long the client takes to succeed. Short → intensive. Long → evergreen.
- **For DFY:** a dedicated account manager is the single point of contact and coordinates the internal teams. Add short course material that fills the client's gaps (for example, a lead-gen agency teaches clients sales and follow-up). Group calls are optional.
- **Make DFY "sticky":** would the client be in trouble if you went away? Best combination: sticky DFY plus a backend community or mastermind.
- Account managers run 1-on-1 onboarding, a 1-on-1 every 2–4 weeks and chat support, and map each client's implementation path. They earn about 10% commission on upsells (course claim).
- Note every client in the CRM and recap each client's progress weekly. Backend: 2× time at 2× price. What drives backend sales: community, and leadership in solving the client's *next* problem.

### CSM / AM Scorecard Example
- Mission: fast support, deep support, and retention.
- Outcomes: 60–65 accounts (cap 70–80); 1–2 weekly clinics; 3 one-on-one calls in month 1 (including onboarding), then as needed; weekday support with 24-hour response; detailed notes per client; daily CS meeting; get clients to post wins.
- **KPIs (their targets, course claim): upsell/upgrade 30%, retain 30% of accounts, refunds and cancellations under 3%.**
- Pay: a 5k monthly floor until commissions pass it; commission per account and per upgrade, paid on cash collected (their numbers; don't reuse).

### Recruiting, Hiring & Managing 8-Figure CSM Teams
- Client-success managers (CSMs) are "the third sales team": setters create opportunities, closers convert them, CSMs retain and ascend clients. B2B offers should always have them.
- Benefits: fewer refunds and chargebacks, better results, more upsells, a different product.
- **Capacity: B2B 40–60 active clients per CSM** (front end plus backend); B2C 80–120 (course claim). Hire before you hit that ceiling.
- **Map the client journey:** purchase → admin onboarding → onboarding call → activation points 1–3 → retain or upsell. An activation point is a moment of new clarity or a quick win. Clients who pass them get results, stay, refer and upgrade. CSMs project daily how many clients they will move past each activation point.
- If clients arrive at different starting points ("non-homogeneous"), build **3–5 journey buckets**, each with its own activation points.
- Lead, don't take orders: diagnose the client's real constraint (most clients misdiagnose it), set their path and hold them to it.
- Pay (course claim): coaching only 5–8k OTE; with upsells, 5k base + 5–8% of backend sales; as a setter for the upsell, 1.5–3%. The founder closes backend deals at first.
- **Daily 60-minute CS meeting:** wins, announcements, late payments, projections (activation points and upsells), training, client problems, turning onboarding forms into plans, upsell call review.
- **Every Monday each client's record gets "Interactions/updates/challenges 1–3" and "Next steps".** The lead reviews these to catch problems early (for example, a client planning to hire without enough lead flow).
- Pipeline stages: needs onboarding material → needs onboarding call → onboarding booked → active account → paused → graduated → refunded. Or use stages by activation point, with yellow and red flags.
- **Front end → backend math (course claim):** a backend at 2× price and 2× time, taken by 50% of clients, doubles revenue. 20–30% take-up adds 40–60%.

### 8-Figure Operations
- "Scale, break systems, then build the new system." Designing perfect systems first is "glorified procrastination."
- Recommended hire order: assistant/CSM → assistant/CSM → bookkeeper → fulfillment → **two salespeople, only once the Optimal Selling System exists** → ops → (2 setters once there are 2–4 closers) → marketing lead or an agency you know enough to check → more sales → more setters.
- Phase 3 (team building) means more of the same, not new things: fix problems with people, not marketing. In Phase 4, when margins dip, add a backend, raise prices, add setters and refresh creatives.
- Hold as few meetings as possible. At about 8 people, one daily all-hands. At 10–20 people, two meetings (sales; ops/fulfillment/finance/marketing). Plus a weekly 30-minute 1-on-1, a monthly 60-minute sync (results, wins, "where we sucked", projections, focus, acknowledgements) and a weekly meeting for each big initiative.
- **P&L layout:** Cash In − Refunds = Gross Cash. Expenses = Labor + Marketing + Overhead. Gross Cash − Expenses = Net Operating Income (NOI). Then other items (write-offs, rent, tax) → Net Net.
- Finance runs daily reconciliation, receivables tracking, refunds and chargebacks, payroll and commissions. The founder keeps marketing strategy and writes first drafts of key copy.
