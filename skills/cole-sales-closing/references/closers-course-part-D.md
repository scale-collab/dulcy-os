## 33 Indirect VSL - Messaging (DOCX) ~8k chars
- Requires market research + copy platform first. Indirect VSL best for B2C; harder for B2-solopreneur (coaches, agencies) - needs a VERY compelling idea. Not a fit for Dulcy B2B services cold outbound directly.
- [Sienna][Pixel][Scribe] Timeline structure: pattern interrupt 0-5s; big promise 5-20s (benefit of consuming the message, not the product); big proof 20-45s; expand promise 45-75s (how you got it WITHOUT what they hate, what it is NOT; "big 4": new/only, safe/easy, predictable/anyone, big/fast); reveal new opportunity ~90s (named mechanism, intriguing not "BS"); transition; headline #2; story; content; pitch; offer/CTA; FAQ.
- [Scribe][Sienna] Belief chain: map 1-3 (4) epiphanies the buyer must believe: pain belief -> narrowing belief (eliminate other roads) -> new opportunity belief -> product belief. Tell the story so the prospect has the same epiphanies.
- [Scribe] Each step: Problem - Reason why - Consequence - Feature - Benefit - Benefit of benefit; us vs them, old vs new; end steps with proof.
- [Scribe][Echo] Offer/CTA: pillars, explicit disqualifiers ("not for..."), "for three types of clients", make the call sound beneficial, worst case / best case framing, case studies.
- Proof should be exclusive to proving the specific belief (case study not always best).
## 34 Indirect VSL - Tech (DOCX) ~4.5k chars ("VSL Math" content inside)
- [Riley] Funnel pieces: opt-in page -> video page -> application -> call booking -> thank-you page (confirm appt with significant other, zoom link, no-tolerance no-show policy, bonus/pre-call video, how to prep) -> emails; application grading; outbound dialing.
- [Atlas][Pixel] KPI philosophy: judge by cash ROAS, upfront cash ROAS, contract ROAS, CPA, cost per booked call - NOT CPM/CPC/CPL/LP conversion (too subjective, algorithm-dependent).
- Course claims: ads ~20% of P&L (5x cash ROAS) at scale; aim 6-8x cash ROAS under 300k/mo; upfront cash ROAS 3-4x start, floor ~2x; contract ROAS 6-10x; CPA target 33-50% of upfront cash and <=16.7% of contract.
- [Atlas][Echo] Cost per booked call math: target CPA / (1/close rate) = cost per LIVE call; then adjust for no-show/DQ -> cost per BOOKED call. Example: 1500 CPA, 20% close -> 300 per live call; 50% no-show/DQ -> 150 per booked call. Keep "booked" vs "live" distinct.
- Course claims: VSL conversion 10-15% needed (theirs >20%); lead-to-close ~3%; booking-to-close ~10% (DQ ~70% of apps); scalable at 1.5% lead-to-close and 5% booking-to-close.
- Optimizing for calls can mean high CPL but great CPA (claim: $120 leads, $1k CPA on a 24k product).
## 37 KPIs For Group Funnel (DOCX) ~2k chars

## 47 Paid FB Group Funnel Training Doc (DOCX ~25k chars; master doc that also contains the text of sibling lessons Group Set Up, Filling, Content Strategy, Easy Button, Email, Writing Great Content, Hiring Content Out, KPIs For Group Funnel)
- Funnel: opt-in (PDF lead magnet) -> thank-you -> bridge page to group. Key: text every opt-in number ASAP; setters lead-gen all group joins; 2-steps + live trainings with soft CTA; 1-2 webinars/mo; double-dial new numbers.
- [Atlas] Who: below ~50k/mo, no VSL experience, B2-solopreneur/personal dev markets. Pros: faster to market than VSL (VSL takes 30-60+ days to dial in). Cons: hard to track direct ROI, does not scale well. Move to VSL above 50-100k/mo.
- [Atlas][Sienna] If no calls: "#1 priority is taking the actions that generate sales calls" - founder does cold calls, messenger, content himself until there's a team. OPT (other people's traffic) only if strapped for cash; migrate an existing audience via a live training.
- [Sienna] Pipeline equity: goodwill in your audience. Adding people + value raise it; bad content + offers lower it. Offer volume drives appointments. Direct offer (pulls most, burns most), indirect (value + CTA), non-CTA (builds equity). Rule: if closers' calendars are full -> more value; if not full -> more direct offers.
- [Sienna] Baseline weekly calendar: Mon engagement + personal post; Tue value post/live + indirect CTA or case study; Wed 2-step (bi-weekly) + direct offer or mission post; Thu promo for Friday; Fri live training with CTA / client interview / guest training. Repurpose the same content to email; personal profile gets 1-2 of the group posts.
- [Sienna] "Easy button" when time-strapped: 1 livestream/wk (soft CTA), 1 promo, 1 recap; once a month swap livestream for a hard-CTA webinar. Main ROI comes from setters working group joins, texting opt-ins, replying to 2-step and live CTAs.
- [Scribe][Sienna] If not a great copywriter: mostly pure value + one direct offer/week + 2-3 indirect (case studies). Hook & promise; 4 U's (unique, useful, urgent, ultra-specific; use 2-3); Why3 (why important to me, why you, why now); open loops; big four (big/fast, new/only, easy/safe, safe/predictable). Framework: attention -> expand -> credibility -> promise -> open loop -> story/steps -> summarize -> CTA; or What/Why/How/Now.

Phase 1 = you draft, copywriter edits (cuts time ~50%, learns voice); Phase 2 = full ownership with clear quotas (e.g. 3 posts/emails per offer per week + promos).

## 48 Perry Belcher | Email Marketing Optimization (DOCX, slide-deck text)
- Scope: newsletter/list email (warm list), not cold outbound - apply carefully. Contains a personal phone number on every slide - excluded.
- [Riley][Atlas] Check reputation (Google Postmaster), blacklists, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, BIMI. Course benchmarks: 95%+ deliverability, 0.01-0.05% complaints, sub-2% bounce, 25%+ opens, 7% clicks.
- [Riley] Domain rules (course claim): 6+ months old, not expiring within 6 months, WHOIS matches, no private registration, .com/.net/.org only; send from purpose sub-domains (newsletter., clients., followup., prospect.); warm-up inboxes.
- [Atlas][Scout] Clean and prune to recent engagers ("prune to 30-day openers - rip off the bandaid"); behavioral segments (1/10/30-day openers, re-engagement); segments under 60k; trickle sending; send best engagers first (7am/9am/11am pecking order).
- [Scribe] Text-only unless HTML needed; lean code; compressed images only if they add curiosity/end result. Promo-ratio: mix content and legal/admin text with the pitch (rough 15/60/25 split shown); use preview text deliberately.
- [Scribe] "6 second story" template: I was here, doing this, when this happened, and I realized, so I did, and here's what happened.
- [Scribe][Echo] Prompt positive behaviors (reply, add to address book, mark not spam, forward); avoid negatives (spam reports, unsubscribe, open-and-delete).
- [Atlas] Test every email (inbox tester); "email stacking" A/B: 10% of list x 3 subjects, then 10% x 3 bodies with winning subject, then winner to full list.
- Course claims: 585% email revenue increase after pruning 80% of list in 66 days; inbox placement ~2.6% priority / 13.4% promotions / 85% spam (unverified).

## 53 Slide Deck High-Ticket Email (PPTX text)

Testimonial emails story-based, do not reveal it is a client up front; video screenshot with play button -> testimonial landing page with booking CTA.

## 54 TOF Acquisition Overview (DOCX)
- Five top-of-funnel models: VSL, group funnel, IG shoutouts, LinkedIn outreach, cold email/E2E. Course claims of best results: group 500k/mo, VSL ~4M/mo, IG 400-800k/mo, LinkedIn ~100k/mo, cold email 306k best month.
- [Atlas] Do NOT run multiple funnels "for diversification"; diversify by traffic channel for the one funnel that works. Focus wins. If one funnel works and >100k/mo, double down on it.
- [Atlas] Below 100k/mo or lead-flow constrained: default group funnel, EXCEPT DFY services, low TAM, mid-market/enterprise -> VSL path; if capital constrained: build VSL, small budget, call every lead yourself, and run LinkedIn + cold email to validate messaging and build cash flow. (Dulcy and its B2B service clients fall in the exception bucket.)
- [Sienna] Big existing audience (course rough thresholds: 50-100k active social, 50-100k active email segment, 30k+ group): first see how many calls daily content with mixed CTAs generates before building new funnels or ads.
- [Atlas] VSL is "generally the best model" per course but needs strong copy, messaging validated on cold traffic, testing budget, 1-2 months to dial in; group/IG/outbound are more forgiving and let you find messaging as you go. Low-ticket ascension is harder and needs a great setting team.
## 56 VSL Funnel - Overview Doc ("New Paid VSL Doc (Full)" DOCX ~35k chars; master doc incl. VSL Math, direct VSL script, ads, FB setup, deck VSL, lead forms)
- [Atlas] Who must use VSL instead of group funnel: DFY services, corporate/mid-market, small TAM (<=15k). B2-solopreneur sellers should still try group first. Big existing following: just make CTAs straight to a call ("DM me" or calendar link).
- [Atlas][Pixel] Always start with a DIRECT VSL; indirect only to try to beat it later ("99.99% of people" don't need indirect).
- [Riley][Pixel] Attribution is mandatory: server-side conversions (Stape) and/or Hyros for which campaigns book calls; author says switching off the native pixel was a big unlock (course claim). Mistake list: no attribution, no opt-in page, no emails, no app grading, deviating from baseline.
- [Pixel] Fast validation (if you can lose 20-30k): 3-5 CBO campaigns at ~$500/day for 4-5 days, same VSL, different creative CONCEPTS (pain point, face, offer, first 30s, market); cut those out of KPI; keep calendar ~48h full after app grading. Slow validation: min $100/day split 3-4 campaigns; need 3-5x target KPI spend before judging; ~3 iterations usually finds a winner (course claim).
- [Pixel] Budget per campaign = target lead cost x 30 / 7 (pixel wants 30-50 fires/week; 20 or 15 if constrained). Judge after >=4 days and 3-5x target cost per call. Over-budget then cut beats stair-stepping 20%/day.
- [Atlas][Echo] Low-budget validation #1: small ad spend + founder texts and outbound-calls every lead personally. "The two most profitable things... generating sales calls, doing sales calls" until you hire setters/closers. An unvalidated offer is hard to hand to a setter.
- [Atlas][Scribe] Validation #2: put the direct VSL inside cold email + LinkedIn sequences to validate before ad spend; course claim it tripled their outbound response. Cold outbound setters need ~60/40 base/commission (SDR) vs near-commission MDR.
- [Echo][Riley] Opt-in page matters: course claim ~50% of bookings from ads and ~50% from setters + email; ~20% of bookings via email (30-40% seen). Do at least the autoresponder; 5-7 broadcasts/week ideal.
- [Echo] App grading: cancel 60-80% sometimes; typical split 33% cancel / 33% to setter to vet / 33% straight to closer calendar.
- [Scribe] Direct VSL script 1: state offer (ideal client + result + timeframe + guarantee) -> 3 key results as linear A->B milestones the buyer wants -> social proof (name drop or 5-7 snappy before/afters "X went from A to B in T despite objection") -> soft CTA -> mechanism (problem/mistake -> consequence -> feature -> benefit -> benefit of benefit; speed, predictability, low risk, anyone can, new/unique) -> hard CTA + more proof. Script 2: offer -> results+mechanism -> proof -> CTA.
- [Pixel][Scribe] Ad script A: offer, 3 results, restate guarantee, proof, CTA; Script B: offer, proof, CTA (when proof is very strong). Written ad = tweak of video script; headline = WHO: OFFER. "The ad is more important than the VSL" (same VSL, new ads made it work).
- [Pixel] Scaling: winner -> 5 new campaigns (2 exact dupes, 1 winner to new audience, 2 slight creative variations); horizontal (new audiences/placements/platforms/ages/devices/countries), vertical (+10-20% when several in KPI), spherical (new concept for a different segment/pain point/market). Review windows 4/7/14/30 days on cost per call.
- [Pixel] Deck VSL (same script in embedded slides) and lead-form variant (cheaper CPL, worse cost per booked call, easier tech; good if you call every lead yourself).
