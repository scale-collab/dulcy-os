## 2. Methods by topic

### 2.1 Sales philosophy: what the prospect must believe
*Sources: 2.1 Sales First Principles; 2.2 The Belief Ladder; Week 2 Overview.*

- People buy an end state, on a surface level and an emotional level. They do not buy the offer, the brand or the testimonials. They buy when your offer is the easiest path from where they are now to where they want to be.
- Build **internal pressure** (they want to act for their own reasons). Do not lean on external pressure (your reasons, such as fast-action discounts). To build it, make the current situation ("hell island") and the desired situation ("heaven island") very clear, with numbers and concrete detail. A bigger, clearer gap means more tension, and the prospect resolves that tension by buying.
- A closer has only two jobs: (1) find out whether and how you can help, and (2) remove objections *before* the close. In the course's picture: show both islands clearly, knock down the other "bridges", deal with the risks ("harpoon the sharks"), and show that your bridge is the safest and fastest.
- Of the six human needs, the course names **certainty and significance** as the strongest.
- **The Belief Ladder: 7 beliefs needed to buy.** Every objection traces back to a missing belief. Build all seven in discovery, by asking questions, before you pitch.
  1. **Pain**: they have a problem (a gap) that keeps them from what they want. No problem, no sale.
  2. **Doubt**: they can't fix it alone, or doing it alone would waste time and money. This removes the DIY objection. Don't try to create doubt that is already there.
  3. **Cost**: doing nothing hurts much more than the time and money it takes to fix. This removes timing objections and creates urgency.
  4. **Desire**: fixing it gives a better future. Get what they want, why they want it, a number for it, and what else in their life it changes.
  5. **Money**: they have the resources and are willing to use them. No resources is a *condition*, not an objection. Low willingness means not enough Cost. Usually leave this belief until last.
  6. **Support**: the people around them (spouse, partner, co-founder) will back them. This prevents partner objections.
  7. **Trust**: they see your method as different from, and better than, what has failed them before. There are two sales here: the sale of the *method* (the pitch) and the sale of the *product*.
- Use **Socratic dialogue**: ask questions so the prospect reaches the conclusion themselves. Consistency bias does the rest. Once they have said the 7 beliefs out loud, not buying feels inconsistent to them. The goal is an "objection-less close", or a close where the only thing left is logistics.
- There are two discovery orders: **problems-first** and **goals-first** syntax (see 2.3). Any order works if all 7 beliefs get covered.

### 2.2 Call frame, rapport and tonality
*Sources: Closer Crash Course; Setter Crash Course; Advanced Tonality & Subcommunication; Call Syntax Bizzop (Setter Masterclass).*

- **Open:** keep rapport light and short. Check they are ready ("got something to take notes with?"). If they seem guarded, ask whether now is still a good time.
- **Frame the call** before discovery. Explain that you will first understand their business and their challenges. If you can help, you'll walk them through it. If you can't, you'll refer them or give them homework. Get a "cool?" Then open with the biggest challenge: what is not working at the level it should?
- **Three filters decide whether an idea is accepted:** the speaker's certainty, the speaker's inner alignment (do they live it?), and social proof.
  - Certainty: aim for **resolve** (calm, relaxed, as if the deal is already done), not hyped "pumped-up certainty". Speak with depth: become an expert in the 1–3 problems your market always has. That moves you from a sales frame to an expert frame.
  - Alignment: be the buyer you want. Your own objections become your prospects' objections.
  - Social proof: use "status drops" (case studies, notable clients) only to *prove a point* you are making, and never in a showy way.
- **Signs of dominance (certainty) in the voice:** a full range of expression, care-free rather than held-back delivery, and changes in pace with pauses.
- **Three rapport tonalities:** an upward inflection *seeks* rapport (sounds unsure, so avoid it), an even inflection is neutral, and a downward inflection *breaks* rapport (sounds certain). In call reviews, coach reps out of the upward inflection.
- **Setter soft intro (Bizzop):** neutral, friendly language. Mention what they responded to. Opening question: what caught their attention and made them reach out?
- **Alignment triad (Success Tips):** certainty comes from alignment with the offer, the market and your process. Change only 1–2 things in your process at a time. Changing everything at once makes you unsure, and the prospect hears it.

### 2.3 Discovery and qualification
*Sources: Closer Crash Course (15-step discovery); Setter Processes for the VSL Funnel (problems-first and goals-first triage); Setter Crash Course; Call Syntax Bizzop.*

**Closer discovery flow (goals-first):** 1 isolate the challenge → 2 probe → 3 desired situation → 4 ultimate goal → 5 monetary goal → 6 why that number → 7 financial qualifier (if relevant) → 8 long-term vision → 9 non-monetary goals → 10 current situation → 11 reason for change ("why now?") → 12 doubt questions → 13 solution questions → 14 cost / why-now questions → 15 support questions.

Key questions, paraphrased:
- Challenge: what's the biggest challenge right now? Probe: tell me more; when you say X, what exactly do you mean?
- Desired: begin with the end in mind; what's the goal; what's the money goal; why that number; what's the long-term vision; what personal (non-money) goals should the business give you?
- Current and trigger: what's the worst part of how it is now? What happened on the day you said "enough"? After all this time, what made it a priority *now*?
- Doubt: what has been the biggest thing stopping you from doing this yourself?
- Solution: have you had help with this before? Are you working with anyone now? Are you looking at other providers?
- Support: who else is involved? Do they support this? Do they know you're on this call?
- (Bizzop extras) How long has this been going on? How long have you seriously been considering a change? What are you looking for in the right solution? (Good moment for a financial qualifier.) Who does your income support? What's your bigger "why"?
- **Pre-transition tie-downs:** "So if you keep doing X, you won't get there?" "And that's why it's a priority now?"

**Problems-first triage (B2B, setter or short call):** frame → biggest challenge → background (offer, price point, ideal client, how a stranger becomes a client) → probing (why is that the biggest challenge? how has it affected the business? how else?) → **chunk down to numbers** (leads last month, the month before, how many were ideal, how many closed, at what price, the price you *want*, total revenue, gross vs. net, delivery hours) → need-payoff (if this got fixed, what's the goal?).

**Don't move on until you have four things:** (1) the full context of the problem, (2) why the problem matters (impact), (3) specific numbers on the problem, and (4) their goals. For a triage call this takes 5–10 minutes.

**Qualify *after* booking (setter):** present the questions as "help me fill out a short form for the closer". Ask: confirm the problem and goal; how decisions get made in the company (who decides); finances on a 1–10 scale ("what does X mean to you?"); and whether they have the time to implement (hours per week).

**Note-taking:** record the prospect's exact words about their logical pain and emotional pain. The pitch reuses them (Pitch formula 2). The course says a "note taking system" lesson exists in Week 3, but it is not in the library.

### 2.4 Transition and pitch (Pitch Codex)
*Sources: 4.1 Pitch Codex Intro; 4.2 How To Pitch; 2.4 Pitch Creation Worksheet; Example Pitch Codex Script; Biz Opp Offer Breakdown.*

- **Transition:** ask whether there's anything else you should know. Then reflect their words back: "based on what you told me… you know how you said X and it's making you feel Y?" State in one line who you help and the result. Then ask where they want to go from here, and offer to walk them through the process A–Z.
- Offer and pitch carry most of the sale. Course claim: "75% of the success/failure of the sale comes down to the offer". Buyers are spoiled for choice, so the best *offer* wins, not the best talker.
- The pitch has three parts:
  1. **High-level promise (hard offer):** one or two sentences of result plus timeframe. Formula: "This is [N] steps to get you from [current] to [desired] in [time]." A promise of outcome beats a list of activities. Only promise what is realistic for *this* prospect ("don't lie").
  2. **Bridge / pillars:** 3–5 logical steps from A to B. Together they explain why past attempts failed and why this is different. The process itself is the unique mechanism. Fancy names are optional, and forced ones backfire. Give 1–2 pillars a proprietary name (usually the early ones that solve the core issue), and keep the rest common-sense.
     - Pillar 1: a quick win plus the foundation (what makes everything else work), and why past attempts failed.
     - Pillars 2–3: follow on logically and solve the core issue.
     - The last pillar removes "fear of success" (the next problem they will hit once the first is solved).
  3. **Delivery:** short, high level and full of benefits. Say how it is delivered and get ahead of objections (speed of support, what happens after). Avoid anything that sounds like "more of the same".
- **Delivery mechanics:** ask them to take pen and paper and write 1–4. After each pillar: "Make sense? Any questions, or ready to move to #2?" (tie down and open up).
- **Three ways to explain a pillar:**
  - F1: feature → logical benefit → emotional benefit → (optional) consequence of not having it.
  - F2: their own words: "you told me [logical pain] and it makes you feel [emotional pain]; what we do is [deliverable] so you can [logical benefit], which lets you [emotional benefit]."
  - F3 (the course's favourite): "most of the market does [mistake]; that's a problem because…; which means…; ultimately…; so instead we do [feature] so you can [benefit]…". This sets up an us-vs-them, new-vs-old frame and anchors the cost of inaction.
- **Pitfalls:** sounding scripted (be an actor: drill it until it's natural), overcomplicating ("confused prospects don't buy"), and caring more about tactics and names than clarity.
- **Building a pitch (worksheet steps):** current vs. desired → list every step → pick the 20% that drives 80% (quick wins, where clients get stuck) → narrow to 3–4 pillars → name 1–2 → write the promise → write the pillar explanations → pick the 2–3 most compelling deliverables → make them benefit-rich, with the cost of not having them → put it in order and practise out loud dozens of times.
- **Pre-pitch method (Biz Opp):** skill development, vehicle, context, why it matters, benefit linking, cost linking, then what gets done. Tie down after the "what", "how" and "why", and knock down the other options ("killed all other bridges").

### 2.5 Committing phase and closing
*Sources: Closer Crash Course; Pitch Codex Intro; Example Pitch Codex Script; 4.6 Closing Patterns & Risk Mitigators (outside library); 4.3 Financial Objections.*

1. **Temperature check:** "On the process specifically, how do you feel?" A certain answer in a certain tone means move on. An uncertain answer or tone means go to the alignment bit.
2. **Alignment bit + 1–10 scale:** explain that alignment matters because you'll be working closely together. Then ask for a 1–10 rating. **9–10** with certain language: move on. **8 or below**: "What keeps you from an 8, 9 or 10?" Handle what comes up, then re-check ("are you sure?").
3. **Next steps / onboarding before the price:** describe what happens right after they say yes (homework, baseline review, first quick wins). This raises certainty before they hear the number.
4. **Investment:** one simple sentence that ties the price to the outcome. Then **stop talking**.
5. **Closing language once they're in but haven't said yes** (from 4.6):
   - "Sharp angle": "If we show you exactly how to get [outcome], is it worth it to you?" → "Are you sure?" → "Ready to get started on some homework?" → ask for billing details.
   - "Feel good": "How do you feel? Feel like the right fit?" → "Sure?" → homework → billing.
   - "Keep it in context": chunk the price up against the outcome, or down to a daily cost.
   - If they say "let's do it", go straight to logistics. If they stall, go back to objection handling.
6. **Risk mitigators** (backups only; never use them for uncertainty objections, only for money or timing logistics, and **Alex must approve them before Cole offers any for Dulcy or a client**): payment plans; "I buy your second month" (a 60-day gap before payment 2); "I buy you a month of time" (extra weeks added for a timing problem); 24-hour refund; 7-day refund; conditional ROI guarantee; conditional results-or-refund.

### 2.6 Objection handling
*Sources: 4.2 Pacing First Objection; 4.3 Financial Objections; 7FSA Uncertainty Based Objections; Reframing Structures & Patterns (all outside library); Closer Crash Course.*

**Rule 0:** objection handling does not fix a weak discovery or pitch. Most strong closes have no objections at all, or only logistics.

**A. Pace the first objection (always do this first).** The goal is to lock in certainty about the *process* before you touch money or partner, find the *real* objection, and keep payment plans back.
1. "No problem. Put money aside for a second: how do you feel about the process? Is it what you need to get to [outcome]?" Listen to the tone as well as the words.
2. If they agree:
   - Haven't raised money or partner yet → **How vs. Should**: "You're not in a *should I* place, you're in a *how do I make this work* place, right?"
   - Already raised money or partner → **Isolation**: "So [objection] aside, nothing else keeps you from being 100% certain?"
3. If they're uncertain or vague, use one of three options:
   - (a) If they gave a high number earlier, call out the gap: "You told me you were a 9 because…, now you sound unsure. What's really going on?"
   - (b) Alignment speech, then a 1–10 on the process only. 10 → "So it's just the money?" 8–9 → "What does 8 mean? What else is in the way?" 7 or below → "What keeps you from 8–10?" If the answer is still vague, ask: "Is it just nerves? Just the risk?"
   - (c) **Three choices**: "Usually it's one of three: you're not sure the process works for you, you don't trust me or the company, or you know it works and it's just making it work financially. Which is it?"

**B. Money / financial objections.**
- Tie down twice (finances aside / how vs. should). Then: "Most clients pay upfront. For some we break it up. We have no set plans; it's customised. Want to explore that?"
- Ask permission for an open conversation about their finances, then: cash on hand? Access to credit? How many cards, what's available on each, how much is used?
- Outcomes:
  - (1) They really can't → a condition. Make a plan for later.
  - (2) They can pay in full → "Can I tell you what I'd do? I'd do it upfront." Give two reasons: full commitment gets results faster, and it makes them the kind of client they want to attract.
  - (3) They need a plan → frame it as upside vs. downside, offer a split ("let you in for half now, the rest in 30 days"), then "if I do that for you, are you willing to move forward now?"
  - (4) They won't share their finances → call it out ("you told me you're certain, but you won't be open so we can figure it out together"). That usually brings out the resources or the real objection. If they won't share cash and credit, it is usually a **trust** issue.
- Don't hand out payment plans freely. That is your re-offer ammunition.
- "It's a lot of money" → "Compared to what?" Set the price against the outcome over a year or a career. For non-ROI offers, tie it back to the fear and desire they told you about, then break the price down to a daily cost.

**C. Uncertainty objections (after pacing):**

| Objection | Course move (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| "Think about it" | Pace the first objection. If they keep repeating it: "Is that how you always make decisions?" → the decision-making reframe |
| "Not how I make decisions / sleep on it" | Acknowledge their pattern. Point out it's what got them *here*, not *there*. Ask what kind of decision-maker they want to be going forward |
| "Need more info / send a proposal" | Ask why. Recap what the call already covered (their pain, their goal, the path). No amount of information removes risk. Contrast doing more research with acting |
| "Due diligence / I just found you" (trust) | Isolate ("if everything I said is true, you're in?"). Then reassure on track record and accountability, using **only verified Dulcy/client facts** |
| "What if it doesn't work?" | "Will you do the work and ask for help when stuck? Then why wouldn't it work?" If "I don't know", go to nerves |
| "No time" | Pace and isolate. Optimise the hours they already work. Or: the problem they came to solve is the very thing stopping them ("self-perpetuating loop"). Decide from vision, not current circumstances |
| "Tried something similar" | Ask what happened. Empathise (a personal story if true). Remind them of their goal and their own admission that they can't do it alone. Ask what lesson they'll take from it |
| "Speak to a client first" | Ask why. Explain why that isn't practical (protecting clients' time), then "how would you like to proceed?" Option: a refund window (needs Alex's approval) |
| "Credit is risky" | Clarify what they mean. "If you knew it worked, would it be worth it?" So the real issue is certainty, not credit. Risk of doing nothing vs. doing something |
| "Is there a guarantee?" | Ask why. Few serious things are guaranteed. What you can promise is support and a track record. They control doing the work (Dulcy: state only real terms) |
| "Too risky" | Look at the real risk: doing nothing vs. doing something. Successful people maximise upside and limit downside |
| "Nervous / scared" | "So it's just nerves? Good." Nerves mean it matters. Don't take advice from your fears |
| Spouse / partner | **Not covered by any dedicated script found.** The course only prevents it through Support questions in discovery and isolates it in pacing step 2 |

**D. Frameworks and reframes (Reframing Structures):**
- **CIA-PATT**: Clarify and understand → Isolate (1–2x) → Appreciate and reassure → ask Permission → Accountability (repeat what they said earlier) → Transform the meaning (reframe) → Time to decide (contrast two choices).
- **Truth hammer**: ask permission, then say honestly what you see.
- **Nice-guy negotiation**: clarify → appreciate → state your position → "how would you like to proceed?"
- **Reframes**: "call out" (you said X, now Y, what's going on?); "it's a simple decision" (stop the pain and get the outcome, or not); "the real decision" (do your actions match your words?); "confusing the problem with the solution"; "that's exactly why"; a client story (only real ones); "how would you feel if… a year from now?"; the cost reframe (you told me what happens if nothing changes, so what will you do?); chunking down to the ridiculous (cost per day).

### 2.7 Follow-up after the call and pipeline
*Sources: Setter Systems & Processes Overview; Closer Crash Course; End of Day Process; Sales Mgmt EOD; 8.6 Data-Driven Decisions; Selling Back End Offers.*

- Course claim: about half of Cole Gordon's personal cash (~$200k of ~$400k/month) came from **long-term follow-up on deals 4–6+ months old**, not next-day follow-ups.
- The pipeline works as an **ecosystem**: (1) a steady stream of content (live trainings, PDFs, masterclasses, client interviews), which gives reps a *valuable reason* to reach out; (2) outbound setters, who naturally run into past consults; (3) **leadership-based follow-up**: lead with value, then pivot to "how are you coming along on [their challenge]?" or the golden question, "what's changed since then?"
- "Re-offers": bring non-buyers back to a call using outreach, content and a fresh offer.
- Motivation for follow-up should be *internal*. Set projections that can't be hit without follow-up and self-generated calls, but that are still hittable about 60–70% of the time when the week is run perfectly.
- **EOD hygiene** keeps deals from slipping: finish the day's hot-list follow-ups, add today's consults to the hot or general follow-up list, update deal notes and the CRM, create follow-up tasks, and hand new clients to client success properly.
- Warning sign: high deposits or commitments but low closes → weak objection handling or closing language, a shallow process, or the rep using a deposit as a cop-out (Data-Driven Decisions). Lots of 2-call closes and delays also pull offer % down.
- **Backend / upsell call** (client already won): book it 2–3 weeks before their term ends as a normal "coaching call". Agenda: progress → 2–3 biggest wins → where they're most stuck (1–3 core problems) → need-payoff → the next problem you can see coming → goals → lay out next steps per problem → pitch. Rule of thumb: 2x the price for 2x the time, keeping the monthly rate the same.

### 2.8 No-shows, show rate and setting
*Sources: Show Rate & Sales Ops Training (videos 1–5); Confirmation Emails; 10 Day Setter Text Sequence; Setter Processes (VSL); Troubleshooting w/ Setters; Data-Driven Decisions.*

- **People show up when they know HOW** (date, time, link, calendar entry) **and WHY** (why this call is the best use of that hour). Most companies only fix the "how".
- "Show rate problems" are often really sales-ops problems. Audit monthly: compare your best month with this month on bookings (setter sets vs. inbound), show rate by source, cancellations and reschedules (company-caused vs. prospect-caused), confirmation rate, and show rate for confirmed vs. unconfirmed. In the course's example, the fix was getting set volume back up and cutting cancellations, not "show rate".
- Gold standards:
  - Booking window of a **rolling 2 days (3 max)**.
  - Lots of open slots (6–8 per closer per day).
  - Basic automated reminders.
  - Setter show rate of 70%+.
  - Right messaging at the CTA and on the confirmation page.
  - A human **Lead Nurture Specialist (LNS)** who confirms every call.
  - Predictive application grading.
  - Double-booking based on data.
  - Monthly ops audit.
- **Framing the call when booking:** the prospect should believe the call is valuable even if they don't buy, that booking is the first step to the result, and that there will be no hard pressure.
- **Confirmation page:** headline "Your call is *tentatively* scheduled". A 90–120s "how" video (logistics, add to calendar, expect a message from the confirmer) plus a "why" video (value of the call, agenda; a VSL that looks like a plain training), with written steps on the page.
- **LNS cadence:** immediately at booking; once a day if the call is more than 2 days out; 24h before (only if booked more than 36h ahead); 8h; 1h; 15 min (with the link). Immediate message (B2B): personal ("saw you do [specific thing]"), plus "reply so I know I have the right number". Ask engaging questions ("what do you want to cover?"). Answer logistics questions fast. Send sales questions back to the call. If they engage, offer to move the call earlier.
- **LNS KPIs:** confirmations sent, confirmations received, confirmation rate, show rate when confirmed, show rate when unconfirmed. To raise show rate, raise the *confirmation rate*. Below 50% confirmation: add "bump" messages, then test copy and timing. Use plain SMS, not iMessage, and not GHL/Aloware numbers.
- **Calendar rules:** double-book any segment (app grade or confirm status) with a show rate under 65%; single-book at 65% or above. **Never cancel unconfirmed calls.** Leave the booking and let someone else book over it.
- **Application grades:** 1 = DQ, 2 = setter triage, 3 = closer (fit but not ideal), 4 = closer (perfect ICP). Prefer fill-in questions to multiple choice (course claim: multiple-choice answers are false 47% of the time compared with financial data). Ask one question at a time, written in terms of pain or pleasure. Work out the grades from a correlation of 200–500 past bookings against show and close.
- **Confirmation emails:** at booking, 24h before and 1h before. Each gives 3 steps (confirm time and link, add to calendar, watch the homework video) and a PS asking them to reply if they can't make it.
- **No-show and non-responder follow-up:** call no-shows (a "curated opportunity" for setters). Text sequence: 10 days, sent at staggered times of day, light and friendly, with the calendar link in some messages, ending with a polite "breakup" message. (Speed to lead: text within 5–10 min, 2–3 calls in the first 2 hours, always double-dial (call, then call straight back, then voicemail, text and email), and use a local-presence dialer.)
- **Setter / closer split:** don't put a setter call *between* a booked call and the closer. That exposes you to no-shows twice. Use setters on marketing leads (MDR model), on grade-2 applications, and on no-apps. SDR (cold) and MDR roles should never be combined in one person.
- Setter-caused low show or close: too much pressure, no value or insight, poor rapport, misleading the prospect, **not tying down the date and time**, no pre-call follow-up. Closer-side pre-call checks: automated SMS and email reminders, a trust-transfer video, homework, and the closer texting the day before and the day of.

### 2.9 Call review, scorecards and coaching
*Sources: The Big 3; Sales Management BOD / MOD / EOD; Day To Day Sales Team Management; 8.3 Diagnosing & Fixing Sales Ruts; Keys To High Performance; Data-Driven Decisions; 3.6 Scorecards; Closer Scorecard template; Enrollment Specialist Setter Scorecard; Sales Playbook Outline; Success Tips; Ramp Up; New Closer RAMP Checklist; 0/0/0 EOD; Mock Call SOP.*

- **The Big 3** (course claim: 95% of underperforming teams are missing one): (1) proper tracking (offers and offer %, show %, 2-call closes, checked daily by an admin, because reps manipulate data); (2) **daily meetings** Mon–Fri (wins, projections, call review or drilling Mon/Wed/Fri; daily reviews while starting out); (3) a **qualitative EOD report** in a Slack channel, with a manager comment or a 5-minute call.
- **Scorecard (from the book *Who*):** mission, then outcomes / KPIs (revenue, units, calls per day, open slots, self-generated calls, minimum live-call close %, huddles, EOD, projections and CRM), then compensation. The rep gets freedom on *how* but none on *what*. Use it again for performance plans.
- **Self-review habits (rep):** while ramping, review 5–10 recordings a day; once ramped, 1–2 a day. Transcribe top calls by hand, especially the pitch. Drill questions, pitch and objections out loud (flashcards for pacing and financial objections). Get calls reviewed as often as possible.
- **Manager cadence after ramp:** a weekly 1:1, check-ins every other day, 1–3 live call breakdowns a week (at least 1), and a light "temp check" listen daily or every other day. While ramping: 3 1:1s a week for the first 7–14 days.
- **Diagnosis map (Data-Driven Decisions):**
  - Low show → funnel messaging, traffic quality, pre-call follow-up.
  - Low offer % → lost certainty, qualifying too hard, only pitching sure things, too many delays, lead quality.
  - High deposits, low closes → objection handling, closing language, payment-plan positioning, a shallow process.
  - Setters → first check volume (dials, minutes), then skill.
- **Ruts:** check lead quality across all reps first (if everyone dipped, it's leads or the manager). Rut definitions conflict between lessons: >10% off baseline over 30–50 calls (8.3) vs. a 5-point swing sustained over 30 days (Keys to High Performance). Catch ruts early through qualitative signals: missed projections, flat EODs, low energy in meetings, admin slipping, calls without "the sound of belief". Fix order: check the 4 keys (energy, clarity, necessity, inspiration; the course claims this fixes about 90%) → personal life → reset to baseline (listen to old wins, reread old EODs) → shift focus from self to service → challenge limiting beliefs (e.g. "my prospects have no money" → check the last 10 prospects' cash and credit) → call review. Don't overcoach small dips. A performance plan lasts 30–45 days with clear agreed numbers.
- **"Going through the motions"** (around 9 months in): two symptoms are skipping discovery ("I know what they'll say") and letting prospects off the hook at the close.
- **Daily rep routine:**
  - BOD: know your numbers (units and revenue for the week and month vs. projections; pipeline follow-ups and deposits due).
  - MOD: do only two things, *doing* consults and *generating* consults, working down a ranked list of lead-gen activities.
  - EOD: admin checklist plus a reflective journal (consults: what went well and what to improve; ratings 1–10 for physiology, clarity, necessity and inspiration; "will I hit projections?").
- **Playbook (for onboarding a rep):** product and company thesis; the 5 best discovery examples (covering all 7 beliefs); the 5 best pitch examples; a temp-check breakdown; 2–3 recorded examples per objection type (uncertainty, money, support); how to process a deal; EOD format; tracking; meeting outline.
- **Validating a new funnel or offer:** one strong closer (usually the founder) takes all calls for the first 7 days, records them, smooths out the process, then teaches it to the team (Lead Quality vs Sales Process). If qualified live-call-to-close is under 20–25%, suspect the sales process before lead quality.
