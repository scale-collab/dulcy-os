# Performance evidence: which real Dulcy cold-email copy worked (Jan 2025 to Nov 2026)

Built 2026-11-25 for Scribe (cold email copywriter). Read-only. No EmailBison, Airtable or repo record was changed.
**Internal evidence only.** Nothing in this file is proof Dulcy or a client may quote in copy (RULES non-negotiable 3). Every number, name and claim inside a script belongs only to the client it ran for. **Never reuse one client's facts, proof or results for another client.**

**How to use this file.** Sections 1 to 6 are the analysis (about the first 2,100 lines). The appendix ("All scripts we ran") is the raw verbatim script library: search it by client heading (`### <Client>`) or EmailBison ID (`EB id 123`); don't read it top to bottom.

## Scope, sources and method
- **Metric:** interested per 1,000 emails sent (= Airtable "Interested" / "Emails Sent" x 1,000). The brief asked for "interested per email sent"; per 1,000 is the same ratio, easier to read (1.66 per 1,000 = 0.166%).
- **Ranking scope:** every Airtable Campaigns record (`appxqINt5XKw7a76A` / `tblAVAA6uBPaMj28P`, any status) with **500+ emails sent**: **367 campaigns across 33 client workspaces**, current and past. The appendix covers all **410 campaigns with 1+ sent**.
- **Clients:** the 6 current clients + Dulcy (including the "Dulcy Free Campaigns" workspace, where Dulcy ran trial campaigns for prospect agencies: Clear River, Great Impressions, PNDigital, Just Digital) and 26 past clients from the Workspace IDs table (`tblcjRBGJEWh5KgOt`, 37 records). McIntyre Digital and Marcello Incorporated have workspace records but no campaign with sends.
- **Copy sources, in order of trust:**
  1. **EmailBison sequences as synced into Airtable "Sequence Analytics"** (`tblmHKxDLELPkj5xk`): 859 rows, one per step and variant, for 188 EmailBison campaigns, with per-variant sent / replies / interested / bounces. This is what was actually sent, and it is the only per-variant (A/B) source.
  2. **Airtable Campaigns copy fields**: Campaign Subject/Body N Version N, Email N Subject/Body Spintax N, Campaign Email N Subject / Body N Version 1, and the (Review) fields. These can be the approved or review draft rather than the exact sent text. No per-step stats.
  3. **EmailBison API direct**, GET only: `/api/campaigns/v1.1/{id}/sequence-steps`.
- **EmailBison access limit (important):** the MCP key is signed into the **Visionair AI** workspace (a past client). Switching workspace needs either a workspace API key (RULES 2026-11-18: Claude never reads or shows those keys) or `POST /api/workspaces/v1.1/switch-workspace` (a non-GET call, forbidden by this brief). So **only Visionair AI campaigns were read directly in EmailBison**. Every other client's numbers come from Airtable's EmailBison sync. EmailBison's per-step stats endpoint (`/api/campaigns/{id}/stats`) is also a POST and was not used. The primary workspace was never switched, so no reset was needed.
- **Coverage gap:** 186 campaigns with sends (1,566,726 emails) have **no copy stored anywhere readable**: mostly Dulcy's 2025 campaigns (CPPC G63, APPC E53, MISS G550, Clutch/Clay/Agency Vista direct offers), Scroll Stop's April 2026 "Direct Offer" wave, and most 2025 past-client campaigns. Their copy is UNKNOWN. They are in the ranking and listed with their numbers at the end of each client's appendix block. Do not reconstruct them from memory.
- **Docs read:** `clients/historical/what-worked-2026-11-17.md`, `clients/historical/historical-client-profiles-2026-11-17.md`, `skills/scribe-cold-email-copywriter/references/campaign-patterns.md`, all 7 files in `clients/*.md` (copy, offer and proof sections), `sops/drive-export/119-Dulcy-Campaigns.md` (Human Marketing review copy; it matches EB 552/553, whose live versions are in the appendix).

## Caveats (read before trusting a number)
- **"Interested" is a label, not a measured outcome.** It is set by different people and tools per workspace (Josh, clients such as MJJ, the Reply Agent from 2026-10-09). Compare rates across clients with care. Meetings are under-recorded (see what-worked file), so they are not the ranking metric.
- **Airtable copy fields may not equal the sent copy.** Where only Airtable fields exist, the ranking says "Airtable", not "EB seq".
- **Sequence Analytics totals are a sync snapshot** (the synced date is printed per row in the appendix), so per-variant sums can differ slightly from campaign totals.
- **Workspace = client.** The "Dulcy" workspace also holds "[TargetonStar] SEO & Web Design Agencies (Direct) - Version 2 copy" (5,399 sent), counted under Dulcy here; it is TargetonStar's copy.
- **Confounding.** 2026 campaigns average 2.16 per 1,000 vs 1.32 in 2025. In 2026 Dulcy also changed lists (DiscoLike), verification and the copy template at the same time, so a copy feature that is common in 2026 looks better partly because of everything else. Patterns below are checked within the same client and within 2026 where possible, and marked weak where they are not.
- **Feature tags** (structure, CTA type, offer, word count) are script-generated from the first spintax option of Email 1 (variant with the most sends). They are a reading aid; the verbatim copy is the evidence.
- **Privacy:** no prospect data is in this file. Two items were redacted in Visionair AI scripts: a sender's personal email address in a fake "forwarded" thread, and a demo phone number.

## Key results (one screen)
- **Agency average: 1.66 interested per 1,000 emails sent** (5,187 interested / 3,118,867 sent, 367 campaigns with 500+ sent, all clients ever). Median campaign 1.09. Current 7 owners: 1.98. 2026 campaigns: 2.16 vs 2025: 1.32.
- **Follow-ups matter:** steps 2-3 produce 51.9% of all interested (P1).
- **Open with an observation or a thing you made, not a question:** 2.43 vs 1.76 per 1,000 in 2026; A/B p = 0.0065 (P2).
- **Offer one concrete thing built from what they already have:** won 3 significant A/B tests (P3).
- **"Or tell me to pound sand" P.S.:** 2.37 vs guarantee P.S. 0.74 and no P.S. 0.88; Scroll Stop 2.48 vs 0.75 (P4).
- **Short topic-word subjects:** 1-2 words 2.24 vs 1.14; Scroll Stop `{our creators|influencers|creators}` 2.65 vs `Use influencer marketing for {COMPANY}` 0.71 (P5).
- **Fit beats copy:** abstract adtech/fintech/SaaS clients sit at 0.15-0.34 whatever the copy (P9). **Seasonal hooks and re-sends die** (P7, P8). **Only 6 of 91 Email 1 A/B tests were significant** (P10).

## 4. Per-variant A/B results

Source: Airtable Sequence Analytics (per step and variant totals synced from EmailBison; 859 rows, 188 EmailBison campaigns). A "test" = one step of one campaign with 2+ variants that each have 200+ sends. Winner = highest interested per send; p = two-sided two-proportion z-test, best vs worst variant.

- **106 tests found (91 on Email 1). Only 6 reach p < 0.05**, all on Email 1. With 91 Email 1 tests, about 5 would reach p < 0.05 by chance alone, so most "winners" in this table are noise. Typical variants had 300 to 1,000 sends and 0 to 10 interested; at a ~2 per 1,000 base rate a variant needs roughly 5,000+ sends to detect a 2x difference.
- EmailBison per-step stats could not be pulled directly (the stats endpoint is a POST; this was read-only, GET only), so Sequence Analytics is the only per-variant source. Variants with no Sequence Analytics rows have no A/B numbers.

## 5. Patterns: what top campaigns share that bottom ones don't

Base: campaigns with 500+ sent **and** Email 1 copy found (196 of 367; 1,555,406 sent; 2,681 interested; 1.72 per 1,000). "Within client" = same client, different campaigns, so offer and buyer type are partly held constant. Evidence grades: **Strong** = a significant A/B result, or a clear gap that repeats inside several clients. **Medium** = a clear gap overall and inside 2+ clients, but confounded with time or list. **Weak** = small samples or mixed within clients.

### P1. Follow-ups produce over half of all interested replies (Strong)
Sequence Analytics, every step of every 500+ sent campaign with per-step data:

| Step | Emails sent | Replies | Interested | Per 1,000 sent | Share of all interested |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 424,922 | 9,989 | 874 | 2.06 | 46.7% |
| 2 | 395,529 | 4,224 | 517 | 1.31 | 27.6% |
| 3 | 370,385 | 3,484 | 455 | 1.23 | 24.3% |
| 4+ | 12,058 | 168 | 26 | 2.16 | 1.4% |

- Steps 2 and 3 bring **972 of 1,872 interested (51.9%)**. Each follow-up converts at about 60% of Email 1's rate (1.31 and 1.23 vs 2.06 per 1,000).
- Lesson: always write a 3-step sequence, and make steps 2 and 3 real emails (a new angle, proof, or a "wrong person?" check), not "bumping this".

### P2. An observation or offer opener beats a question opener (Medium, one significant A/B)
- 2026 campaigns: Email 1 opens with a statement or observation **2.43 per 1,000** (91 campaigns, 619,777 sent) vs opens with a question **1.76** (50 campaigns, 485,611 sent). All years: 1.83 vs 1.55.
- Within client: Dulcy 2.40 (10 camps, 75,960 sent) vs 0.92 (12 camps, 95,961); Twenty West 3.40 (17 camps, 152,415) vs 2.21 (20 camps, 270,061); Shop Innovator 5.53 (5 camps, 24,958) vs 2.15 (3 camps, 22,287). Against it: A-Wing Visuals 3.88 for its one question-led campaign vs 0.75; Human Marketing 1.20 vs 1.03 (small).
- **A/B (p = 0.0065), Shop Innovator EB 465:** "{I made|I put together} a short video walking through product pages like {COMPANY}’s..." + "Want me to send it over?" got **18 interested on 2,586 sent (6.96/1k)**. The question-led variant ("{Do you know what’s|Have you figured out what’s} actually stopping your ecommerce store from converting...?" + "We’ve helped 500 home and garden brands...") got **5 on 2,589 (1.93/1k)**.
- Lesson: open with what you noticed or made for them. Keep the question for the CTA.

### P3. One concrete thing, built from what the prospect already has, beats a service pitch (Strong: 3 significant A/B results)
- **Twenty West EB 599 (p = 0.011):** one idea ("I had an idea for showing ur {HEROPRODUCT} in a {CONSUMERUSEOCCASION} moment... Mind if I put that together?") **9 interested / 373 sent (24.1/1k)** vs two ideas side by side ("I had two ideas... Mind if I put together the two versions?") **1 / 376 (2.7/1k)**. Small samples, big gap.
- **Twenty West EB 609 (p = 0.046) and EB 610 (p = 0.078), same test twice:** "We could turn usable product photos and clips you already have into a series of social posts" **14 / 976 (14.3/1k)** and **3 / 975** vs "We could handle turning product ideas like that into social posts, so your team isn't planning, writing and putting together every post themselves" **6 / 1,064 (5.6/1k)** and **0 / 1,009**. "Uses what you already have" won both runs; "we do the work for you" lost both.
- **Lead Flo EB 514 (p = 0.009), 9 variants:** "Could I send you 5 leads from people already looking for those services?" **9 / 537 (16.8/1k)** vs the pay-per-lead pitch "we help {SERVICE} companies get at least 4 new jobs a month on a pay-per-lead model" **1 / 556 (1.8/1k)**. (The winning variant's "we've already done this for 12 companies" line was later removed as unsupported. The offer won, not that claim. Lead Flo can only fulfil free leads in Southern California.)
- Offer tags, 2026 only: free audit / review / ideas / notes **3.17 per 1,000** (23 camps, 132,273 sent) and creators/talent 2.31 (24 camps) vs free work / trial / demo **1.29** (18 camps) and research/interview requests **0.87** (4 camps). All years: research/interview 0.69 (6 camps, 26,150 sent), the lowest offer type.

### P4. "Or tell me to pound sand" P.S. beats a guarantee P.S. and no P.S. (Medium, confounded with the 2026 template)
- All years: opt-out / permission P.S. ("P.S. or tell me to pound sand if you'd like..", "If you're already booked up, just tell me to go pound sand...") **2.37 per 1,000** (101 campaigns, 867,380 sent, 12 clients) vs **no P.S. 0.88** (48 camps, 359,483) vs **guarantee / no-cost P.S. 0.74** (25 camps, 174,874).
- Within client: **Scroll Stop 2.48** with the opt-out P.S. (16 camps, 166,849 sent) **vs 0.75** with "P.S. We always offer a guarantee around ROI and traffic outcomes to our clients. Just ask!" (24 camps, 173,126 sent). Twenty West 2.91 (30 camps) vs 1.04 with no P.S. (7 camps). Dulcy 1.90 (18 camps) vs 1.01 (3 camps). Forever Card 0.74 vs 0.26.
- Why only Medium: there is no A/B test that changes only the P.S. The opt-out P.S. arrived in 2026 together with DiscoLike lists, shorter copy and new subjects. MJJ's client also said the line "got good responses" (client file, 2026-11-06).
- Guarantee P.S. lines are also off-limits for most clients (no approved guarantee: Scroll Stop, Human, Twenty West, MJJ, Lead Flo).

### P5. Short 1 to 2 word subject lines, ideally the prospect's own topic word (Medium)
- Subjects of 1 to 2 words (first spintax option): **2.24 per 1,000** (90 camps, 825,597 sent) vs 3+ words **1.14** (106 camps, 729,809). 2026 only: 2.30 vs 1.71.
- Within Scroll Stop, same client and product: **`{our creators|influencers|creators}` 2.65 per 1,000** (13 camps, 153,518 sent) vs **`Use influencer marketing for {COMPANY}` 0.71** (26 camps, 196,943 sent).
- **`{KEYWORD}`** (the search term the prospect wants to rank for, used with an "I asked ChatGPT for {KEYWORD} in {CITY}" opener): **6.58 per 1,000** (8 camps, 42,241 sent; Twenty West AEO campaigns + Juiced Digital).
- Against it: Lead Flo's 3+ word local subjects (`event rentals in {CITY}?`, `{RESTAURANT} - yes or no?`) did better than its short ones (4.37 vs 1.14), and so did Shop Innovator (7.23 vs 2.15). So "short" is not the rule by itself. What fails is the pitch-style subject ("Use influencer marketing for X", "Would this make sense for {COMPANY}?" 0.17, "Quick question {FIRST_NAME}" 0.21).

### P6. Reference what the prospect already does, not a generic value (Weak: two small but repeated A/B tests)
- Scroll Stop EB 228 (p = 0.024): "{COMPANY} {works with|collaborates with|partners with} creators to show the best of {PERSONAL}" **5 / 2,969** vs "{COMPANY} is {all about|focused on} staying true to {PERSONAL}" **0 / 3,023**. EB 232 (p = 0.028), the same swap for fashion: **5 / 2,337 vs 0 / 2,253**. Same direction twice, but 5 vs 0 is a tiny count.

### P7. Re-sending the same copy decays toward zero (Medium)
- Scroll Stop numbered re-runs (same copy family, later batches): Sports (Data Bank) 0.55 → 0.48 → **0.00 → 0.00** per 1,000 (runs 1 to 4, 5,834 to 7,242 sent each). Consumer Electronics (Hugh) 0.78 → 0.81 → 0.24 → 0.49. Apps (Jack) 0.90 → 0.19. Fashion OV 0.76 → 0.41 → 0.09.
- All campaigns named as copies or re-runs ("Copy of", "Re-Up", "2nd/3rd round", numbered): 1.42 per 1,000 (37 camps, 442,855 sent) vs 1.70 for the rest. The gap is smaller overall because some re-ups went to fresh lists (Twenty West Local Contractors Re-Up 2.10).

### P8. Timely or seasonal hooks with no offer get nothing (Strong, consistent)
- Scroll Stop "Post Q4 reset, pre scale efficiency" **0 interested / 10,446 sent**, "Pre-Season Advantage for 2026" **0 / 4,767**, "MWC Barcelona" **0 / 2,813**, "Beauty Seasonal (May)" 7 / 6,333 (1.11), "Spring Campaign" 0 / 962, "Fashion Winter wear" 0 / 2,679.

### P9. Offer-market fit sets the ceiling. Copy can't rescue an abstract product (Strong)
- Lowest clients, many campaigns, many copy styles: Retail Media Group 0.15 per 1,000 (6 camps, 40,816 sent; geo-fenced ad targeting), Unleashed Technologies 0.22 (11 camps; enterprise web builds), LuckyDiem 0.29 (card-linked offers), Alpha Software 0.30 (QC form software), TargetonStar 0.34 (white-label programmatic), Collabor 0.00 (influencer SaaS). TargetonStar's two "Likihood" campaigns: **0 interested on 24,820 sent**.
- Highest: concrete, visual or local offers. Slogan Social 6.59 (exclusive creator roster), Shop Innovator 3.94 (video teardown of their product page), MJJ 3.65 (jewelry repair overflow at wholesale), Lead Flo 3.19 (free local leads / free property shoot), Twenty West 2.63 (AEO notes, product-post ideas).

### P10. Most A/B "winners" are noise; test bigger (Strong, method finding)
- 91 Email 1 tests with 2+ variants of 200+ sends: **only 6 reached p < 0.05, about what chance alone gives (~5).** Typical variants had 300 to 1,000 sends and 0 to 10 interested. At ~2 per 1,000, detecting a 2x lift needs roughly 5,000+ sends per variant. Lesson for Scribe: test 2 variants that differ in **one** element (opener, offer or P.S.), not 9 variants of everything.

### Weak or no evidence (don't claim these)
- **CTA type:** meeting/call ask 2.26 per 1,000 (36 camps), permission-to-send ("Want me to send it over?") 1.61 (78), interest check ("Worth a chat?", "Open to it?") 1.25 (34). Mixed within clients, and no clean A/B. The two significant A/B tests that changed the CTA both favoured permission-to-send.
- **Word count:** ≤50 words 1.88 per 1,000, 51-80 1.70, 81+ 1.26 (all years). 2026 only: 2.28 / 2.04 / 2.01, which is flat. Short is not proven to win on its own.
- **Proof in Email 1 (incl. P.S.):** 1.91 per 1,000 with proof (71 camps) vs 1.63 without (125) overall, but mixed within clients. Proof did better at Dulcy (3.10 vs 1.02) and Shop Innovator (4.20 vs 1.96). No proof did better at Lead Flo (3.76 vs 2.47), Twenty West (2.88 vs 2.18) and Scroll Stop (1.54 vs 1.30). In the Shop Innovator A/B the variant carrying "We’ve helped 500 home and garden brands" lost.
- **{VARIABLE} in subject:** 2026 2.75 with vs 1.95 without, but all years 1.59 vs 1.81. Mixed.
- **Links in Email 1:** 0.54 per 1,000 with a link (8 camps, 57,354 sent) vs 1.77 without. Few campaigns, but consistent with deliverability advice. Avoid links in Email 1.
