## Reusable structure

Use one supported relevance cue or concrete offer, one clear deliverable/condition, and one next decision. The source often makes a tangible resource, sample, finding or comparison easier to understand than a generic service pitch. Treat that as a writing hypothesis, not proof of reply lift.

Adapt these scaffolds only with approved facts; brackets are unresolved fields, not send-ready copy:
- Available resource (1, 20, 70, 95): “[Verified resource] is available for [relevant use case]. Would [approved next step] be useful?”
- Sample (23, 49, 58, 73): “We can provide [bounded usable sample] on [approved terms] so you can evaluate [specific fit]. Want [delivery next step]?”
- Diagnostic (5, 29, 56, 89): “[Verified observation, if available]. We can check [specific question] and provide [output]. Would you like [next step]?”
- Comparison (22, 37, 69, 83): “We can compare [like-for-like scope] using [necessary input]. You’ll receive [output] on [approved terms]. Worth comparing?”
- Contingent fee (2, 28, 64, 88, 99): “We [approved service]; payment is triggered by [precisely defined event and material conditions]. Want [approved next decision]?”

## Avoid importing unsafe assertions

- “I checked/called/built” requires evidence of that exact completed action. If not done, offer to do it only when feasible; otherwise omit it.
- A competitor’s ranking does not prove stolen calls; hiring does not prove desperation; a slow site does not establish lost revenue. Separate the observation from the hypothesis.
- Do not carry over amounts, percentages, dates, guarantees, named people, phone numbers or scarcity. Use only current same-client approval and supporting evidence.
- Do not claim financing eligibility, legal exposure, security vulnerabilities or recoverable money merely because a similar example says so. Escalate unsupported consequential claims.
- “Free,” “no upfront cost,” “pay on results” and “keep the sample” are different promises. Preserve approved conditions without a surprise call, purchase or access requirement. Never turn a sample yes into purchase intent.
- Keep existing claim-ledger, promise-contract, variant QA and review-only controls. Choose one CTA and do not send or save platform drafts through this reference.

## Finished-output offers: state and timing fidelity

For Aaron Shepherd's [October 22 four-image post](https://x.com/AaronxShepherd/status/2113344870249099323), resolve Atlas and read `references/front-end-offers-aaron-shepherd.md` for all 15 examples and source limits. Do not duplicate its offer policy. Describe produced, delivered, deployed and performance-validated as different states: a prepared ad is not tested creative, a rebuilt flow is not live, and a drafted quote is not sent. Separate an approved delivery deadline from a business-result forecast; “ready in 48 hours” cannot silently become orders within a week. “Outperforms your current best” requires the actual approved comparison evidence, not an intended test. Keep remaining buyer inputs and usage conditions visible. The source's illustrative charts and promotional results supply no client proof or conversion benchmark.
