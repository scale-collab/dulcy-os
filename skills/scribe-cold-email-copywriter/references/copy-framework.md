# Campaign copy framework

Version 2.2 · November 6, 2026

## Start with a message contract

Write these fields in plain words before the draft: who, why this buyer, one desired improvement, exact offer, reason to believe, and the meaning of a yes. Use the approved Atlas offer strategy recipe and canonical client/promise contract. Do not redesign the offer while polishing sentences.

## Select a structure, not a script

Choose after inspecting the approved capability, buyer context and available evidence. Lead with the strongest truthful reason to care: a comparable result, verified observation, useful insight, tangible preview or clear direct offer. Do not force every client into a free-work or audit template.

Question-led: ask a specific non-presumptive question, connect the approved solution and relevant proof, then one useful next step. A diagnostic question must allow a genuine answer that disproves our premise; use it to learn, not to force discomfort or treat a satisfactory existing solution as an objection to overcome. Avoid asking experts to explain an obvious problem merely to set up a pitch.

Offer-led: state the useful deliverable or outcome, explain briefly why this client is credible, then ask for the matching next step. Use when the value is immediately legible. Exact delivery terms outrank brevity.

Signal-led: state a verified observation, connect it cautiously to a relevant need, explain the approved mechanism/deliverable and ask for one next step. A signal provides context, not proof of a hidden problem.

One-line preview: use only when the recipient, deliverable and conditions remain unmistakable. Do not compress away the fact that a call or inputs are required, or turn an undefined free offer into a stronger promise.

A structure may omit a component already obvious from context. Never fill a box with a fabricated claim. Two frameworks blended coherently are not a failure merely because a template says so.

## Belief and specificity

Answer both: “Is this useful to a buyer like me?” and “Is there a credible reason this provider can do it?” Evidence may be an approved same-world result, verified technical capability, useful specific observation or relevant category source. The latter does not prove the provider's results.

Keep the recipient's real outcome in view. Money, time and status are useful lenses, not an exhaustive taxonomy: quality, delivery reliability, capacity, risk or a procurement milestone may be the immediate value. Do not add a monetary lift without evidence.

Explain a mechanism only enough to make the claim understandable: required input, what happens, usable output. Do not invent a named system or assume technical buyers want marketing claims instead of specifications.

Check the reason to choose this message, not just its structure. Use Atlas’s verified competitor evidence: if swapping the provider name leaves the same generic promise, prefer a relevant, supported difference in capability, proof or useful output. Treat unknown competitor/inbox exposure as unknown, and do not invent superiority or force novelty into winning copy. For an optional personalized opener, remove it temporarily: if the offer and reason to care are unchanged, connect it to a defensible useful implication or omit it. A signal may still help list selection without belonging in the email. These are editorial checks, not performance guarantees. Source scope: Atlas’s November 6 review ledger.

Illustration: Nick Abraham’s November 4 hairnet campaign describes counting each case before shipment. A real, approved process difference can explain buyer value more concretely than “high quality.” Use only the current client’s actual process and substantiated consequence; do not borrow the example’s results or make free samples the default.

## Openers and subject/preview

Use true business relevance. A name/company variable alone is not a signal, but a well-defined segment can establish relevance without a bespoke compliment. Prefer a clean segment sentence to AI-generated familiarity.

Review subject plus first visible line as one entry point. Make them clear and consistent with the body. Do not imply an existing relationship, urgent deadline, referral, customer status or prior thread that does not exist. No false Re:/Fwd: or invented “following up on our chat.”

Keep customer/personal data within the authorized use and relevant sources. Do not research private life to manufacture personalization.

## CTA selection

Choose one decision that maps to a real, approved action:
- Receive a specific ready or fulfillable resource by its stated method.
- Provide one genuinely necessary input.
- Review an example or technical fit.
- Arrange expressly agreed call-delivered value.
- Take a useful product/RFQ/pilot/meeting step.

An “interest” CTA is not harmless if it conceals delivery conditions. “Can I send X?” commits to send X if accepted, not to ask for a sales call first. A meeting CTA must have an actual purpose/output; avoid empty “pick your brain” framing.

After a reply, use Scribe reply decisions. Answer requested questions, honor owed value, and recognize explicit meeting readiness without compulsory nurturing. Do not reuse a cold CTA regardless of what the prospect said.

## Language and editing

Write like a competent person to another competent person. Use concrete nouns and verbs. Remove inflated adjectives, abstract benefits, needless hedging, generic flattery and long setup. Preserve honest uncertainty and technical qualifiers.

Usually 30–75 words, 3–4 short sentences for a first touch. Fewer can work; material terms may need more. Keep one main offer. Read aloud and remove phrases the actual sender would not use.

Warmth comes from relevance and clarity. Do not add thanks-for-replying, “great question” or phatic padding to inbound replies. Use the selected client tone/language rather than a creator's personality or old template.

## Sequence and variants

Keep one approved offer throughout the cold sequence. Give follow-ups distinct work:
1. Clarify the mechanism or immediate output.
2. Add relevant approved proof or a practical example.
3. Close the loop or offer an appropriate smaller next decision without guilt or false urgency.

Use only the required number of steps. Do not force four emails, a “breakup” trope or a universal cadence. Stop cold-sequence logic on a reply, opt-out, booking or other qualifying state; execution belongs to the authorized workflow.

Meaningful tests change one stated lever: pain hypothesis, entry offer, proof, genuine signal, mechanism explanation or CTA friction. Prefer strategy-relevant tests to endless synonyms. A clean subject test is permissible when deliberately isolated and sufficiently measured; it is not the first fix for a broken offer or list. Label multi-component concept tests honestly.

## Review

Use the separate copy-review contract for claim traceability, promise fidelity, variable rendering, human approval and the exact handoff. A style score does not prove truth, launch approval or deliverability.
