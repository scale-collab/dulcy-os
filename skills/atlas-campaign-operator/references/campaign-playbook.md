## Purpose

Use this reference for the campaign-level logic Atlas should apply. It is distilled from Dulcy's Cold Email Source of Truth, Agency Scaling notes, and 2-Hour Cold Email Campaign SOP.

## New-client evidence checklist

Useful inputs include:

- current offer, packages, and pricing
- current customers and best-fit customer patterns
- case studies and Loom/video proof
- guarantees
- reactivation lists and exclusions
- client pain research
- client interviews
- previous sales calls
- what makes the client unique
- current go-to-market process and assets
- free value the client can realistically provide

Do not require every item if the campaign can be decided with less. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the campaign decision.

## Offer build-out

### Understand current GTM
Find out what the client already does and what assets can be reused.

### Find free value
Good front-end value can include an audit, sample deliverable, teardown, benchmark, free build, or another concrete low-friction asset tied to the paid service.

### Identify a comfortable guarantee
Use a guarantee only if the client can actually support it. It may be a timeline, deliverable, or result.

### Centralize the message
Choose the strongest single element: pain, free value, guarantee, unique mechanism, or another compelling differentiator. Build one campaign around one clear message.

## Campaign creation rules

- Start broad enough to learn, then narrow using results.
- If the pain/angle is not validated, test 2–3 distinct hypotheses rather than combining them.
- Do not mix offer types inside one campaign.
- Once an angle is validated, create variants around pain, personalization, proof, or related execution levers.
- Do not test five ideas in one campaign.
- Do not write copy while the ICP is still fuzzy.
- Do not keep rewording a script that already works.

## Campaign thesis

Use:

We believe [ICP] struggles with [PAIN].
Most currently solve it through [ALTERNATIVE].
This creates [NEGATIVE CONSEQUENCE].
Our client helps solve this through [MECHANISM].
We can credibly make this claim because of [PROOF].
The desired outcome is [DREAM OUTCOME].

## Dream outcome

Choose the dominant outcome that matters to the recipient. These are useful lenses, not an exhaustive list; quality, capacity, reliability, risk and procurement progress may be more relevant:

- **Money** — revenue, profit, ROI, pipeline, cost savings
- **Time** — less manual work, faster execution, fewer bottlenecks
- **Status** — recognition, credibility, influence, acquisition appeal

Make it specific to the recipient's situation and believable from where they are now.

## Proof selection

Proof should reinforce the exact dream outcome.

Useful proof types:

- **Social proof** — a similar client achieved the desired result.
- **Third-party authority** — a source may support the category/mechanism, but cannot prove this client delivered a result or imply endorsement.
- **Knowledge proof** — specific research or niche expertise demonstrates understanding.

Prefer similarity in industry, business model, company stage/size, team structure, and desired outcome. Generic case studies are weaker than closely matched proof.

## Existing campaign decision tree

First isolate delivery, list quality, relevance, intent, fulfillment, reply ownership, attendance and sales-stage bottlenecks with comparable denominators and sufficient observation time. Apply the branches below only when that evidence supports them. A poor booking rate alone does not justify a market pivot. Offer selection and readiness use `references/offer-strategy.md` and `references/offer-framework.md`; no alternate offer hierarchy is implied here.

### 1. Performing at or above average
Signal: email-to-lead / outcome ratio is close to or better than the relevant agency/client benchmark.

Action: **Top off.**

- Reuse the previous targeting logic.
- Build lookalikes / net-new accounts.
- Enrich and validate new contacts.
- Add volume without changing the core offer/copy unnecessarily.

### 2. Materially underperforming and a new vertical is viable
Signal: campaign is significantly below the relevant benchmark and the same offer can solve the problem for another vertical.

Action: **Same offer, new vertical.**

- Adapt targeting and relevance to the new industry.
- Launch as a fresh campaign so the test is interpretable.

### 3. Underperforming and vertical is constrained
Ask: Can the client make a better/new offer?

If yes: **New offer.** Build around it and launch a fresh test.

If no: **Copy/angle testing** within the existing market is the fallback. Treat this as lower-probability than a substantive offer/market change.

## Lead-list quality rules Atlas should enforce

Atlas does not build the list, but should require the list specialist to:

- start with a small company sample and validate ICP fit before scaling
- use detailed ICP descriptions rather than generic category labels
- separate clear fits, borderline fits, and cuts
- aim for roughly 80–85%+ company usability before scaling; 85%+ is a strong target
- evaluate contacts based on title relevance plus company size, not title string alone
- manually QA the final output and refine recurring false positives

## Launch gate

Before launch, the whole campaign should reduce to:

- one ICP
- one pain
- one proof direction
- one CTA direction
- one clean list

If the campaign cannot be summarized this simply, it is probably testing too many things at once.
