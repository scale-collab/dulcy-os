# Specialist Handoffs

## Lead List Specialist handoff

Give the lead-list specialist this information whenever available:

- Client
- Campaign thesis
- Offer / front-end value
- ICP description
- Geography
- Company size or other hard filters
- Required company signals
- Exclusion signals
- Relevant decision-maker functions/personas
- Minimum desired volume
- Existing customer/domain exclusions
- Whether this is a top-off/lookalike build or a fresh-market build
- Any personalization variables the copy will require

Require the specialist to return:

- account targeting logic
- contact/persona logic
- QA method and usability score
- Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Cut interpretation when applicable
- expected usable volume
- unresolved assumptions or risks

Do not dictate exact titles unless the evidence requires them. The lead-list specialist should account for title relevance and company size.

## Copywriter handoff

Give the copywriter:

- Client
- Campaign thesis
- ICP / decision-maker
- Primary pain
- Current alternative and negative consequence
- Mechanism
- Dream outcome
- Front-end offer / lead magnet / guarantee
- Strongest relevant proof
- Market-specific observations
- Any personalization variables available from the list
- Whether the campaign is a new angle, proven angle variant, or vertical adaptation

Require the copywriter to keep the campaign centered on the same thesis. Do not allow the copywriter to invent a different offer just to make the email more interesting.

## Reconciliation check

After both specialists return, verify:

1. The list targets people who experience or own the stated pain.
2. The copy references an outcome relevant to those people.
3. Personalization variables actually exist in the final list.
4. Proof matches the ICP and outcome.
5. The CTA and offer do not require information the lead list cannot support.

If any answer is no, reconcile before launch.


## Canonical offer → copy review handoff

Read Atlas’s `references/offer-strategy.md` and `references/offer-framework.md`, then Scribe’s `references/copy-review-contract.md`. Pass client/workspace/campaign, approved context/offer version, thesis, buyer/anti-ICP, observation vs inference, exact offer/promise, mechanism, proof with claim-level sources and limits, fulfillment readiness/owner, CTA meaning, prohibited claims, available variables/fallbacks and one test hypothesis.

Scribe returns final copy, meaningful variant labels, claim/variable checks, promise consistency, rendering coverage, status and exact approval gaps. Atlas reviews alignment; it does not duplicate specialist rules. A blocker stays a blocker even when creative quality is high. Preserve the Scribe v1.1 promise/settings contract and Scribe’s reply-decision module v1.1 as the canonical dependencies.


## Strategy-to-script handoff

For new campaigns and material pivots, pass the GTM brief from [gtm-strategy-to-simple-copy.md](gtm-strategy-to-simple-copy.md). Distinguish campaign-recipient ICP from any downstream end-buyer segment. Include the evidenced acquisition route, proposed opportunity, feasibility limits and per-variable source/date, observation status, approved wording, required/optional status, fallback/hold rule and verified platform mapping. Scout returns sourced usable fields and coverage; Scribe returns the simple script and representative rendered leads. Reconcile them before readiness: an appealing variable idea without source coverage or a field mapping is not campaign-ready personalization.
