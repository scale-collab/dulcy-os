## Purpose

Use this reference for detailed onboarding, reporting, retention, and account-health decisions.

## Dulcy delivery arc

A normal outbound relationship progresses through four broad delivery phases:

1. **Setup (Day 0–1)** — onboarding, Slack/channel setup, domains, inboxes, ESP access, masking and required access.
2. **Strategy (Day 1–5)** — review onboarding inputs, campaign thesis, offer, sales assets, automations, reply context, case studies, client interviews and sales calls.
3. **Build (Day 6–15)** — lead lists, scoring, verification, copy, variables, reply preparation and approval.
4. **Launch / Optimize (Day 15–45+)** — ramp sending, test meaningful angles, monitor replies and optimize based on actual market feedback.

These are operating expectations, not guaranteed deadlines. Missing client inputs or infrastructure problems can shift timing.

## Client inputs that matter

Strong delivery depends on receiving enough context to make outreach credible. Useful inputs include:

- client offer, packages and pricing
- clear explanation of what makes the company different
- target ICPs and exclusions
- case studies / testimonials / interviews
- previous sales calls and common objections
- guarantees or risk reversals
- free-value assets or lead magnets
- current go-to-market process
- current clients or prior positive-reply domains for exclusion/lookalikes
- sales-process details after a meeting is booked

Do not treat onboarding as administrative completion. The purpose is to acquire the evidence and language needed to generate and convert demand.

## Set expectations around the full funnel

Cold outbound performance is not only an email problem. Track the chain:

**Emails / people contacted → Replies → Positive replies → Booked calls → Held calls → Opportunities → Closed deals**

Also examine:

- bounce rate / sender health
- lead quality
- speed to lead
- show rate
- sales close rate
- objections and market feedback

A client can have healthy outbound with a weak sales process, or poor outbound with a strong sales process. Diagnose the correct layer before communicating a fix.

## Practical benchmark framing

Use agency or client-specific benchmarks when available. Do not represent a generic benchmark as a guarantee.

A Dulcy partner model has historically framed a 20K-email example as approximately:

- 400 replies
- 40–80 positive replies
- 8–16 calls booked
- 2–4 closed deals

Results depend on offer, niche, pricing, proof, speed to response, sales conversion, infrastructure and other factors. Use actual campaign data whenever possible instead of leaning on this example.

## What "good" means

Every client should have a specific definition of success. Good examples:

- reach a target number of qualified held meetings
- validate a specific ICP / offer combination
- establish a repeatable email-to-lead ratio
- make outbound a reliable acquisition channel
- create enough qualified pipeline to justify expansion

Weak definitions:

- get more leads
- improve outreach
- scale faster

When a client lacks a finish line, each renewal becomes a subjective decision about whether they still "like" the service.

## Why clients churn

Do not assume churn is caused only by weak delivery. Common structural causes include:

1. Wrong-fit clients were signed in the first place.
2. There is no expansion path after the initial outcome.
3. The client was never shown what success specifically looks like.
4. The relationship is dependent on one founder or individual rather than the team/process.
5. Problems are surfaced too late.
6. The client cannot connect top-of-funnel activity to downstream business outcomes.

Retention actions should address the real cause rather than simply increasing communication volume.

## Account health model

### Green

- client understands the plan
- approvals are timely
- campaign is producing usable signal or clear learning
- next action is known
- client sentiment is constructive
- sales follow-up is functioning

### Yellow

- results are below expectation but diagnosis/action is credible
- lead-quality concerns are emerging
- client is slow to approve or respond
- no-shows or close rate are weakening perceived value
- client repeatedly asks for clarification on progress
- next milestone is unclear

### Red

- client is openly questioning continuation, refund, or value
- results are poor and no credible recovery plan exists
- trust has deteriorated
- repeated commitments were missed
- serious service or billing dispute exists
- client is disengaged while dissatisfaction signals are present

Health is evidence-based, not an emotional label.

## Weekly account review

For each client, review:

1. performance since last review
2. meaningful market feedback
3. booked / held / closed outcomes where available
4. infrastructure or volume constraints
5. outstanding client actions / approvals
6. churn-risk signals
7. testimonial opportunity
8. expansion opportunity
9. next milestone
10. internal owner and deadline for the next action

When infrastructure or offboarding may explain a constraint, ask Airtable operations for a read-only reconciliation of current client status, provider inventory and sequencer inventory. Flag disconnected senders, unexpected warmup-state differences, missing client assignments, provider-canceled domains still in the sequencer, active provider domains absent there, and retained infrastructure for confirmed ended clients. Check whether an exception is intentional or shared before recommending a change. Return the discrepancy, source/time, affected client, owner and next review action; do not silently cancel, reupload, reassign or alter warmup. ## Testimonial timing

Ask after a meaningful, specific result. Capture:

- what was happening before
- what Dulcy changed
- specific measurable result
- timeframe
- downstream business impact
- what became easier / possible afterward

Outcome-based proof is more useful than vanity metrics.

## Expansion timing

Expansion should follow evidence. Examples:

- validated campaign needs more volume
- winning offer can move into a new vertical
- client has sufficient sales capacity for more opportunities
- LinkedIn / calling / SMS can support an already-working email motion
- additional domains/inboxes are justified by economics

Do not upsell merely because the account is old enough.
