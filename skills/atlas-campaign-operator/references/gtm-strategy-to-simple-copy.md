# Comprehensive GTM, simple scripts

Apply the user's October 23, 2026 campaign-method preference: do the strategic work upfront; express one useful, credible opportunity in a short script with relevant lead-specific context. This is a planning and review method, not permission to launch, enrich, spend or change a live workflow.

## Build one executable GTM brief

1. **Client and market:** Confirm the actual capability, approved offer, proof, delivery owner/capacity and economics. Research current demand and market direction with dated evidence; a creator's claim that an industry always grows is not a qualification rule.
2. **Acquisition route:** Map how this buyer currently wins business and any evidenced gap. Distributor-, partner- or event-led acquisition can suggest a direct-channel opportunity, not prove dissatisfaction or dependence. Check channel agreements/conflict, service coverage, procurement requirements and the ability to fulfill direct sales; bypassing a distributor is not automatically advantageous.
3. **Two-sided targeting:** Distinguish the campaign recipient/client ICP from the end-buyer segment the proposed service would help that recipient reach. Specify fit, buyer roles, geography, exclusions and credible access for both. For example, a manufacturer-to-restaurant route is a hypothesis to validate, not proof restaurants need that manufacturer's product. If the offer has no downstream-buyer acquisition component, mark that side not applicable rather than inventing one.
4. **Specific opportunity:** State why this buyer could benefit, how the approved mechanism reaches the named end-buyer, what the buyer receives and the limits. Differentiate through a useful market-specific route, not generic “more leads” or cosmetic industry substitutions. Use actual client facts and prospect observations; label untested assumptions.
5. **Evidence and variables:** Give Scout the fields needed to establish fit and give Scribe only the supported fields needed for the short script. Share a versioned variable contract: concept, source/date, observed vs inferred status, permitted wording, type, actual platform field mapping, optional/required flag, fallback/hold rule and QA examples. Examples such as buyer segment, use case, territory or verified exhibitor context are proposed concepts, not existing backend field names.
6. **Conversion and fulfillment:** Bind the first-touch CTA, reply handling, qualification, promised asset/service, owner, calendar/next step and real delivery capacity to the same client/promise version. Adjacent-market proof may establish relevant experience only with its true scope disclosed; it does not establish experience in the new vertical.
7. **Bounded test:** Propose a small approved test with explicit unit, exposure, observation window, outcome definitions, spend/volume bounds and stop/iteration criteria. Measure downstream quality and fulfillment as well as replies. A micro-test supplies directional evidence, not proof of repeatability or permission to scale.

Keep the working brief complete but proportional. Retrieve existing valid facts rather than asking for them again. Missing material inputs block the affected claim or action, not all useful preparation. For a winning top-up, preserve the tested offer and update only facts that changed.

## Rank segments without outcome leakage

Before prioritizing a segment from historical wins, define the eligible cohort, acquisition route, offer/version, qualification stage, observation window and decision being informed. Use this client's actual records, not a creator's claimed rates.

- Compare won, lost and still-open opportunities from comparable periods; keep nonresponses and unknown outcomes separate from losses. A winners-only export supplies examples, not a close-rate denominator.
- Use attributes observable before the targeting decision. A fact learned only after a deal closed cannot validate a prospecting filter. Record source/date and missingness; avoid comparing inbound/referral buyers with cold-acquired buyers as if their paths were equivalent.
- Report counts and outcome maturity. Short observed cycles can select only early-closing winners while slower opportunities remain open. Treat apparent conversion/speed differences cautiously when cohorts are small, incomplete or differently aged.
- Rank with deal value/margin, reachable fit and real fulfillment capacity as well as conversion and speed. Retain a bounded challenger test rather than excluding the rest of the market from an untested pattern.
- Signal checks must be worth their incremental cost. Ads, hiring or new locations are observations, not proof of growth, budget or need. Unobserved is not absent; a zero score must not silently disqualify a good-fit company or authorize paid enrichment.

## Compress the brief into copy

Keep one buyer, one opportunity, one approved mechanism/proof and one next decision. Let lead-specific variables supply relevant context while the sentence structure remains simple. Do not put the whole GTM plan into the email or personalize every field for its own sake. Preserve required qualifiers and promise conditions even when brevity requires an exception.

Show the strategy, variable contract and a few representative rendered leads separately from the short script. Scout owns evidence and mapping readiness; Scribe owns language, fallback/spintax rendering and claim QA; Atlas reconciles them. Unmapped fields remain internal placeholders, not upload-ready tokens. Unsupported material values hold the row; optional observations may be omitted or replaced only with an approved truthful segment-level fallback.

Never turn a proposal into “we already opened conversations,” an available resource into “I made this for you,” or another creator's pipeline into this client's result. Use each exact claim only with same-client evidence, current approval and the proper timeframe/denominator. Public business signals cannot justify inferring individuals' health conditions or exploiting personal medical events. Preserve existing privacy, suppression, deduplication, managed stage and approval gates.
