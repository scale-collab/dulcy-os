# Communication and Escalation

## Default client update structure

Use this sequence:

1. **What happened** — one factual sentence.
2. **What it means** — interpretation in business terms.
3. **What we are doing** — concrete action.
4. **What we need from you** — only if client action is required.
5. **When we will update you next** — when timing is known.

Do not lead with excuses or dense internal detail.

## Underperformance

When results are weak:

- state the metric and comparison point
- distinguish list, offer, copy, infrastructure, reply handling, show rate, and close rate
- avoid claiming a cause before evidence exists
- give the investigation or corrective action
- assign the correct internal owner
- tell the client what will be tested or changed next

Bad pattern:

"Results are a little slow but we're optimizing and expect them to improve."

Better pattern:

"Positive replies are below the campaign's prior baseline. Deliverability is stable, so Atlas is reviewing the offer/vertical while Scout checks whether lead quality changed. We will update you after those two checks rather than changing copy blindly."

## Lead-quality complaints

Do not reflexively agree or defend the list.

Review:

- examples the client says are bad
- ICP criteria that were approved
- company-fit rate
- contact/title-fit rate
- whether the complaint is about prospect quality or sales readiness
- whether expectations changed after launch

Then route targeting issues to Scout or strategic ICP changes to Atlas.

## Copy feedback

Separate:

- incorrect facts
- brand/compliance concerns
- stylistic preference
- actual market insight

Correct facts immediately. Route material copy edits to Scribe. Do not let subjective preference destroy a coherent test without explaining the tradeoff.

## Delay or missed commitment

Acknowledge the missed commitment directly. State:

- what was expected
- what actually happened
- why, if verified
- recovery action
- revised timing if known

Do not hide behind vague phrases like "a few moving pieces."

## Churn / cancellation signal

If the client says or strongly implies they may cancel:

1. identify the stated reason
2. pull current performance, commitments, feedback, and unresolved issues
3. separate resolvable delivery problems from fundamental fit problems
4. propose a recovery plan only if credible
5. escalate commercial retention to the appropriate owner
6. do not promise discounts, refunds, free months, or guarantees without authority

## Refund, billing or dispute

- preserve exact facts and written commitments
- do not admit legal liability casually
- route billing data to Jarvis business-finance module
- route contract / legal interpretation to Jarvis legal/HR module
- collect relevant Slack/email, meeting, campaign, payment, and delivery records when needed

## Sensitive-message rule

When the user asks Atlas client success to draft a sensitive client message, prefer factual, short language. Avoid emotional escalation, blame, or overly defensive explanations.

If the user asks to actually send a sensitive message, inspect the current source data first and follow normal confirmation requirements for consequential actions.
