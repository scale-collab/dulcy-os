## Recipe and brief

Draft the internal recipe before writing the cold email:

For [specific buyer/use case] already [verified investment/current alternative], help address [one supported problem or desired improvement] through [approved input → process → deliverable], toward [buyer outcome]. Offer [immediate deliverable/next decision] with [exact quantity, mode, inputs, scope, cost and conditions]. Support it with [relevant evidence and its limits].

This is a planning scaffold, not a sentence to copy into every email. Omit a signal rather than manufacture it. Separate outcome hypothesis from what is contractually promised.

Capture:
- Client/workspace, campaign/thesis, context version, owner and source date.
- Buyer/company/use case, anti-ICP, stage, current alternative and desired outcome.
- Observation and source; inference and uncertainty; reason the segment can care.
- Paid service, actual mechanism, constraints and difference from alternatives.
- Immediate offer, exact scope/quantity, usable deliverable, delivery mode, required inputs, free/paid status, conditions, approved turnaround and owner.
- Proof approved for this client, exact supported claim and limitations.
- Cost, capacity and readiness; what proves delivery.
- CTA path and what a yes means: receive asset, provide input, schedule agreed fulfillment, request technical review/RFQ, or explore buying.
- Allowed claims / prohibited claims / missing approval.
- Recommended angle, testable hypothesis, main metric and guardrails.
- Status and approval evidence; never self-approve a proposed commercial term.

## Capability-to-offer check

Before inventing a lead magnet, inventory what the client already reliably produces during discovery, qualification, implementation or delivery. Pick a bounded output that answers this buyer's real next question and has a credible connection to the paid work. Existing work can reduce incremental effort; it does not prove that free delivery is economical or authorized.

Check both sides: the client's staffed capacity and the buyer's necessary source assets, access, quality, usage rights and willingness to supply inputs. Material eligibility belongs before commitment. If required inputs are absent, choose another useful next step or mark the offer incomplete; never promise finished output from nonexistent inputs.

Translate a capability into the buyer's actual process and economics, not a cosmetic industry-name swap. For example, the same humidity-control capability might relate to print-head reliability, crop conditions or electronics quality, but each claim still needs that client's technical evidence. An agency preview may reuse a real discovery output; a manufacturer may need an approved specification review or paid sample instead. These are method illustrations, not approved claims or default free work.

When onboarding leaves a mechanism unclear, corroborate it with current official product/documentation sources and surface conflicts for client resolution. AI suggestions and a website's marketing claim cannot substitute for approved delivery capability, terms or proof. This research does not authorize a second paid contact-review pass.

## Evidence ledger

For every material claim keep: claim ID, exact intended wording, client/offer ID, source URL or record, source date, evidence type, supported scope, qualifier, approver/date, and expiry or recheck trigger.

Use these distinctions:
- Approved client fact: may support a matching claim.
- Verified prospect observation: may support the exact observation; causal meaning may still be a hypothesis.
- Category/third-party evidence: may support the category; cannot prove this provider delivered it.
- User-SOP illustration or creator case: method/example only.
- Hypothesis or idea: internal test concept; cannot be asserted as a fact about the prospect.
- Unknown/conflict: block the affected claim or offer-readiness field.

Check denominator and timeframe for numerical proof. Revenue, profit, pipeline, appointments, attended qualified meetings, infrastructure cost and all-in acquisition cost are different quantities. Keep claimed contribution vs causal attribution separate. A famous company's method is not an endorsement or a right to reuse its results.

## Front-end selection

Choose on usefulness, belief, buyer effort, delivery feasibility and route to the paid outcome:

| Situation | Possible next step | Check before promising |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low trust, repeatable service output | Small service sample or useful preview | Standalone value, owner, capacity, quality and real transfer |
| Buyer needs insight before acting | Specific evidence, benchmark, teardown or example | Actual source/research; no claim the work is already done unless true |
| High-cost delivery or sensitive technical inputs | Scoped paid pilot or feasibility step | Approved cost/inputs/confidentiality route; no invented free work |
| Real fit/intent, technical purchase | Specification review, RFQ or focused discussion | Approved capability, procurement path and genuine required inputs |
| Known ready-to-meet interest | Concrete meeting purpose | Do not force a nurture asset or make the buyer re-qualify unnecessarily |
| Genuine research project | Interview/research exchange | Real project, purpose and deliverable; no disguised sales bait |

A front end should make the next buyer decision easier. It need not be digital, free or a meeting. A low-effort CTA that creates a large hidden fulfillment burden is not low friction overall.

For finished-output previews, define acceptance in buyer terms: what can they use, what work or access remains, and what usage rights actually transfer? Keep four states separate: produced, delivered, deployed and performance-validated. A finished asset is not a live implementation or proven improvement. Separate a controllable production/delivery deadline from time to orders, savings or other business results. For an incumbent comparison, specify the actual baseline, success metric, comparable exposure and observation window before claiming superiority; a proposed test is not completed proof. These checks adapt the [GrowthFlare examples](front-end-offers-aaron-shepherd.md), not their promised outcomes. Deployment, quote sending, CRM reactivation, paid testing and access changes still need their own bounded authorization; a free trial or skill update grants none.

## Economics and capacity

Use approved inputs; otherwise mark the calculation as a scenario and request the few missing values. These are internal planning checks, not guarantees:

- Fulfillment hours = expected accepted offers × hours per deliverable, plus QA/rework and handoff time.
- Fulfillment cost = production labor + tools/data/materials + QA/rework + delivery costs.
- Capacity check = demand under low/base/high acceptance scenarios vs available staffed throughput; include turnaround and backlog.
- Downstream value = qualified progression and actual sales contribution, not raw asset requests.
- Allowable acquisition/preview cost depends on approved margin, sales conversion and payback assumptions; do not invent them.

A high acceptance rate can make an offer worse if delivery fails. Cap or narrow the proposal before launch when the owner cannot fulfill at expected volume. Do not silently shrink quantities after acceptance. Price/free/guarantee/capacity changes require the right commercial approval.

Before recommending revenue-share, pay-per-sale or revenue guarantees, distinguish cold-acquired sales evidence from referral/inbound results. Establish the actual closer, relevant lead-to-sale and booked-to-held denominators, follow-up ownership, attribution evidence and responsibilities controlled by each party. Missing evidence is an unresolved dependency, not proof the client cannot sell. A refund does not reimburse all buyer time, opportunity cost or implementation burden.

## Hard gate and diagnostic

Hard gate: all applicable material items must pass for a complete review packet. `ready_for_review` never means approved to launch:
1. Specific buyer and believable relevance; no fabricated observation.
2. Same-client approved capability, proof and exact claim scope.
3. Exact promise, costs and conditions; no bait-and-switch.
4. Named owner, usable output, required inputs, capacity and supported turnaround.
5. Appropriate stage/CTA and legitimate route to fulfillment.
6. Approved commercial terms and appropriate confirmation controls.
7. Compatible Atlas thesis/Scout list criteria/Scribe copy/context version.

Use `concept` for incomplete exploratory ideas, `needs_client_facts` for a blocker, `ready_for_review` for a complete review packet, and `approved_for_test` only when explicit approval evidence covers the exact current offer/version and test scope. Never treat score, generated copy, a historical example or source visibility as approval.

Optionally rate buyer/problem clarity, belief/proof, value, ease, economics and channel fit as weak/adequate/strong, each with a reason. This is an editorial diagnostic, not a validated conversion predictor. Do not average away a hard-gate failure.

## B2B adaptations

All examples below are hypothetical structures, not approved Dulcy client promises.

Agency: paid creative production may be previewed by one approved concept, a relevant portfolio example or a bounded workshop. Specify whether the buyer receives a finished usable creative, a concept, or just a discussion. Do not call concepts finished ads or promise revenue from their existence.

Manufacturer: technical credibility may come from verified processes/materials/certifications and an approved sample or drawing review. Preserve exact tolerances, quantities, sample charges, lead times, confidentiality and procurement requirements. No default free audit, guaranteed cost saving, or one-call-close target.

Professional service: a documented method and a scoped diagnostic can establish fit without inventing a financial or legal result. Keep eligibility and professional review boundaries explicit.

SaaS/product: demo, sandbox, trial or a specific integration check can be sensible, but do not invent compatibility, security certification, setup effort or trial terms. Support implementation claims with current approved sources.

Dulcy list offer: 1,000 free usable leads on an explicitly disclosed fulfillment call is legitimate if approved and deliverable. Count actual usable transfer, not booking, preview or an unverified raw pull.
