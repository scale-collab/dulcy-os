# Next campaign: top off, new angle, new vertical or new offer (Atlas reads this)

Sources:
- Alex's "2-Hour Cold Email Campaign SOP": the tabs "When to top off, rebuild, or pivot a campaign" and "How to decide what campaign to launch next".
- His "COLD EMAIL SOURCE OF TRUTH" doc. The thresholds from it are added below when found.

## 1. Which move (SOP table)

| Signal | Question | Move |
|---|---|---|
| **At or above average:** the client's email-to-lead ratio is close to or better than the agency average | Is it converting at a healthy rate? If it's within range, it just needs volume | **Top off:** pull the previous targeting, build a lookalike list, enrich, validate, and add net-new contacts to the existing campaign |
| **40%+ below average** | Can the same offer go to a new vertical? "Usually the highest-probability play" | **Same offer, new vertical:** adapt the messaging and launch as a new campaign |
| **Underperforming and locked in** (one industry, the offer can't change) | Can the client create a new offer? | **New offer** if possible. If not, copy and angle iterations, the "lowest likelihood" of a real change |

## 2. Make each campaign improve the next (SOP)

1. **Separate the replies:** interested, objections and unanswered. Keep the message each person received next to their reply, and add sales-call notes plus anything the transcripts missed.
2. **Research the companies that showed interest** (site, what they sell, who to, team set-up). Find a pattern that is much more common among the interested ones than in the rest of the list, and make it a segment. Example: "80% of booked calls came from companies without sales leadership."
3. **One angle per buying situation:**
   - Use internal learnings, industry changes and the prospect's own words (Reddit, Trustpilot, reviews).
   - Keep their wording.
   - Give each situation its own insider message.
4. **Answer recurring objections earlier** with an honest pre-objection line.
5. **Turn this into the next campaign:**
   - lookalikes of the interested companies, with the message that worked;
   - a new segment with its own message;
   - a new angle for the remaining contactable leads (never unsubscribed, bounced or suppressed contacts).
6. **Check delivery health first.** "Changing the copy doesn't fix a sending problem."
7. **Write down the experiment:** who, what is said, and why it should matter. Keep the replies with it.

## 3. Alex's testing rules (2026-11-25)

- One direction per campaign, with 2 Email-1 angles that test something meaningful: USP, pain point or solution angle. Never word swaps.
- The winner is kept for top-ups.
- 1 strong proof minimum. No call ask in the cold sequence unless it is the only logical next step.
