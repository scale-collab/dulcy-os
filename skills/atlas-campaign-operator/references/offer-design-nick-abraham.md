## Source method, summarized

Abraham adapts Hormozi’s value equation: desired outcome and confidence in achieving it increase perceived value; waiting and required effort reduce it. Map obstacles to solutions, then package useful, economical components. He prioritizes industry/offer tests over subject-line tweaks and suggests four entry-offer types: fix a narrow issue, reveal an overlooked issue, provide a sample, or complete an initial step. His suggested prompt gathers service, industry, strongest result and affordable giveaway; proposes a package and three entry offers; then drafts a short email with relevance, offer, proof and a small permission-based ask. He also proposes guarantees, bonuses and urgency. These are options, not required ingredients.

## Dulcy adaptation: use only the incremental diagnostic

Load the existing offer framework and Scribe client-promise contract first; they remain authoritative. Do not repeat discovery already captured in the current client context. For a new offer or justified material revision:

1. Reuse verified service, buyer segment, approved relevant proof and fulfillment budget/capacity. Ask only for missing material inputs. Confirm unresolved assumptions with the owner before treating them as facts; confirmation of inputs is not commercial or launch approval.
2. Make a compact internal barrier map: **buyer obstacle/evidence → feasible solution/delivery mode → value lever improved → remaining buyer work → incremental cost/owner → approval gap**. Ground obstacles in client/buyer evidence or label them hypotheses. Prefer existing reliable capabilities over invented package elements.
3. Assess outcome specificity, confidence/evidence, time-to-usable-value and total buyer effort separately as weak/adequate/strong with reasons. Use this as an editorial diagnostic, not numerical multiplication, a conversion prediction, or permission to promise instant results or zero effort. Keep the paid outcome distinct from the immediate deliverable.
4. When a free front end is appropriate, use the four source types to generate up to three feasible alternatives. Rank by buyer usefulness and credibility, genuine effort, fulfillment economics/capacity and qualified progression to the paid service—not ease of saying yes alone. Provide fewer if fewer are supported. Retain paid pilots, technical reviews and direct discussions when they fit better; do not force a giveaway.
5. Add a bonus only to address an evidenced unresolved objection with approved scope and capacity. Add a guarantee or outcome-linked fee only under existing commercial/economic/legal checks. State urgency only from a current verified reason; otherwise omit it. Do not manufacture two bonuses, scarcity or a branded name to complete the source’s template.
6. Pass the selected offer, rejected alternatives/reasons, exact promise, approved proof, remaining inputs and test hypothesis to Scribe. Its existing writing and QA rules govern the email. For an existing campaign, diagnose the actual bottleneck first; this method does not override winning-campaign preservation or authorize changing several variables at once.

Most of the source reinforces existing Atlas/Scribe policy. This reference adds the explicit obstacle-to-solution/value-lever worksheet and the four-type ideation lens only. Preserve source priority, promise fidelity, hard gates, current SOPs, review-only drafting and separate approval for launch, sending, platform changes or new commercial commitments.
