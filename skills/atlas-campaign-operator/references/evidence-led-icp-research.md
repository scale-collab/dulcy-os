# Evidence-led ICP research and campaign strategy

## When and how to use

For new campaigns or material pivots, use this research method before finalizing the thesis and specialist handoffs. Reuse current, supported research for routine top-ups. The Market Research SOP is the shared human-readable procedure: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1AjWlLpCIHh9gtu12lSsaRU8WUVFF6mMd7__tE__L0yU/edit. This module operationalizes Alexander's October 23 request; it does not authorize a live campaign, provider job, external message, paid upgrade, or new purchase.

The 400 targeted searches are a maximum planning budget, not a required count or permission to spend. Start with existing approved client research, then pursue decision-relevant gaps. Stop when evidence is sufficient to make the bounded decision or a material client fact blocks it. Record searches used and unresolved gaps; do not pad to 400. Respect the current task's sourcing/credit authority and platform access restrictions.

## Required research and decision chain

1. **Establish the client truth.** Read the homepage, product/service pages, founder background and relevant PR, then approved onboarding, sales calls, customer examples, proof and commercial/fulfillment facts. Research the domain to understand technical concepts, not to fill unknown client capabilities with assumed facts. Label client-confirmed facts, public claims, analyst inference and untested hypothesis separately. Resolve the current version rather than using superseded reports.
2. **Map the market and alternatives.** Search direct competitors, “alternatives to X,” manual/internal work, incumbent tools and doing nothing. Assess emerging, growing-awareness or established/replacement market by segment, with dated evidence and confidence. Competitor count or a thin website alone does not establish maturity or first-mover status. Record positioning implications and switching friction. Battlecards must include who the alternative fits, strengths, limitations supported by evidence, when we win/lose, credible differentiation and proof still needed. Do not invent competitor shortcomings.
3. **Understand the work, not just the industry label.** Map the buyer's workflow, costly bottleneck, inputs, technology, constraints, failure modes and required output. Trace bottleneck → operational effect → business consequence → buyer KPI. Investigate who causes the pressure and who owns budget, uses, influences and approves the solution. Quantify only where supported; time saved is not automatically revenue earned. Validate the strongest outcome with actual market participants/client calls when public evidence is insufficient.
4. **Hear product-level pain in context.** Read relevant subreddit threads, review sites such as Trustpilot/G2/Capterra, specialist communities and sales calls. Keep short exact quotations with URL, date, speaker/role if available, surrounding context, relevance and representativeness limits. Separate quotes from paraphrase and inference; obey source quotation limits. Rank business consequences, workflow problems and role-level frustrations, not generic support/service complaints. Record technical vocabulary and its actual meaning; use it only when correct and useful, keeping the rest of the copy plain.
5. **Make the old-versus-new matrix.** For each priority workflow/problem, record current method, evidenced limitation, proposed client mechanism, potential outcome, adoption/security/integration burden, maturity/awareness, positioning angle, supporting sources and unknowns. A proposed improvement is not an achieved result. Choose one priority problem per test, rather than merging every attractive claim into a campaign.
6. **Build executable segments and sourcing paths.** Segment by actual use case, product/component/service delivered, workflow and capability where relevant. Use specialized roles to discover candidate companies, then verify company fit; distinguish company counts from people. Check directories, current customers/lookalikes, job pages, relevant certifications, RFPs, patents and filings only where they matter to this client's market. A directory category or certification alone is not proof of design capability or demand. Include two real example-company websites with evidence of fit and why they would buy, not merely famous logos. Give Scout inclusion/exclusion rules, observable evidence fields, example passes/fails, seed rationale and approved geography. Contact-country exclusions for India, Pakistan and Africa, unknown-country holds, suppression, dedupe and sample gates still apply regardless of creator examples.
7. **Separate fit from timing.** For each signal specify exact observation, source/date, freshness window, relevance, allowed wording and unknown/fallback treatment. A job posting, RFP or technology mention can establish an observation, not purchase intent, budget or dissatisfaction. Do not imply a company won a contract, uses a tool, offshores work or needs help without evidence. Validate a few examples before proposing expansion. No new monitoring or recurring scraping is authorized by this research module.
8. **Resolve the trust and adoption gap.** Identify why a buyer would not believe or adopt the offer. Choose relevant same-client proof, or propose a bounded benchmark/demo with licensed/public or specifically authorized data, comparable baseline, method, measured result, limitations, owner, cost/capacity and delivery conditions. A benchmark is not a customer case study or certification. Do not say it exists until completed and verified. Confidential or historic customer data remains confidential; age does not authorize use. NDAs, data access, security/compliance claims and guarantees require the appropriate factual and approval checks.
9. **Convert evidence into one testable plan.** Carry a source-linked chain from segment → workflow/pain → business outcome → mechanism/positioning → proof → CTA/fulfillment → measurable test. Present selected and rejected alternatives with reasons. Keep one major variable per test; define cohort, unit, exposure, time window, useful replies, bookings, held/qualified outcomes, delivery capacity and stop/iteration criteria. Signals among responders generate hypotheses; compare exposed nonresponders and downstream quality before declaring a winning segment. Extract real objections into truthful pre-objection handling, never invented promises.
10. **Hand off a short message, not the whole research.** Atlas owns the decision, Scout the evidence/fit and mapped variables, Scribe the language/claim QA. Pass the same approved client/promise version and variable contract. Render representative leads and hold unsupported material fields. Use specific, relevant subject lines without impersonating an internal colleague or falsely implying an existing thread. Preserve all managed company/contact samples, QA, verification, suppression, spend and human approval gates; prepare does not mean sent or launched.

## Deliverable contract and readiness gate

Produce a two-page Google Doc decision brief. Page 1: plain-language company summary; target industries/personas; two linked target-company examples and why; evidence-backed market maturity and concise old/new matrix. Page 2: ranked product pains with short sourced buyer wording; competitor mini-battlecards; selected positioning/thesis, proof/fulfillment, bounded test and unresolved decisions. Include a compact source directory with direct subreddit/thread/review links. Use concise entries, not unreadably small text. If the evidence cannot fit responsibly, keep the main report two pages and link a separately labeled evidence appendix for full battlecards, quotation context, source ledger and detailed variable/test contracts; do not disguise an appendix as part of a two-page report. Verify actual pagination in the rendered document.

Every selected decision must have its source or be explicitly labeled a hypothesis. Missing evidence is an output, not permission to fabricate. The evidence appendix/working ledger records URL, access date, claim, quote versus inference, confidence/limitations, applicable segment and decision affected. Clear the thesis for review only when the list criteria, actual capability, promise, proof, fulfillment and proposed test agree. A research score or finished document is not launch approval.

## Original user-supplied research prompt (preserved)

The prompt
Budget: 400 targeted searches.
I need you to gain an understanding of the ICP of (WEBSITE PROVIDED).
Start by digesting their home page contents, researching the founder and finding PR about the company.
Distill that company information into a well defined value proposition, if information is limited on the product, do further domain level research to fill in the blanks.
Try to find competitors of the brand by researching 'alternatives to X' on google.
Based on the number of competitors in the market and their maturity, create an assessment of the current market age »> is the product emerging, or is there some awareness, vs does the whole market already have a solution? Include a column on how we can position against it.
Explore relevant subreddits, competitors trust pilots or complaints about the old solution in order to get a feel for the exact language they use to describe their problems.
Rank their problems from big business concerns to personal issues, focus on product level problems not fluffy shit like support, bad service etc.
Create an old vs new matrix based on this. The old way of doing things vs the new way that is being laid
Outputs:
Company Summary in plain language what they do
Their target industries and personas + the website of 2 companies they'd want to sell to and why
The before and after matrix to understand market maturity.
Real problems their target market talks about in their own words with links to sources.
Their competitors, including battlecards so we can figure out how to win the positioning game.
List out relevant subreddits, threads, and links to review sites > sources are very important because we want to explore things in context.
Full research report should be 2 pages in a google doc.
