## 1. Role design

Start with the bottleneck, not the title.

Ask:
- What work is currently falling on the founder/manager that should not?
- What measurable business result must improve after this hire?
- What does this person own end-to-end?
- Which responsibilities are recurring versus occasional?
- Which failures are unacceptable?

Turn those answers into 3–5 measurable outcomes. Avoid scorecards with 15 equally important KPIs.

## 2. Interview design

Use structured interviews. Ask all candidates the same core questions and score them against the same rubric.

Useful question types:
- Evidence: "Tell me about the closest thing you've done to this exact job."
- Depth: "What did you personally own versus the team?"
- Process: "Walk me through how you did it."
- Numbers: "What was the baseline, target, and actual result?"
- Failure: "What went wrong and what did you change?"
- Coachability: present feedback, then ask the candidate to redo part of the exercise.
- Judgment: give an ambiguous scenario and ask what they would do first and why.

Avoid relying on hypothetical questions when a real work sample is possible.

## 3. Work-test rubrics

Score 1–5 or 1–10 on dimensions that matter to the role. Example dimensions:
- accuracy
- judgment
- prioritization
- communication
- speed
- commercial awareness
- coachability
- process discipline

Define what a passing score means before interviews start.

### Sales-role roleplay example
Stage 1: script read
- clarity
- pace
- confidence
- ability to sound natural

Stage 2: live prospect roleplay
- discovery
- objection handling
- listening
- control of conversation
- CTA / next-step clarity

Stage 3: coaching loop
- give 2–3 specific corrections
- repeat the scene
- score how quickly the candidate adapts

The coaching loop is often more informative than the first attempt.

## 4. Probation framework

Probation should answer one question: can this person reliably perform the actual role?

Example structure:

### Week 1
- access complete
- SOPs reviewed
- shadowing complete
- first supervised output delivered

### Day 30
- performs core tasks with moderate oversight
- meets quality threshold
- uses required systems correctly
- surfaces blockers without waiting too long

### Day 60
- owns recurring workload with limited oversight
- meets most KPI targets or shows credible trajectory
- handles normal edge cases

### Day 90 / end of probation
- consistently owns the role's primary outcome
- quality and cadence are sustainable
- manager intervention is appropriate rather than constant

Do not wait until the final probation day to tell someone they are failing. Use regular checkpoints.

## 5. Performance diagnosis

When results are weak, test these causes:

1. **Clarity** — were expectations explicit?
2. **Skill** — can the person perform the task?
3. **Training** — were they taught the current process?
4. **Tools** — do systems block performance?
5. **Capacity** — is workload realistic?
6. **Prioritization** — are they spending time on the right work?
7. **Ownership** — do they follow through without unnecessary escalation?
8. **Role fit** — is this the right person for the actual job?
9. **Management** — are feedback and decisions happening quickly enough?

Create corrective plans with:
- one issue statement;
- 2–4 measurable expectations;
- support/resources provided;
- review dates;
- consequence if expectations are not met.

## 6. Hiring metrics

Track only metrics useful for decisions:
- applicants by source
- qualified-screen rate
- work-test pass rate
- interview-to-offer rate
- offer acceptance rate
- time to fill
- probation pass rate
- 90-day retention
- manager satisfaction / role-output attainment

For recurring high-volume roles, maintain a candidate database and a repeatable screening workflow rather than restarting from zero each time.
