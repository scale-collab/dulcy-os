## Rules for all business content
1. Identify destination, existing project, source file, latest edit. Unnamed OBS recordings: match by the opening/subject, caption text and a stable file ID; recording date only corroborates.
2. Keep original media and approved assets. Make a review version when revising. Replace a scheduled asset only after checking exactly what is queued and what Alex authorised.
3. Cut for clarity and rhythm; let the last thought land. Remove black tails, cut-off phrases, unrelated inserts, QR/end cards.
4. Watch and listen to the output: cuts, speech/music levels, crop continuity, captions, timing, clutter, letterboxing, ends, sensitive info in screen shares. Flag glaring contradictions, unsupported guarantees, wrong numbers. If he says a wrong number, don't silently fix only the caption: flag it.
5. Check the **exact final render** that will go out (not the editor preview, another version under the same clip ID, or the other channel's export). Sample beginning, middle, end and every layout change; play transitions and conclusions in real time. Never call a spot check a full watch.
6. Report the real stage in plain words: rough cut, edited, final export checked, in Frame.io, synced in Airtable, thumbnail ready/approved, uploaded, scheduled, published. One does not prove the next.

## Thumbnails and packaging
- Real 16:9 (1280×720 standard). No black bars, clipped letters, unreadable text, trailing punctuation or headlines cut mid-thought. Alex's approved portrait/reference direction; one readable idea, short complete headline. Check it at desktop and small size.
- Changes: "Request thumbnail changes" beside the approval control, with the exact short headline and what to keep. Review the new image, not the old one still showing while it renders.
- Titles, descriptions, chapters, CTAs come from the actual video; verify links and chapter times against the uploaded cut. Client evidence approved or anonymised. Thumbnail approval and publish approval are separate.

## Post-edit SOP: OpusClip → Frame.io → Airtable Content Center
1. Edit in OpusClip (DaVinci is in the toolkit, but Main uses OpusClip's visuals; OBS is a recording source; no Picsart).
2. Find the exact Content Center item (check title, content, source identity, channel Main vs Raw) before any record action; create a new one only after checking.
3. Use that item's Frame.io setup; upload the latest correct full-length export to its private **Final** folder; press the record's **Sync Frame.io**; verify the final asset link and the real next stage. Never fake a status.
4. Thumbnail: generate/locate on the same record, inspect the exact image, get Alex's approval.
5. YouTube: managed workflow and exact channel; verify upload, processing, thumbnail, visibility, time zone, date and URL. A planned date in Airtable is not a YouTube schedule.
6. Repurpose from the final cut in the separate short-clips project. Project complete but 0 clips in Airtable → use "Check clip status", don't resubmit. After editing a short: "Refresh current preview", confirm the final render, review/approve it, then generate and review platform drafts; schedule only as authorised.
7. After scheduling: match project, clip ID and approved render to the channel/account, platform, caption, date, time and receipt. Dedupe jobs. Instagram and YouTube are separate.

## Shorts and distribution

- Minimum OpusClip score 85 (Content Automation Settings). **Clips Wanted** = the shortlist count, not what OpusClip generates (often 20-30); check the record (Alex has discussed 5 vs 10).
- Reels and Shorts run through the OpusClip / Content Center workflow. LinkedIn, X, Reddit: native versions when asked; follow each account's rules; never repost a Reddit draft to another subreddit without approval.
- Cadence (last clarified): **2 Main + 4 Raw long-form a week** (Raw normal range 4-5). Recording targets, YouTube slots and short cadence are separate.

## Voice and topics
- Founder/operator voice: a person showing a decision and what happened, a usable lesson. Dulcy company assets: a team explaining what it learned, no generic corporate tone, no pitch in every piece.
- Topics: cold email targeting and offers, deliverability, reply handling and booked meetings, agency ops and QA, proof and experiments, founder decisions, useful AI and GTM workflows. Real examples only; no fake proof, guarantees or confidential client info.
