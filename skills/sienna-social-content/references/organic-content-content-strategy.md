## Editorial principles

1. Build content from real operating material first: client work, campaign results, sales conversations, internal systems, tests, and founder decisions.
2. Use proof selectively. Dulcy's business positioning is a full-cycle outbound engine: targeting, offer, infrastructure, reply conversion, and booked meetings. Social content should make those systems understandable and credible.
3. Treat social proof as a trust layer for outbound as well as an inbound channel. A prospect who receives an email may check the founder or company profile before replying.
4. Make each piece about one main idea. Dense lists can become carousels or threads, but a standard post should not carry five unrelated lessons.
5. Repurpose long-form material aggressively. One useful meeting, interview, Loom, or podcast can create many atomic posts.
6. Create demand through education and point of view; do not make every post a direct pitch.

## Content source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:
1. Direct founder statement or firsthand experience
2. Dulcy operational data and internal process
3. Client-approved proof/case studies
4. Sales/client calls and recurring objections
5. Internal SOPs/frameworks
6. External research when requested or necessary

Never turn an unverified internal idea into a factual market claim.

## Content idea formula

Strong content often combines:
- a specific audience
- a recognizable problem
- an unexpected observation or decision
- a mechanism/explanation
- proof or example
- a useful takeaway

Example pattern:
"We stopped judging lead lists by job title alone. A VP at a 20-person company can be a buyer; the same title at 5,000 employees may be irrelevant. Now we score company fit and contact fit separately before paying to verify the list."

## Repurposing map

One source can become:
- tactical lesson
- contrarian opinion
- checklist
- case-study breakdown
- mistake / lesson
- before / after
- short video
- carousel
- Q&A post
- X thread
- founder story

Do not paraphrase the same post ten times. Change the angle or format.

## Approval states

Use simple states when managing a content pipeline:
- Idea
- Draft
- Review
- Approved
- Scheduled
- Published
- Repurpose

If a posting connector is added later, use record IDs/post IDs as durable references and have Airtable operations own automation reliability.
