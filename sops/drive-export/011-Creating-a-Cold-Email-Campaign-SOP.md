How to create a cold email campaign from scratch—-----------------------------------------------------
1. Understanding the Foundation of a Cold Email Campaign
Before writing any email, two critical factors determine the success of a cold email campaign:
✅ Accurate Lead List – Ensuring your offer is relevant to the right people.
✅ Strong Deliverability – Ensuring your emails actually land in inboxes.
However, conversion comes down to how well you position the offer. Your copywriting will determine if leads respond positively.
2. Understanding the Demand Capture vs. Demand Generation
Every offer exists somewhere between Demand Capture and Demand Generation:
✅ Demand Capture Offer → Prospects are already aware of the solution and actively looking for it.
✅ Demand Generation Offer → Prospects don’t know they need it yet, so you must create demand.
Example Breakdown:
📌 Demand Capture Offers (Easy to sell, competitive)
        •        Websites
        •        IT services
        •        Insurance
📌 Demand Generation Offers (Harder to sell, but high ticket)
        •        Cold calling
        •        Cold email
        •        FB/Google/TikTok ads
Positioning for Cold Outreach Success
If you are selling a Demand Capture Offer using a Demand Generation Mechanism (Cold Email, Cold Calling, etc.), you must focus on three key factors:
1. High Volume Outreach
Since prospects only buy when they need it, you must reach a huge number of contacts to find the ones already in-market. This means running broad campaigns at scale.
2. Unique Positioning
If you can’t out-volume the competition, you need to stand out by positioning your offer differently.
Example: Instead of pitching “We build websites,” position it as:
🚀 “We build revenue-driven websites that increase conversion rates by 42%.”
3. Targeting Demand Capture Indicators
If possible, use triggers that indicate a business might need your service right now.
        •        Selling IT security services? Target companies that recently had a security breach.
        •        Selling web design? Look for companies running paid ads but have an outdated site.
        •        Selling insurance? Find businesses hiring rapidly (indicating growth and risk management needs).
How This Translates to Cold Email Campaigns
Your cold emails must first sell the need for your service before selling the service itself.
For Demand Capture Offers, the approach is simple:
❌ “Hey, do you need a website?” (Generic, low response)
✅ “Noticed {COMPANY} is running paid ads, but your landing page is leaking conversions. A quick fix could add 15% more revenue. Want me to show you how?” (Specific, problem-aware)
For Demand Generation Offers, the goal is education & trust:
❌ “We do cold email lead gen, want leads?”
✅ “Could I send over 1,000 free leads just like {CASE STUDY}? It will include name, email, phone number, linkedin, company and everything you need to book them.
I’ll show you how to do it + build it out live.”
Final Takeaway
If you’re selling Demand Capture Offers using Cold Email, you must:
🔹 Do high volume outreach to find in-market buyers.
🔹 Position your offer uniquely to stand out.
🔹 Use demand triggers to target prospects at the right time.
🔹 Educate first, then pitch the service.
This framework will maximize conversions and prevent your emails from blending in with the hundreds of other generic pitches prospects receive daily.
3. Creating a Compelling Offer
Mindset Shift: Sell the Outcome, Not the Service
🔴 Bad Example (Service-Focused):
        •        “We do SEO, copywriting, and blog writing for SaaS companies.”
        •        “We create Facebook Ads to grow your eCommerce store.”
🟢 Good Example (Outcome-Focused):
        •        “We add $40K per month in new revenue to SaaS companies with SEO.”
        •        “We help eCom brands add 20% more sales through Facebook Ads.”
People buy results, not services. Position the offer around what they actually care about: Revenue, Growth, Profitability, Market Share.

4. Crafting Your Cold Email Campaign Offer
How to Structure an Offer in a Cold Email Campaign:
✅ Clear Value Proposition: What specific pain point are you solving?
✅ Outcome + Timeframe: What result will they see, and how soon?
✅ Risk Reversal (Optional): Can you offer a guarantee/risk reversal to reduce hesitation?
✅ Social Proof: Has this worked for others in their industry?
🔹 Bad Offer:
“We help businesses improve their email marketing.”
🔹 Good Offer:
“We help B2B companies recover lost deals and increase revenue by 35% through personalized email follow-ups.”
5. Types of Cold Email Campaigns
Depending on your target audience and offer, you’ll use different campaign types:
A few options are:
* Sniper Campaigns
This campaign targets a specific job title within a specific industry, addressing a specific pain point.
* Shogun Campaigns
This campaign targets a specific industry, addressing a specific pain point on a company level.
* Personalized Campaigns
This campaign is a hyper-personalized campaign leveraging AI that get’s data from either a job title or company level.
* Broad Campaigns
This campaign is a simple campaign with no flashy personalization. This is typically to test an offer and see if it has PMF.
* Broad Campaigns (tiny personalization)
This campaign is a simple campaign with a little personalization. This is typically to test an offer and see if it has PMF VERY FAST.
When Do I Use Each Campaign?
Each campaign is used for different offers or scenarios. Depending on what you or your client is selling, you use these campaigns to increase the results from launched campaigns.

7. Personalization & Spintax (To Increase Deliverability)
To improve deliverability and avoid spam filters, always use Spintax and personalization:
❌ Bad Subject Line:
“Hey {FIRST_NAME}, wanted to connect.”
✅ Good Subject Line (Using Spintax):
{Quick one {FIRST_NAME}?|Thoughts on this?|What happened?|Hey {FIRST_NAME}, real quick}
Use Spintax in the email body too:
❌ Bad CTA (No Variability):
“Would you be interested in discussing?”
✅ Good CTA (With Spintax):
{Would you be open to a quick chat?|Is this something worth exploring?|Worth a chat?}

8. A/B Testing for Campaign Optimization
Never assume your first email is the best—it’s crucial to ALWAYS BE A/B testing different angles. A good A/B test should be 1-2k leads per variation.
✅ How to A/B Test a Cold Email:
        •        Test CTAs (e.g., “Worth a chat?” vs. “Mind if I send more info?”)
        •        Test subject lines (e.g., “Quick question” vs. “Our Outbound”)
        •        Test first lines (e.g., “Saw your LinkedIn post on X” vs. “Noticed {COMPANY} is hiring for X”)
        •        Test Personalization  (e.g., “I noticed {COMPANY} is being outranked by {COMPETITOR1} and {COMPETITOR 2} for key search terms?” vs. “I checked {COMPANY}’s Google Page Speed analytics, and your results came back at {STAT}/100.”)
        •        Test P.s. Lines
Example A/B Test for a CTA:
📩 Email A:
“Would it be okay if I send over 1,000 free leads for you to check out?”
📩 Email B:
“Could I build a list of 1,000 free leads for {COMPANY}. It will include their email, name, linkedin and everything else you need to book them in”
📊 Whichever gets a higher positive reply rate → use that for the campaign.

10. Common Cold Email Mistakes to Avoid
🚫 Mistake 1: Asking for a Call Too Early
✅ Instead: Offer a valuable insight first.
🚫 Mistake 2: Writing Like a Corporate Robot
✅ Instead: Keep it conversational and direct.
🚫 Mistake 3: No Personalization
✅ Instead: Always mention something specific about the recipient or their company.
🚫 Mistake 4: Weak Subject Lines
✅ Instead: Make them curiosity-driven & relevant.
🚫 Mistake 5: Low value or marketing lingo
✅ Instead: Make them high-value, unique & natural.
11. Spam Content Checker
Before launching your campaign, always run the spintaxxed copy through an email spam checker (e.g., EmailGuard). Even seemingly harmless words can trigger spam filters, hurting your deliverability.
https://app.emailguard.io/dashboard
Steps to Check for Spam Words in Your Cold Email Copy:
1️⃣ Copy and paste your spintaxxed email into an email spam checker (e.g., EmailGuard).
2️⃣ Review flagged spam words (common examples: “free,” “guarantee,” “money,” “urgent,” “act now”).
3️⃣ Copy and paste the flagged words into your spintax tool and ask it to remove or rewrite them.
4️⃣ Ensure the new copy still makes sense and keeps the same value & relevance for the prospect.
5️⃣ Run a final check in the spam tool to confirm no issues before launching.
🚀 Best Practice:
✅ Keep language natural & conversational—avoid “salesy” or “hype” words.
✅ Double-check subject lines—these get flagged easily.
✅ Always test with multiple variations to improve inbox placement.
🔍 Why This Matters?
A high spam score will tank deliverability and land your emails in spam or promotions instead of the inbox. Better inboxing = more replies.
