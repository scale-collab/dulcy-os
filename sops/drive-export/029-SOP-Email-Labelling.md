﻿📩 SOP: Drafting & Answering Emails as an Executive Assistant
📌 Purpose
To ensure the Executive Assistant (EA) manages, drafts, and replies to emails on behalf of the Executive in a timely, accurate, professional, and strategic manner that reflects the executive’s voice and priorities.
________________


🎯 Objectives
* Maintain a professional and consistent tone that aligns with the executive’s communication style.

* Save the executive time by pre-drafting, triaging, and responding to appropriate emails.

* Ensure nothing urgent or important is missed.

* Uphold confidentiality and accuracy in all communications.

________________


🧰 Tools Required
   * Gmail/Outlook (or applicable email client)

   * Calendar (Google Calendar, Outlook Calendar, etc.)

   * Slack / Teams / Internal communication tool

   * CRM (e.g., HubSpot, Salesforce – if applicable)

   * Notion, Evernote, or other note-taking tool

   * Grammarly or another writing assistant (optional)

________________


🧾 Step-by-Step Process
________________


1. Inbox Triage (3–5x Daily)
Time to complete: 10–15 min per check-in
When: Morning (9AM), Midday (1PM), Late Afternoon (4PM)
Goal: Prioritize what requires the executive's attention vs what you can handle.
✅ Actions:
      1. Label, Flag, or Folder Emails as:

         * 🔴 Urgent (Needs executive input ASAP)

         * 🟡 Review (Can be drafted/replied to with EA oversight)

         * 🟢 Actioned by EA (You can handle and respond)

         * 🔵 For Information Only (FYI for the executive)

         * ⚫ Spam/Unimportant (Filter out)

            2. Check for meetings, requests, deadlines, VIPs, press/PR, and internal team escalations.

2. Drafting Emails on Behalf of the Executive
Tone should always reflect the executive’s voice. Use past emails as tone references.
✍️ Drafting Process:
               1. Read & Understand the incoming message in full.

               2. Determine Type of Reply:

                  * Confirmations

                  * Delegations

                  * Follow-ups

                  * Scheduling

                  * Approvals

                  * Rejections

                  * Strategic relationships

                     3. Compose the Draft using this format:

Hi [Recipient Name],


[Concise acknowledgment or reference to the original message]


[Main content — answering question, providing input, pushing for a next step, etc.]


[CTA or wrap-up, if necessary]


Best,  
[Executive’s Full Name or Initials — depending on style]


                        4. Use Executive’s Signature. Always maintain professionalism.

                        5. Run spell-check and grammar check before sending.

                        6. Save in Drafts for approval, or Send directly if previously authorized.









📌 Common Phrases to Use:
                           * “Circling back on this…”

                           * “Let me know your availability…”

                           * “We appreciate your patience while we review this.”

                           * “We’d love to explore this further with you — does [Day] work for a quick touch base?”

                           * “Thanks for reaching out. I’ll pass this to the team and loop back shortly.”

________________


3. Responding to Emails as the Executive
💼 When You Can Respond As the Executive:
                              * Calendar requests

                              * Follow-ups

                              * Thank-you notes

                              * Intro emails

                              * General inquiries

                              * Confirming details

🛑 When to Escalate to Executive:
                                 * Legal, HR, or contractual topics

                                 * Pricing/Negotiations

                                 * Strategic partnerships

                                 * Conflict or complaints

                                 * Press/Investor/Board communications



4. Scheduling Requests
When an email involves a meeting request:
✅ Steps:
                                    1. Check the executive's calendar.

                                    2. Offer two time slots based on availability.

                                    3. Draft response like:

Hi [Name],


Thanks for reaching out. [Executive's Name] is available at [Time Option 1] or [Time Option 2]. Please let us know which works best for you.


Looking forward to your response.


Best,  
[Your Name] (on behalf of [Executive's Name])


                                       4. Once confirmed, create the calendar invite with:

                                          * Zoom/Google Meet link

                                          * Agenda (if available)

                                          * Location/Instructions

                                          * Invite all attendees









5. Follow-Up Protocol
Use a follow-up system (e.g. Boomerang, follow-up labels, reminders).
⏱️ Timeline:
                                             * Internal/team follow-ups: 24–48 hrs

                                             * External/business critical: 48–72 hrs

                                             * General inquiries: 3–5 business days

6. Weekly Executive Email Review
Every Friday (or preferred day), send a Weekly Email Digest to the executive.
📋 Include:
                                                * Emails awaiting reply or review

                                                * Important threads to monitor

                                                * Scheduled follow-ups

                                                * Contacts that reached out (investors, media, high-priority)

________________


7. Confidentiality & Security
                                                   * Do not forward executive emails externally without permission.

                                                   * Always use official business accounts.

                                                   * Log out of shared/public computers.

                                                   * Use strong, unique passwords + 2FA on all email accounts.

________________

                                                      * Email examples to emulate tone and style

✅ Final Checklist Before Hitting Send
                                                         * Tone matches the executive

                                                         * All names and companies spelled correctly

                                                         * Message is clear and free of errors

                                                         * Attachments/links included (if referenced)

                                                         * Email tagged/archived appropriately

                                                         * Calendar invite sent (if needed)

🧾 Objective
To ensure timely, professional, and accurate communication on behalf of the executive. This SOP outlines the process for managing, drafting, replying, and organizing emails in the executive’s inbox.
________________


🧩 Scope
This SOP applies to all internal and external email communications received or sent from the executive's official email account or behalf, excluding personal or sensitive legal correspondence.
________________


🧑‍💼 Responsibility
The Executive Assistant is responsible for:
                                                            * Monitoring the inbox daily

                                                            * Drafting and sending emails where appropriate

                                                            * Flagging or escalating messages that require executive input

                                                            * Maintaining a clean and organized inbox

________________


🔁 Daily Email Workflow
✅ 1. Inbox Monitoring
                                                               * Check the inbox every 30–60 minutes during working hours

                                                               * Enable desktop/mobile notifications for real-time alerts

                                                               * Prioritize emails using the Labeling System (see below)

✍️ 3. Drafting Emails
🧠 Key Principles:
                                                                  * Write in the executive’s voice: Professional, confident, and direct.

                                                                  * Keep it brief and purposeful.

                                                                  * Always propose next steps or actions.

                                                                  * Avoid jargon or passive voice.

🧾 Draft Structure:
Subject: [Clear & specific subject line]


Hi [Name],


[Brief context or reference to their previous message or meeting]


[Main message body: Answer their question, provide context, offer value]


[Call to action with a proposed time, deliverable, or solution]


Best,  
[Executive's name] (written by EA)


________________


📨 4. Replying to Emails
Guidelines:
                                                                     * Respond within 1 hour for time-sensitive emails; same business day for others.

                                                                     * Use templates only when appropriate — always tailor language to the conversation.

                                                                     * Keep tone consistent with the executive’s personality (e.g., formal vs. casual).

                                                                     * Never commit the executive to decisions unless pre-approved.

                                                                     * CC executive only when necessary.

________________


🔁 5. Follow-up Protocols
                                                                        * For unanswered important threads: Follow up every 3–4 business days.

                                                                        * Use creative and context-aware language (avoid: "just bumping this up").

                                                                        * Track follow-ups in [Airtable/CRM/Spreadsheet] for visibility.

________________


📥 6. Escalation Rules
Escalate immediately if:
                                                                           * Email involves legal, financial, or HR risks

                                                                           * CEO or board-level communication is involved

                                                                           * Executive was directly requested for action/input

Use Slack/Signal/Phone for high-priority pings.
________________


📁 7. Filing and Archiving
                                                                              * Move fully resolved threads to labeled folders (e.g., Archived > Completed Tasks)

                                                                              * Maintain folders for:

                                                                                 * Receipts

                                                                                 * Invoices

                                                                                 * Meetings & Notes

                                                                                 * Clients

                                                                                 * Internal Comms

Weekly inbox audit to ensure no tasks are missed.
________________


🛑 8. What NOT to Do
                                                                                    * Never send emails without proofreading twice.

                                                                                    * Avoid using passive or uncertain language (e.g., “just checking in”).

                                                                                    * Never share pricing, contracts, or sensitive documents unless pre-approved.

                                                                                    * Don’t reply to spam, phishing, or unknown senders without review.
