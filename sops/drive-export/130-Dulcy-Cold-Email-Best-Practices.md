﻿Cold Email Best Practices
(as of 1/4/2026)


So long as you leave warmup enabled, and leave the settings as they are you’ll have no issues.


Warmup settings


Total warmup emails per day = 5
Miscellaneous settings:


* Daily ramp up = 2
* Reply rate = 30%
* Randomise = 2 - 5 (if the platform supports it)
* Scale to 1:1 after 4 weeks, 2:1 after 6


Sending settings:
* Max 10 - 15 emails per day
* After warm up, start with 5 per day cold, 1 week later 10 per day, and monitor from there. If positive reply is 15%+ consistently move to 15.
* 61 minute time gap


After the inboxes are uploaded to your sending platform, let them warmup for at least 14 days, and up to 4 weeks for best results before launching any campaigns.


Campaign settings and setup (IMPORTANT):


If just one of the following configurations aren’t followed you’re at a great risk of burning your infra and/or domain, so follow them to a tee.


Lead verification:


ALWAYS verify your leads. We recommend MillionVerifier as they’re extremely reliable and get you the best bang for your buck.


Only use “Good” leads .
NEVER send to “Bad” or “Risky” leads.


There are some verifiers that can verify “Risky” leads, we recommend Scrubby/Lead Magic although they’re expensive.


Campaign setup:


DONT’S:


Use open tracking, as it will attach an invisible image to every single email that goes out, which has 100% chance of triggering spam filters.(HTML Code)


Include links in copy.


MUST DO’S


Use spintax. Every 2nd or third word/phrase must be spintaxed, especially the signature. (ex. {Hey|Hi|Hello})


Use this GPT Spintax Generator and https://sp1n.me/index.html for spintaxing.


Additional considerations:


Words/characters that trigger spam filters:


* Dollar signs ‘$’
* Essentially anything that references money.
* Use spam word checker to verify copy


Sending to Outlook/Google inboxes:


For the past few months Outlook has imposed extremely strict, AI spam filters. So strict even internal Microsoft emails go to spam. They will lower the spam filters soon, but for now you must take extra consideration.


You’ll have no issue hitting the inbox for other email host’s, except Outlook which takes some extra consideration.


It’s best to separate your lead lists and campaigns by ESP’s. Meaning, one list/campaign should be all outlook leads and the other list/campaign is everything else (Google, Zoho, etc.)


The easiest way to do this is to use the email host lookup in Emailguard (it’s free).


For campaigns that have no outlook leads, send as you normally would.


For campaigns that are entirely Outlook, you’re email copy should fit this criteria to maximise your chance of hitting the inbox:


* 1 line MAX.
* Ideally 25-30 words or less, 50 max.
* Use as little spam words as possible.
* Make the email sound as casual as possible, and as far from a “pitch” as possible.


For Gmail or other accounts


* 3-4 Lines MAX
* <75 Words
* Close to 0 spam words as possible
* Make it sound casual
