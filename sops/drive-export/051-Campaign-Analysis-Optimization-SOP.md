﻿Campaign Analysis & Optimization SOP


Purpose:
This SOP ensures that campaigns are continuously monitored, analyzed, and optimized for better performance by reviewing key metrics, responses, and adjusting messaging, targeting, and strategy accordingly.


✅ Regularly analyze campaign performance based on replies, open rates, bounce rate, positive responses, meetings booked
✅ Identify patterns in objections and adjust messaging accordingly
✅ A/B test scripts, subject lines, CTAs, and targeting to improve conversions
✅ Monitor deliverability and ensure emails are reaching inboxes
✅ Continuously refine targeting and segmentation to improve results

Step 1: Reviewing Campaign Performance
Before making any adjustments, you need to analyze key performance indicators (KPIs) to see what’s working and what’s not.
Please refer to the email reply sequence for troubleshooting common issues: Lucidchart
Key Metrics to Track:
✅ Positive Reply Rate – How many people are responding with interest?
✅ Reply Rate – The total percentage of responses received (both positive & negative)
✅ Bounce Rate – If it’s above 2.5%, email health needs to be reviewed
✅ Open Rate (if tracking is enabled) – This is less important but can give insight into subject line effectiveness
✅ Meeting Booked Rate – How many positive replies convert into scheduled calls?
✅ Response Patterns – What are prospects saying? Are there common objections?
Where to Check These Metrics:
        •        Google Sheet Report (automated weekly)
        •        Email Bison Dashboard (or your sending platform)
        •        Inbox Manager (for qualitative insights on replies)
✅ Example Analysis:
                •        Reply rate: 3% (good)
        •        Positive replies: 12% (average, but needs improvement)
        •        Bounce rate: 3.1% (too high, check deliverability)
        •        Common objections: “Not the right time” and “We already have this covered”
Step 2: Analyzing Responses & Adjusting Messaging
1. Categorize Replies to Identify Patterns
Go through responses and categorize them into:
✅ Positive Replies – Interested, asking for more info, or booking a call
✅ Soft Objections – Interested but has concerns (e.g., timing, budget, relevance)
✅ Hard Objections – Not interested at all, already have a solution, or don’t see value
✅ No Response – No engagement after multiple touchpoints
2. Addressing Objections in Your Copy
Based on the trends in responses, adjust the messaging. Example:
Objection
	Optimization Strategy
	“Not the right time”
	Add urgency or offer a soft next step (e.g., free resource)
	“We already have this covered”
	Refine messaging to differentiate from competitors
	“Not interested”
	Test new angles or industry-specific pain points
	No response
	Adjust subject lines, first lines, or personalize more
	✅ Example Optimization:
If you see many replies saying, “We already have an internal team for this,” tweak your messaging to highlight a unique selling point or an angle that shows how you complement their current team instead of replacing them.
Step 3: A/B Testing for Optimization
A/B testing (split testing) helps refine what works best in your emails.
1. What to A/B Test:
✅ Subject lines – Test different hooks or personalization styles
✅ First sentence – Vary opening lines to see what grabs attention
✅ Call-to-action (CTA) – Compare direct (“Want to book a call?”) vs. soft (“Would you be open to exploring this?”)
✅ Offer Positioning – Test different ways of framing the value proposition
2. How to Run an A/B Test:
        •        Create two versions of your email
        •        Send each version to an equal sample size
        •        Track which version gets more positive replies
        •        Apply winning variations to the rest of the campaign
✅ Example A/B Test:
Version A
	Version B
	Subject Line: “Quick question, {{First Name}}”
	Subject Line: “Should we chat, {{First Name}}?”
	First Line: “Noticed you handle growth at {{Company}}, wanted to share something that might help…”
	First Line: “Curious—how are you currently handling {{pain point}}?”
	*Note: Never forget when you A/B test something that should be the only variable that is different.
✅ Results: If Version B gets more replies, roll out that structure to the entire campaign.
Step 4: Optimizing Deliverability
1. Check Bounce Rate
        •        If bounce rate is above 2.5%, review email verification process
        •        Use Clay.com, Apollo.io, and other verification tools to revalidate emails before sending
2. Test Inbox Placement
        •        Run Inbox Placement Tests (send test emails to see if they land in inbox or spam)
        •        If emails land in spam, reduce sending volume & quarantine emails properly
3. Copy & Email Accounts
        •        Ensure your email scripts contain as little spam words as possible
        •        If an email account drops below 60% health, quarantine it and set the sending to 1 per day until it heals after a couple days
Step 5: Refining Targeting & Segmentation
Sometimes, bad targeting = bad results no matter how good the copy is.
1. Review Lead List & Criteria
        •        Are the leads truly within the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)?
        •        Do they fit the right industry, job title, company size, etc.?
        •        Are they decision-makers or just lower-level employees?
2. Use Clay.com for High-Intent Signals
        •        Look for companies already using relevant tech (e.g., BuiltWith for Shopify stores)
        •        Target companies hiring for relevant roles (e.g., hiring SDRs = likely need more leads)
        •        Filter by recent funding rounds to find companies with fresh budgets
Step 6: Scaling Winning Campaigns
Once a campaign is performing well, scale it while maintaining quality.
✅ Increase volume gradually – Don’t go from 100 to 5,000 emails overnight
✅ Expand targeting – Once you validate an angle, apply it to similar industries
✅ Tweak for optimized performance – Continue to test what works. For example if the DEMO CTA worked the best then A/B test the wording of it:
* Would you be open to a 5m demo?
* Up for a short demo?
* How about a quick demo?
✅ Example Scaling Strategy:
( if it is a new client )
        •        Week 1-2: 500 emails/day → 5,000 emails total
        •        Week 3-4: 1,000 emails/day if deliverability stays strong
        •        Week 5+: 1,500 emails/day if deliverability stays strong
–
( if it is an old client )
        •        Week 1-2: 1,500 emails/day minimum
        •        Week 3-4: 2,000 emails/day if positive reply rate stays strong
        •        Week 5+: 2,500 emails/day if positive reply rate stays strong
Step 7: Communicating Campaign Insights & Adjustments
Keep the internal team and clients updated on what’s working and what’s being adjusted.
✅ Weekly Internal Update:
        •        Summary of what’s working & what’s not
        •        Changes made to copy, targeting, or deliverability fixes
        •        Any major trends or opportunities spotted
✅ Friday Client Check-In:
        •        Summarize results in Google Sheet (already automated)
        •        Send a short video breakdown covering trends & next steps
        •        Slack message updates on any adjustments being made
Final Notes
🔹 Don’t just track numbers—analyze responses. The best optimizations come from reading actual replies.
🔹 Keep testing. Small tweaks in messaging, targeting, and positioning can drastically improve results.
🔹 Ensure high email health. Poor deliverability kills even the best campaigns.
🔹 Adapt based on data. If something isn’t working, pivot and test a new approach.






Campaign Analysis & Optimization: VisionairAI Case Study
Overview
This campaign analysis breaks down the process of testing and refining outreach for VisionairAI, a new software company searching for its niche. Through extensive A/B testing and iterative improvements, we identified a high-performing market segment and optimized messaging to maximize conversions.
1. Initial Testing & Market Exploration
When VisionairAI started working with us in June/July 2023, they had no clear niche. To determine product-market fit, we tested campaigns across different industries:
✅ Industries Tested:
        •        Supplement Brands
        •        Real Estate Agents
        •        Property Managers
        •        Specific Realtor Groups
        •        Recruiting Firms (early positive signals)
2. Initial Campaign & Low Performance
The original script targeted recruiters with a broad, benefit-driven pitch:
Original Script:
        Hi Mark,
        I simplify repetitive outreach for recruiting companies, allowing them to reduce wasted time and drive consistent growth with our Visionary AI Employee.
        For instance, we recently helped the Johnson Group to optimize their processes, reducing their outbound team from 18 to just 2, while still scheduling 25-50 meetings per week—all made possible by our AI technology.
        Care to try calling it?
🚨 Issues Identified:
        •        Too vague: “Simplify repetitive outreach” isn’t specific enough.
        •        Weak personalization: No direct mention of the recipient’s pain points.
        •        Lack of strong CTA: “Care to try calling it?” is weak and non-urgent.
This campaign did not yield significant results in other tested industries. However, recruiting firms showed early positive signals, leading us to refine our approach.
3. Campaign Refinement & Breakthrough Results
We adjusted the messaging to focus directly on the pain points of recruiters by making it hyper-personalized and results-driven.
Optimized Script:
        Hi Ryan, as VP of Marketing & Sales Operations at Temp Experts, I noticed you’re still manually screening hundreds of candidates.
        I designed a software that acts & sounds like a human recruiter—finding qualified candidates and booking interviews 24/7.
        Would you be interested in testing it out?
✅ Key Improvements:
        •        Hyper-personalization: “Hi Ryan, as VP of Marketing & Sales Operations at Temp Experts…” (directly addressing role + company name).
        •        Pain-point focused: Directly calls out “manually screening hundreds of candidates.”
        •        Stronger CTA: “Would you be interested in testing it out?” is more action-driven.
4. Performance Surge
📈 Results After Optimization:
        •        17.51% Interested Reply Rate (first improvement)
        •        24.04% Interested Reply Rate (after further refinements)
        •        11 positive replies in 24 hours (indicating strong product-market fit)
🚀 What This Means:
        •        The recruiting niche is the most responsive for VisionairAI.
        •        Positioning the AI tool as a 24/7 automated recruiter resonates strongly.
        •        Personalization + direct pain point focus + clear CTA = better results.
5. Lessons & Next Steps
Lessons Learned
        •        Finding a niche takes testing: Instead of assuming product-market fit, we let the data dictate where VisionairAI should focus.
        •        A/B testing is critical: A simple rewording of the email led to a 37%+ increase in response rates.
        •        Personalization & pain-point targeting win: Generic value propositions don’t convert—prospects need to see exactly how this solves their specific problems.
Next Steps
        1.        Double down on recruiting: Expand outreach within this segment.
        2.        Further refine messaging:
        •        Address objections from replies.
        •        Add social proof (e.g., “Other recruiting firms like Temp Experts have cut hiring time by 40%”).
        3.        Increase sending volume to scale the campaign while maintaining deliverability.
Conclusion
This case study on VisionairAI proves the power of market testing, iterative improvements, and targeted messaging. Through this approach, we transformed a low-performing campaign into one of our highest-converting campaigns to date.
This same process should be applied to all new clients & campaigns:
        •        Test multiple industries → Identify strong reply rates → Optimize messaging → Scale success.
