﻿Market Research SOP


Purpose:
This SOP outlines the steps required to conduct thorough research on a client before launching a campaign. Proper research ensures that we fully understand the client’s offer, their competitive advantage, and how to best position them within their target market. This process directly informs our campaign thesis, targeting strategy, and messaging framework.
Using all the information you learn here helps you towards the next step which would be: Creating a Cold Email Campaign SOP

Step 1: Understanding the Client and Their Offer
Before doing any outreach, you need to fully understand what our client sells, how they provide value, and what makes them different from competitors. This ensures we are positioning the offer in the most compelling way possible.
Key Questions to Answer:
        1.        What is the product or service?
        •        What exactly does the client sell?
        •        Is it a product, a service, or a hybrid?
        •        What are the deliverables they provide?
        2.        What problem does it solve?
        •        What is the main pain point it addresses?
        •        What happens if the target customer doesn’t use this solution?
        •        What is the alternative solution if they don’t buy from our client?
        3.        Who is the ideal customer? (ICP - Ideal Customer Profile)
        •        Industry: Which industries does this product/service serve?
        •        Company size: Are they targeting startups, SMBs, mid-market, or enterprise companies?
        •        Job titles: Who is the decision-maker? Who influences the buying decision?
        •        Common characteristics: What do their best clients have in common?
        4.        What makes our client different from competitors?
        •        What is their unique selling proposition (USP)?
        •        Do they offer better pricing, better service, better results, faster turnaround, a guarantee, etc.?
        •        Are they leveraging social proof (case studies, testimonials, results)?
Where to Find This Information:
        •        Client’s Website & Marketing Materials: Read their homepage, product/service pages, case studies, blog content, and FAQs.
        •        Sales Calls or Recordings: If available, listen to calls to hear how they position their service.
        •        Competitor Research: Identify competitors and see how they position themselves.
        •        Internal Documents: Review any materials provided by the client (pitch decks, presentations, etc.).
Step 2: Researching the Market & Target Audience
Once we understand what the client sells, we need to understand their target market and the pain points of their ideal customers.
Industry Research
        •        What are the major trends in the industry?
        •        Are there any current challenges that businesses in this industry face?
        •        Are there any regulatory changes or shifts affecting this market?
        •        Are there any seasonal factors that impact demand?
        •        Who are the biggest competitors in the space?
Tools to Use:
✅ Google News & Industry Reports
✅ LinkedIn Posts & Thought Leaders
✅ Reddit & Quora (to see what people are talking about)
✅ YouTube & Podcasts (to hear expert insights)
✅ Market Research Websites (Statista, IBISWorld, etc.)
Competitor Research
Understanding competitors helps us position our client in a way that makes them stand out.
        •        Who are their direct and indirect competitors?
        •        What services or products do competitors offer?
        •        What is their pricing model?
        •        How do they position their offer in marketing materials?
        •        Do they have case studies or testimonials that we can analyze?
        •        What objections are they addressing in their messaging?
Tools to Use:
✅ Google Search (“Top [industry] companies” or “Best [service] providers”)
✅ BuiltWith (to see competitor tech stacks)
✅ Competitor Websites & Sales Pages
✅ LinkedIn & Glassdoor (to see how they position themselves)
Target Audience Research (Decision-Makers & Pain Points)
We need to go beyond the surface and understand what really drives purchasing decisions.
        1.        Common Pain Points & Frustrations:
        •        What are their biggest challenges in their role?
        •        What are their goals & KPIs?
        •        What solutions have they tried before? Why didn’t they work?
        2.        Objections & Hesitations:
        •        Why might they say no to our client’s offer?
        •        What risks do they perceive?
        •        What kind of ROI do they expect to justify a purchase?
        3.        Language & Terminology:
        •        What industry-specific terms do they use?
        •        How do they talk about their problems? (This is crucial for writing email copy that resonates.)
Where to Find This Information:
✅ LinkedIn Posts & Comments (See what prospects talk about)
✅ Reddit & Quora (Look for common questions in their industry)
✅ Industry Blogs & Podcasts (Learn how experts discuss challenges)
✅ G2 & Capterra Reviews (See what they like/dislike about similar services)
✅ Twitter/X & Facebook Groups (Find discussions related to their problems)
Step 3: Structuring the Research for Cold Email Positioning
Once the research is done, we need to synthesize the information into something actionable for our cold email campaigns.
        1.        Summarize the Offer Clearly
        •        What does the client sell?
        •        Who is the ideal customer?
        •        What makes them different?
        2.        List the Top Pain Points & Frustrations of the Target Audience
        •        Example: If the client sells lead generation services for B2B companies, a pain point might be:
        •        “Sales reps waste hours chasing bad leads that never convert.”
        3.        Position the Offer to Address These Pain Points
        •        Example: Instead of saying: “We generate leads for B2B companies”
        •        Say: “Most B2B sales teams waste time on bad leads. We deliver pre-qualified, ready-to-buy prospects so they can focus on closing deals.”
        4.        Find Social Proof or Case Studies to Strengthen the Pitch
        •        Example: “We recently helped [Competitor] increase inbound leads by 300% in 90 days.”
Step 4: Translating Research into a Campaign Thesis
A campaign thesis is a one-sentence summary of what the campaign will focus on. This helps keep messaging clear and consistent.
Example Campaign Thesis:
        •        For an AI sales tool:
        •        “Most sales teams struggle with follow-ups. Our AI-powered tool automatically follows up on leads, closing 30% more deals with zero extra effort.”
        •        For an eCommerce agency:
        •        “We help eCommerce brands scale from $50K to $200K per month by fixing their broken ad funnels.”
        •        For a SaaS security company:
        •        “75% of companies don’t realize they have security vulnerabilities until it’s too late. Our software scans, detects, and fixes issues before hackers do.”
Step 5: Document Research & Prepare for Launch
Before launching the campaign, all research findings should be documented for reference.
1. Summarize Key Insights
   * Client’s unique positioning & differentiators.
   * Market trends & competitor insights.
   * Target audience breakdown.
2. Prepare Messaging Guidelines
   * Draft key value propositions.
   * Define the core angles for outreach.
   * Outline how objections should be handled in replies.
3. Review & Finalize
   * Confirm findings with the client if necessary.
   * Ensure alignment with the Creating a Cold Email Campaign SOP.
   * Proceed with campaign setup in Clay, Apollo, and Email Bison.

Final Checklist Before Moving to Campaign Setup
✅ Do you fully understand the client’s offer?
✅ Have you identified the right ICP (industry, company size, job titles)?
✅ Do you know the biggest pain points of the target audience?
✅ Do you have a clear positioning angle to address those pain points?
✅ Do you have competitor insights that help differentiate the client?
✅ Is the campaign thesis clear and specific?
Once all of these boxes are checked, you are ready to move on to writing the cold email sequences.
Conclusion
Market research is one of the most important steps before launching a cold email campaign. It ensures that we are reaching the right people, speaking their language, and addressing the problems they care about most. This increases reply rates, improves conversions, and makes our campaigns significantly more effective.
