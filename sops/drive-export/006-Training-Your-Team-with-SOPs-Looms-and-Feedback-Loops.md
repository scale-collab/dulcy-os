Training Your Team with SOPs, Looms, and Feedback Loops
Training your team with SOPs, Looms, and feedback loops is essential, even if it’s “just a VA role.”
Your people will only be as good as you train them to be.
Even if someone has all the expertise in the world, if you don’t give them the resources they need to succeed inside your business, it won’t work consistently. And if you don’t systematize training, you’ll keep repeating the same onboarding cycle hire after hire after hire, burning founder time every time someone doesn’t work out.
This ties directly back to the foundation module: you laid the groundwork early so that as you scale, everything “loops back” and runs smoother.
________________


Step 1: Ask How They Learn (During the Interview)
One of my go-to interview questions is:
“How do you learn best?”
I keep it open-ended so they don’t just pick what they think I want to hear. Once you know how they learn, you tailor training around that so they ramp faster and make fewer mistakes.
________________


Step 2: Build a Library So You Don’t Re-Train Every Hire
As I go through my business, I create guides for everything:
* every campaign type

* every tool/software

* every “if this, then that” scenario

* every repeated task that causes questions

Over time, that turns into a training library. In my case, it’s a collection of 120+ documents and 120+ Loom videos that covers basically every question someone could ask.
I update these about once per year (usually end of year), but the library itself builds continuously.
Why this matters:
At the beginning, it feels “nice” to onboard people live: welcome call, show them the tools, explain the vision, walk through the process.
But as you hire more people:
   * if a hire doesn’t work out, you lose 1–3 months of training time

   * you repeat the same explanations over and over

   * you become the bottleneck because everyone needs you to answer questions

A library fixes this.
________________


Step 3: My SOP + Loom Workflow
Here’s the exact system I use:
1) Record the Loom
I record Looms of me:
      * explaining the task

      * showing the screen

      * clicking through steps

      * demonstrating the process

      * explaining “why” (not just “what”)

2) Transcribe → Turn into SOP
Then I take the Loom transcript and turn it into a clean SOP using ChatGPT with a prompt I made.
This is the cheat code:
         * I don’t manually write 120 SOPs

         * I speak it once on Loom

         * the SOP becomes searchable and readable

         * now it lives forever in the library

3) Store it in the SOP library
Now every new hire gets the same training resources, delivered the same way, with no founder time needed after creation.
________________


Step 4: The “SOP First” Rule
If someone asks you a question in the morning and you’re busy, they shouldn’t need you to move forward.
So the rule is simple:
Check the SOP first. If it’s not there try to figure it out briefly, then ask.
This trains proactive behavior and stops you from becoming “human Google.”
(You still support your team but you don’t want to be forced to answer the same things daily.)
________________


Step 5: Feedback Loops Are the Real Training
SOPs teach the “how.”
Feedback loops teach the “standard.”
This is where people actually get good.
How I run feedback loops:
            * If you have fulfillment team / closers:

               * meet daily or weekly depending on role + volume

                  * I meet with my fulfillment team every single day

                  * I keep it tight: about 30 minutes

What we do in that 30 minutes:
                     * we go through a checklist per client:

                        * “Did we do X?”

                        * “Is Y done?”

                        * “Any blockers?”

                        * “Any fires?”

                           * we review work outputs:

                              * copy

                              * campaigns

                              * lead lists

                              * documents/presentations

                              * anything client-facing

The key:
When something isn’t great, I don’t just say “redo it.”
I say:
                                 * here’s why I don’t like it

                                 * here’s what I would write

                                 * here’s the standard going forward

That’s how they level up fast.

Step 6: Improvement Compounds Over Time
When you do this right:
                                    * the SOP library reduces questions

                                    * Looms reduce founder involvement

                                    * feedback loops raise quality

                                    * your team gets better week over week

                                    * and the business becomes less dependent on you
