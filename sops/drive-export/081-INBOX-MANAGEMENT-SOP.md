Inbox Management Rules:


✅MUST
- Follow up (forever, every 3 days)
- Provide value
- Reply within 5-10 minutes
- Push for the call
- Pitch times/dates instead of Calendly link
- Have clear next steps/CTA
- Double-check that all messages sent are relevant and related to the conversation.
- Triple-check that all messages sent are free of spelling errors.
- Be creative with your copy (don’t repeat the same message for every follow up)


❌ MUST NOT
- Send Calendly Link to prospect
- Thank them for the reply
- Ignore prospect question
- Ask multiple questions
- Use phatic talk e.g. "hope you had a nice weekend", "just bumping this up", "just following up". "Would love/like to"
- Give Price over email
- Give up with the follow ups until you get a clear "YES" or "NO"
- Explain the offer in depth

Understanding The Inbox Manager Setup:


We have an inbox manager setup within Airtable.


The inbox manager has 6 different sections in which a lead can move to:


1. New Replies
1. Every reply that comes in from our initial email that is not tagged as "interested", "not interested", or "out of office" yet.
2. Follow Ups
1. Every reply that comes in from our initial email that has been tagged as interested and has not responded yet to our response. Every 3 days that passes a yellow bar will light up on the left indicating to reach out to them.
3. Positive Replies
1. Every reply that comes in from our initial email that has been tagged by our Al tagger seems to be of interest.
4. Meeting Requested
1. This section stores every person who is interested in our service and/or looking to book in a meeting with us/our clients.
5. Won
1. This section stores every person who has booked a meeting with us or our clients.
6. Lost
1. This section stores every person who is not interested in our service or our clients service.


When a reply comes into "New Replies"/"Positive Replies" - there are 4 different tags you can give them after responding to their request:


1. Won

⁉️Other Objections


💡Here are some common ways your prospects want you OUT of their inbox without having the balls to say so - and how to turn them into your advantage.


Pricing/Cost?


Never tell the price over email - it's a trap. Something that has been working for us has been:


> Our services are custom solutions for each client we have, so it would be better to discuss a pricing structure tailored to your needs.


I really don't want to overquote you as well, so does ({time}) work for a short call to discuss?


> Happy to discuss pricing with you over a call so we don't waste both our time over email.


I also really don't want to overquote you as well, so does (time); work for a short call to discuss?


> Hey [Name], our pricing depends on a few factors, but here’s a general idea:


We typically have a monthly investment, which takes care of our entire service with no minimum commitment. We work with you to find a solution to what you want to achieve and get you the best results.


Let’s set up a brief 7-9 minute conversation to go over specifics. I can send over an invite, or if now isn’t the right time, no worries!


> Hey [Name], our pricing depends on a few factors, but here’s a general idea:


Our {{service/offer}} is custom-built and tailored to each of our clients based on their needs. A general structure would be {{structure here}}.


Let’s set up a brief 7-9 minute conversation to go over specifics. I can send over an invite, or if now isn’t the right time, no worries!
